## 6.2 Where the marks actually are

The current rubric: 20 points, four criteria.

| Criterion | Marks | % | Top band wants |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Executive summary | 2 | 10% | Brief yet richly detailed; all required information, connections to the topic apparent |
| **Building performance, WALE, financial/operational analysis** | **6** | **30%** | Comprehensive *and critical* three-year analysis; performance indicators evaluated; changes explained through **the sub-market, supply and demand, economic conditions and the sector** |
| **Review of property manager performance and skills** | **10** | **50%** | The issues the manager had to consider and the skills they needed; conclusions supported by well-argued logic **linking the state of the economy to the sector** |
| Formatting, presentation, referencing | 2 | 10% | Professional style; **wide** use of books / journals / newspaper articles; QUT Cite\|Write |

Three things fall out of that table.

**One — the manager review is half the assessment on its own.** Ten marks.
Most students write the building analysis first and longest, because
describing a building out of an IM is easy, and leave the manager review as
an afterthought. The rubric weights it the other way around.

**Two — both big criteria demand the economy-to-sector linkage.** The
building criterion adds the sub-market and supply and demand. So there is no
free-standing "Economic Overview" section — the economics earns marks only
when it's welded to an indicator or a manager decision.

**Three — the word budget follows the marks:**

```
Substance marks inside the word count: 6 + 10 = 16
1,500 / 16 ≈ 94 words per mark

Manager review        10 marks  ≈ 900 words
Building + WALE        6 marks  ≈ 500 words
Intro + conclusion              ≈ 100 words
```

The two sections should also cross-feed: the building analysis establishes
**what happened** (the indicators and their movements); the manager review
explains **who did what about it, why, and what skill it took**. Write them
in that order and the second section largely writes itself.

> **Executive summary, tables and appendices are all excluded from the word
> count**, and there is a ±10% rule — so 1,350–1,650 words of body text.
> Anything you can legitimately put in a table stops costing words. The FAQ
> says it outright: *"The property overview could be presented in a table and
> use appendices to reference any useful images, maps or plans."*

**Industrial:** swap MAT/GOCR for net face rent $/sqm against prime and

(CBRE/JLL industrial reports — Brisbane's vacancy has run near record lows,
which is most of any industrial performance story).

The FAQ accepts two structures: a sub-heading per indicator discussing the

### The rubric's test is causal, not descriptive

The 6-mark top band: changes in performance **explained** by linkages to the
sub-market, supply and demand, the economy and the sector. Every indicator
paragraph should close the loop:

```
INDICATOR   what moved            "occupancy fell from 98% to 91% in X−1"
CAUSE       why, with evidence    "as [sub-market] vacancy rose to Y% on new
                                   supply and two tenant failures"
LINKAGE     economy → sector      "consistent with [rate cycle / consumer
                                   spending / sector demand], per [source]"
```

Sub-market evidence lives in: the PCA Office Market Report (office), anchor
results and SCCA data (retail), agency industrial reports, **RP Data**
(comparable sales — what similar assets traded at across your three years)
and **Brisbane Cityscope** (tenant movements in the sub-market).

> **Site visits are optional now — but a look still sharpens the analysis.**
> The FAQ calls visits enrichment, *"not a requirement for Assessment"*.
> Nothing stops you walking the common areas or using Street View across the
> three-year imagery history: capex the IM brags about is usually visible,
> and so is capex it doesn't mention. If you use anything from a
> conversation on site, cite it in-text as a personal communication (it does
> not go in the reference list).

## 6.5 The manager review (10 marks)

Half the assessment. The criterion asks two things — **the issues the
property manager may have had to take into consideration** and **the skills
they will have needed to employ** — and the top band adds a third: logic
that links the economy to the sector.

### The unit of analysis: issue → action → skill → impact

Every paragraph in this section should be one of these chains, grounded in
something the IM actually shows:

```
ISSUE     what the operating environment threw at them
          (rate cycle, cost inflation, a 2029 expiry cluster, an anchor
           at 3.3% GOCR, insurance premiums, ESG pressure)
ACTION    what the manager demonstrably did
          (the IM is the evidence log: occupancy achieved, WALE built,
           capex undertaken, recoveries structured, ancillary income
           created, incentives written)
SKILL     name it — see the table below
IMPACT    the number it moved
          (net income, outgoings $/sqm, WALE, occupancy, capital value)
```

The IM is a *sales* document, so it records the manager's wins — read the
investment highlights as a list of claims about management performance, then
verify each one against the schedule and financials before repeating it.

### The skills vocabulary

From Topics 1–2 and Phil Levesque's guest lecture:

| Skill | What it looks like | Impact you can point to |
|---|---|---|
| **Communication** | Proactive tenant updates, owner reporting, crisis comms | Retention, fewer disputes |
| **Negotiation** | Rent reviews, renewals, incentive levels, contractor tenders | Face vs effective rent, outgoings $/sqm |
| **Commercial thinking** | Choosing rate over occupancy (or the reverse) against owner objectives | WALE, occupancy, income profile |
| **Income generation** | Casual mall leasing, media contracts, telco renewals, EV charging, storage — income that does not exist unless someone creates it | Ancillary income $ and its share of total income |
| **Financial literacy** | Budgets, variance analysis, outgoings recovery, arrears management | Outgoings ratio, arrears, net income |
| **Lease administration** | Critical-date diary: reviews, options, expiries, make-good | A missed review is permanent income loss |
| **Technical knowledge** | HVAC, essential services, fire compliance, BMS, capex sequencing | Downtime, capex deferral, NABERS |
| **Risk management** | WHS, contractor induction, insurance, contingency planning | Incidents, claims, liability |
| **Organisation** | Scheduled work vs emergencies | Response times, planned vs reactive maintenance |
| **Leadership** | Managing contractors and the on-site team | Presentation, service standards |

The three things owners care about — **income, risk, value** — are the frame.
Every skill you name should end in one of those three, because that is what
"maximising the performance of an investment" means.

### The issues menu for the past three years

Date these to *your* EOI window and evidence them — don't assert the macro
from memory. The generic list to test against your property:

- **The rate cycle** — what the cash rate did across your three years, what
  it did to cap rates, values and buyer depth in your sub-market (RBA
  releases + RP Data comparables).
- **Cost inflation through the outgoings** — insurance, energy, cleaning and
  security wages, statutory charges; who wore it (recoverable vs not).
- **Sector demand** — office: hybrid work and flight to quality; retail:
  consumer spending and anchor turnover; industrial: e-commerce and near-zero
  vacancy.
- **The expiry profile** the manager inherited vs the one in the IM — what
  was renewed, at what incentive, and what that did to the WALE.
- **ESG and compliance** — NABERS expectations, essential services, WHS.

> **"This is not a recommendation for future actions."** The brief's own
> line. The review is what the manager *did* and *had to consider* — the
> moment you write "the manager should now…", you are answering Part B's
> question with Part A's words.

## 6.6 WALE — the compulsory calculation

You **must** include a WALE calculation, **as at the EOI date**, **by income
and by area**. The full walk-through on a real schedule — every edge case,
income and area, with a sensitivity — is
[Tutorial 2: Auburn Quarter](#/USB244/06-tutorial-2-auburn-quarter).

```
WALE (income) = Σ (tenant gross income × remaining term in years) / Σ (tenant gross income)
WALE (area)   = Σ (tenant area         × remaining term in years) / Σ (tenant area)

remaining term = (lease expiry date − EOI date) / 365.25
```

### Finding the EOI date

| Situation | What to use |
|---|---|
| IM states an EOI closing date | Use it — usually near the **end** of the IM, in the sale process section |
| IM has no EOI date | Use the **date on the financial pack / tenancy schedule** |
| EOI date ≠ financial pack date | Assume the financial data is identical at both, and say so |

> **Richlands Plaza — the teaching example — has no stated EOI close date**,
> so the fallback applies: its financial pack is "approximately as at 31
> December 2025", and that becomes the calculation date. Your IM may do the
> same to you; state the assumption and move on.

### The edge cases, decided

Every one of these comes from the FAQ. Getting them wrong is the most common
way to lose marks on a calculation you actually performed.

| Line in the schedule | Income WALE | Area WALE |
|---|---|---|
| **Vacant tenancy** | **Omit** — there is no income | **Include at 0 years** — the area exists |
| **Holdover / monthly tenant** | **Omit** — nominal 1-month secured income | Omit (state the assumption) |
| **Car parking, no separate lease** | Roll into that tenancy's gross rent | Follows the tenancy |
| **Car parking, separately licensed on a formal lease** | **Include** as its own line | No area — discuss it |
| **Signage** | Include if you include car parking — **and discuss it** | No area assigned |
| **Tenant with an option** | Assume the option is **not** exercised; expire at the stated date | Same |

> **Car parking income cannot be ignored.** The FAQ says so directly. CBD and
> suburban assets have different parking arrangements, and industry practice
> goes both ways, but the unit advises **including** it. Whichever way you
> go, state it.

> **Zero-area income lines flatter the income WALE.** ATMs, signage and
> licensed car parks carry income but no square metres, so they move the
> income WALE and not the area WALE. The FAQ asks you to raise this as a
> **discussion item in the body of the report** — one sentence earns the mark.

### Worked example

An illustrative multi-tenant centre — constructed to exercise every edge case
above. **EOI date: 30 September 2026.**

| Tenant | Area (sqm) | Gross income p.a. | Expiry | Term at EOI |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Supermarket (anchor) | 2,810 | $925,000 | 31 Dec 2032 | 6.2533 |
| Specialty 1 — pharmacy | 320 | $160,000 | 31 Mar 2029 | 2.4997 |
| Specialty 2 — café | 110 | $88,000 | 30 Sep 2027 | 0.9993 |
| Specialty 3 — holdover | 95 | $76,000 | monthly | *excluded* |
| Shop 12 — vacant | 60 | — | — | 0.0000 |
| ATM licence | 0 | $19,706 | 30 Jun 2028 | 1.7495 |
| Car park licence (formal) | 0 | $42,000 | 30 Sep 2029 | 3.0007 |

The anchor holds options beyond 2032; per the FAQ they are assumed **not**
exercised.

```
INCOME WALE
  925,000 × 6.2533 = 5,784,257
  160,000 × 2.4997 =   399,945
   88,000 × 0.9993 =    87,940
   19,706 × 1.7495 =    34,475
   42,000 × 3.0007 =   126,029
                     ─────────
                     6,432,646   ÷  1,234,706  =  5.21 years

AREA WALE
  2,810 × 6.2533 = 17,571.6
    320 × 2.4997 =    799.9
    110 × 0.9993 =    109.9
     60 × 0.0000 =      0.0     ← vacant, counted at zero
                    ─────────
                     18,481.5   ÷  3,300.0  =  5.60 years
```

**Area WALE (5.60) > income WALE (5.21)** — the long lease sits on the
low-rent-per-sqm anchor space, so income rolls over faster than floor area
does. (Richlands Plaza shows the same pattern: 3.76 income / 4.02 area.)

### Show your assumptions move the answer

The cheapest way to demonstrate you understand the calculation rather than
having merely performed it. Same schedule, three defensible variations:

| Assumption changed | Income WALE | Area WALE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base case above | 5.21 | 5.60 |
| Exclude zero-area licences (ATM, car park) from income WALE | **5.35** | 5.60 |
| Exclude the vacant shop from area WALE | 5.21 | **5.70** |
| Anchor exercises its first 10-year option | **12.70** | **14.11** |

A sentence naming the assumption, and one line showing what it does to the
number, is worth more than three decimal places. Workings go in an appendix
— free — with the result and its meaning in the body.

## 6.7 Ancillary and non-space income

Every building earns money from things that are not floor area — rooftop
telecommunications, signage, ATMs, car parking, casual mall leasing, media
screens, storage, utility on-selling. It is the part of the income statement
students skim past, and it is the single clearest evidence of an **active**
property manager, because none of it happens by itself. Base rent arrives
whether the manager is any good or not. Ancillary income does not.

This is Topic 1's **gross income strategies** family made concrete, and it
feeds three parts of Part A: the WALE, the income analysis, and the 10-mark
manager review.

### Where it hides in an IM

Look for *Sundry income*, *Other income*, *Ancillary income*, *Casual mall
leasing*, *Percentage rent*, and separate licence schedules behind the main
tenancy schedule. It is often a single line in the financial summary with the
detail somewhere else entirely.

Richlands Plaza (the teaching example), worked from the IM's own figures:

```
ATMs (listed as a tenancy category)                        19,706
Electricity profit — Richlands Plaza                       59,181
Electricity profit — Richlands Business Centre              2,946
                                                          ───────
Total sundry income                                        62,127
Zarraffa's percentage rent                                 10,441
                                                          ───────
Non-space / ancillary income                               92,274

As a share of gross market income   92,274 / 2,791,905  =   3.31%
Capitalised at 6.0%                 92,274 / 0.06       = $1,537,900
```

**$1.5 million of capital value from income that occupies no lettable area.**
The ATMs alone are 0.71% of income and 0.0% of GLA.

> **The electricity profit line is an embedded network.** The centre buys
> energy in bulk and on-sells it to tenants at a margin — $62,127 a year here.
> It is a real management activity with real regulatory exposure (embedded
> network operators need an AER exemption or authorisation), and it is exactly
> the kind of thing to name in the manager review. Verify the current
> regulatory position before you cite it.

### The catalogue

| Source | Retail | Office | Notes for the report |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rooftop telecommunications** | ✓ | ✓ | Long leases (10–15 yrs + options), high margin, zero area |
| **Signage** — pylon, façade, third-party billboard | ✓ | ✓ | Naming rights on an office tower are a separate negotiation |
| **Digital media** — mall screens, lobby screens, car park boom-gate ads | ✓ | ✓ | Usually a revenue-share with a media operator |
| **Casual mall leasing** | ✓ | — | Carts, kiosks, promotional sites, seasonal (Christmas, tax agents) |
| **ATMs** | ✓ | ✓ | Licence, not a lease; no area |
| **Car parking** — casual, monthly, after-hours, valet | ✓ | ✓ | The biggest ancillary line in most CBD office assets |
| **EV charging** | ✓ | ✓ | Capex now, income and ESG credential later |
| **Storage** — surplus BOH, basement cages | ✓ | ✓ | Monetises space that is not lettable |
| **Parcel lockers / click-and-collect** | ✓ | ✓ | Small income, real foot-traffic value |
| **Vending, kiddie rides, massage chairs** | ✓ | — | Trivial individually, adds up |
| **Utility on-selling / embedded network** | ✓ | ✓ | The Richlands electricity profit line |
| **Solar PV** | ✓ | ✓ | Export income or on-sell, plus NABERS |
| **Waste and recycling rebates** | ✓ | ✓ | Reduces outgoings rather than adding income |
| **After-hours HVAC charges** | — | ✓ | Usually recovery, sometimes margin — check the lease |
| **Carrier riser access / rights of entry** | — | ✓ | Fibre providers paying for building access |
| **Meeting-room, event and film location hire** | ✓ | ✓ | Underused in most assets |
| **Percentage / turnover rent** | ✓ | — | Not strictly ancillary, but it is income the manager earns by driving trade |

### What it does to the WALE

This is where ancillary income stops being a footnote and starts changing a
number you have to submit.

Take the worked schedule from §6.6 and add one rooftop telecommunications
lease — $28,000 p.a., 15 years remaining at the EOI date, **zero lettable
area**:

```
                          Income WALE      Area WALE
Base case                    5.21 yrs        5.60 yrs
+ rooftop telco lease        5.43 yrs        5.60 yrs   ← unchanged
                            ────────
                             +0.22 yrs from 2.2% of income
```

One long, zero-area lease adds nearly three months to the income WALE and
does nothing to the area WALE. **Ancillary income systematically widens the
gap between the two WALEs**, and in the opposite direction to an anchor.

| What is driving the gap | Which WALE is higher |
|---|---|
| A big anchor on a long lease at low $/sqm | **Area** WALE (Richlands: 4.02 vs 3.76) |
| Long zero-area licences — telco, signage, billboards | **Income** WALE |
| Both present | They partly cancel — say so, don't just report the net |

> **Casual mall leasing is invisible to the WALE, and that is the point.** CML
> licences run for days or weeks, so they are excluded from the calculation
> entirely — like holdover tenants. A centre earning $80,000 of CML income has
> a WALE that says nothing about it. The income is real, it is high margin,
> and it is **completely unsecured**. If your asset has material CML income,
> the WALE overstates the security of total income and one sentence saying so
> is a genuine analytical point.

### The valuation trap

Ancillary income is not worth the same as base rent, and treating it as though
it is will get picked up.

```
$92,274 of ancillary income, capitalised at

   6.0% (same as the base rent)  =  $1,537,900
   7.0%                          =  $1,318,200
   8.0% (risk-adjusted)          =  $1,153,425
                                    ──────────
   Haircut from 6% to 8%            $384,475   =  25% less value
```

Why a valuer discounts it: casual and licence income is short, unsecured, and
foot-traffic dependent; percentage rent moves with turnover; utility margins
are regulated; media revenue depends on an operator's contract. Some valuers
capitalise sundry income at a higher rate, some deduct a portion, some exclude
volatile items altogether.

> **Say which treatment you assumed.** "Ancillary income of $92,274 has been
> capitalised at a 200 bp premium to the passing yield to reflect its shorter
> and unsecured nature" is the sentence that separates bands. Capitalising
> $80,000 of pop-up income at the same rate as a Woolworths lease is the error.

### The risks a property manager has to manage

| Source | The management problem |
|---|---|
| **Rooftop telco** | Restricts roof access and complicates roof replacement; make-good and removal are contentious; carriers assign these leases to specialist tower companies that negotiate renewals hard; carriers also hold statutory installation powers for low-impact facilities under Commonwealth telecommunications law — check the current provisions before citing them |
| **Signage** | Council approvals, third-party billboard leases outliving the owner's plans for the façade |
| **Casual mall leasing** | Volatile, collapses with foot traffic, competes with permanent specialty tenants who will complain about a cart selling their product |
| **Embedded network** | Regulated; tenants can and do challenge on-sell margins |
| **Car parking** | Ties to trading hours, WHS, and in retail to the anchor's customer expectations |
| **All of it** | Almost none of it appears in the tenancy schedule, so it is easy to miss in due diligence — and easy for a manager to leave on the table |

### If you inspect the property

Site visits are no longer part of this assessment, but nothing stops you
standing in the car park — or working through Street View — looking for
income the IM never itemises:

```
LOOK UP      rooftop antennas, dishes, telco cabinets, solar array
LOOK OUT     pylon sign, billboard, façade signage, naming rights
LOOK AROUND  digital screens, mall carts, kiosks, ATMs, vending,
             kiddie rides, parcel lockers, EV chargers
LOOK DOWN    paid parking, boom gates, car share bays, storage cages
ASK the agent (or the data room):
             "What does the asset earn outside base rent, and how has
              that moved in three years?"
             "Who holds the media contract, and when does it expire?"
             "Is there a rooftop telco lease, and when does it renew?"
```

The answer to that last one is often *"it was inherited and nobody has looked
at it since"* — which is a finding about management, and findings are what
the 10 marks are for.

## 6.8 Benchmarking outgoings against the PCA benchmark

The earlier brief made this a second MUST alongside the WALE. The current
text mandates only the WALE — but the FAQ keeps its full Outgoings Benchmark
section, the Week 4 tutorial teaches it, and it is the cheapest analytical
differentiator inside the 6-mark building criterion. Do it.

### Getting the benchmark

- **QUT library database**, under **Property Council of Australia**. There is
  a Canvas page with access instructions (*USB244 Access to Library
  Databases*) and a tutorial recording walking through it — the FAQ flags
  that students struggle with access, so do this early.
- **Match the benchmark year to the EOI date** on your IM. If that year isn't
  available, use the latest available and **justify in the report why** —
  the FAQ explicitly permits this, but only with the justification.
- **Office benchmark for office, retail for retail.** If you chose
  **industrial**: the PCA benchmark survey covers office and retail, not
  industrial — benchmark the comparable lines (statutory charges, management
  fees, insurance) against the nearest series or published industrial
  outgoings guides, justify the substitution, and flag it to the coordinator
  early.

### Method

```
1.  Pull every outgoings line the IM discloses.
2.  Split statutory vs operating vs non-recoverable.
3.  Convert each to $ per sqm on the SAME area basis the PCA uses
    (NLA for office, GLA/GLAR for retail).
4.  Line up against the PCA benchmark for the matching year and sector.
5.  For each material variance: is it above or below, by how much,
    and WHY — and what did the property manager do about it?
```

Step 5 is the part that earns marks. A table of variances with no explanation
is a statistics dump, and the brief warns that *"simply providing
property/economic statistics will not result in a passing grade."*

> **Not every line will exist in both.** The FAQ's own example: if the
> property has no gas connection there is no Gas & Oil charge to compare.
> Compare what you have, say what is missing, don't manufacture a zero.

### Presentation template

Using the Richlands Plaza figures as an example of the *format* — the
benchmark column is what you fill from the PCA tool:

| Outgoings | $ p.a. | $/sqm GLA | PCA benchmark $/sqm | Variance | Comment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Statutory expenses | 147,349 | 29.79 | *from PCA* | | Rates, land tax — limited manager control |
| Operating expenses | 345,032 | 69.75 | *from PCA* | | Cleaning, security, HVAC, management fee |
| Non-recoverable | 111,400 | 22.52 | *from PCA* | | Straight off net income |
| **Total** | **603,781** | **122.05** | | | **21.63%** of gross market income |

```
Total GLA          4,031 (main centre) + 916 (business centre) = 4,947 sqm
Outgoings ratio    603,781 / 2,791,905 = 21.63% of gross market income
Recoverable        147,349 + 345,032 = 492,381  →  $99.53/sqm
```

> **Watch the area basis.** $/sqm on NLA and $/sqm on GLA are not comparable —
> GLA is measured to the *outside* of external walls and is always the larger
> number for the same envelope, so the same dollars produce a *lower* $/sqm on
> GLA. Benchmark office on NLA and retail on GLA/GLAR, and say which you used.
> Full measurement rules in [Formula Sheet & Exam Traps](#/USB244/09-formula-sheet).

> **Non-recoverable outgoings are where the argument is.** Every dollar comes
> straight off net income, and at a 6% cap rate each dollar destroys $16.67 of
> capital value. Cutting $20,000 of non-recoverables through better lease
> drafting at renewal is worth about **$333,000** of value with no rent
> increase at all — which is exactly the kind of manager action the 10-mark
> criterion wants identified and quantified.

## 6.11 Traps checklist

> **1. It is a historical review.** "This is not a recommendation for future
> actions." Recommendations belong in Part B.

> **2. Run the eligibility gate before writing a word.** SEQ, 3+ tenants, not
> Richlands or 41 Park Road, different from your group members — and
> **pre-March 2024 is an immediate fail**.

> **3. Stay inside 12 months on the IM date.** The brief says both "last 12
> months" and "no older than March 2024" — recent satisfies both readings.

> **4. The manager review is half the marks.** Ten of twenty. Budget ~900
> words for it and write it as issue → action → skill → impact chains.

> **5. WALE is at the EOI date, not today.** And the three-year window runs
> back from the EOI date, not from today.

> **6. Both WALEs, every time.** Income *and* area. One is half a calculation.

> **7. Vacant space counts in the area WALE at zero years and is omitted from
> the income WALE.** Not omitted from both.

> **8. Holdover tenants are excluded.** One month of secured income is
> nominal — including them shows you didn't read the FAQ.

> **9. Assume options are not exercised — and say so.** Either assumption is
> acceptable; an unstated assumption is not.

> **10. Match the PCA benchmark year to the EOI date**, or justify why you
> couldn't. Office benchmark for office, retail for retail — and the PCA
> survey doesn't cover industrial, so industrial pickers must substitute and
> justify.

> **11. Never compare $/sqm across NLA and GLA.** Different denominators,
> different numbers, invalid variance.

> **12. Statistics without interpretation fail.** The brief says so in terms.
> Every figure needs a "which means the manager…".

> **13. Don't skip the sundry income line.** At Richlands it is $92,274 — 3.31%
> of income and $1.5m of capital value at a 6% cap rate — and it is the part of
> the statement that only exists because a manager went and earned it.

> **14. Long zero-area licences inflate the income WALE.** A rooftop telco
> lease at 2.2% of income added 0.22 years to the income WALE and nothing to
> the area WALE. If both an anchor and long licences are present the two
> effects partly cancel — explain the net, don't just report it.

> **15. Don't capitalise casual income at the base-rent cap rate.** Moving
> $92,274 of ancillary income from 6% to 8% costs 25% of its value. State the
> treatment you assumed.

> **16. Tables and appendices are free.** Everything descriptive moves out of
> the body; the body carries only analysis.

> **17. Confirm you're on the current brief and rubric.** This assessment was
> revised mid-August 2026; the slides and some Canvas artefacts still describe
> the superseded two-building version.
