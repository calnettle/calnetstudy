# Final Exam Drill — 30 MCQs and 7 Short Answers

answers (70 points).** The questions follow the order of the
[Final Exam Guide](#/USB244/15-final-exam-guide), so a miss sends you
straight to the right item. These are practice questions written from the
slides, not the lecturer's paper.

How to use it: do Part A closed book in **30 minutes**, mark it, then go
back to the guide item for every miss. Do Part B as notes, about

## Part B — 7 Mock Short Answers (10 points each)

Write each as notes: define, list the points, give a number or a real
asset, finish with the value consequence.

**Q1. Explain how a property manager can increase the capital value of a
neighbourhood shopping centre. Use `CV = NI / CR`.**

<details><summary>Marking points</summary>

- `CV = NI / CR`: value rises if net income rises **or** the cap rate falls
- **Gross income:** timely market reviews, minimise vacancy, manage arrears, active renewals, diversify income (signage, licences, kiosks, car parking), promotions, tenant mix
- **Outgoings:** tender and long-term service contracts, preventive maintenance, metering for user pays, structure leases for maximum recovery
- **Cap rate:** attractive WALE, good tenants, low vacancy, limited arrears, few disputes, well maintained
- Number: $30,000 more net income at 6% = **$500,000** of value
- Land tax can't be recovered from Qld retail shop tenants, so it's a cost to manage, not pass on

</details>

**Q2. Draw the risk management matrix and place three risks for an office
building on it, with a response for each.**

<details><summary>Marking points</summary>

- Axes: likelihood (probability) × consequence (impact). Risk = variance from the expected, either way
- Four boxes with their responses: HP/HI specific plan and strategy change · HP/LI adjust systems, benchmark · LP/HI **insurance**, lower the risk profile · LP/LI maintenance response, systems, trends
- Three placed risks, e.g. lift breakdowns (HP/LI, preventive maintenance), major tenant leaves (LP–MP/HI, early renewal talks, staggered expiries), fire (LP/HI, insurance and essential services compliance)
- Systemic (cash rate) vs non-systemic (inside the building)

</details>

**Q3. What does a commercial lease contain, and why does each part matter
to the owner?**

<details><summary>Marking points</summary>

- Definition: exclusive possession for a term, for rent. Gives certainty to both parties
- Parties, premises, term (feeds the WALE), options and the exercise window
- Rent and reviews (fixed, CPI, market; ratchet, cap and collar)
- Outgoings: which are recoverable (net vs gross)
- Permitted use (controls the mix), assignment and subletting with consent
- Repairs, landlord's entry rights, make good, damage and destruction (abatement, termination)
- Security: bank guarantee, personal guarantees; insurance (public liability)
- Example: Kessels Road, $144,210 + GST, fixed 4%, market review at the option, 9–12 month option window, tenant's $20m public liability, no car parks

</details>

**Q4. Compare Green Star and NABERS, and explain the Commercial Building
Disclosure program.**

<details><summary>Marking points</summary>

- Green Star: GBCA, design and construction, one-off certification
- NABERS: government, actual measured performance (energy, water, waste, indoor environment), 1–6 stars, renewed annually from 12 months of bills
- A well-designed building run badly shows up in NABERS, not Green Star
- CBD: *Building Energy Efficiency Disclosure Act 2010*; office space **≥ 1,000 m²** (2,000 m² before July 2017) for sale, lease or sublease
- BEEC before marketing: NABERS Energy rating + Tenancy Lighting Assessment, valid 12 months, accredited assessor, rating in all ads, public register
- Value: green premium, brown discount, lower outgoings

</details>

**Q5. Explain the tax treatment of property: CGT, GST, and capex vs opex.**

<details><summary>Marking points</summary>

- Tax stack: federal (income tax, CGT, GST), state (land tax, stamp duty), council (rates)
- CGT: part of income tax, assets acquired since 20 Sept 1985, gain = proceeds − cost base, 50% discount for individuals after 12 months; worked number
- Recent CGT and negative gearing changes affect residential only (the Week 6 slide)
- GST: commercial rent and purchases yes, with input tax credits; residential rent input taxed; going concern GST-free
- Opex: restores, 100% deductible now. Capex: improves or changes material, depreciated; building allowance 2.5% over 40 years on construction cost
- Initial repairs trap

</details>

**Q6. Define corporate real estate and explain the five types and the
space portfolio strategy.**

<details><summary>Marking points</summary>

- Property a company uses for its own operations; its value is its contribution to the business, not investment value
- Five types: strategic (own and control), landmark (control, image), core (control, not necessarily own), peripheral (nearly always leased), surplus (dispose)
- Space split: core 70–80%, flexible 15–20%, serviced 5–10%, with what each is
- Efficiency (cost, capacity) vs effectiveness (value, potential)
- Challenges: flexibility vs long-term commitment, volatility, inflexible real estate
- Future of work: AI, portfolio decisions waiting on workforce strategy

</details>

**Q7. Explain step by step how to calculate a WALE, and why it matters to
value.**

<details><summary>Marking points</summary>

- Definition and both formulas
- Steps: measure date, list tenants, years remaining ÷ 365.25, special cases (vacancy in area at 0 and out of income; options not exercised; holdover out; car park licences in income), multiply, sum, divide
- Worked example with both WALEs (Part A Q30's numbers work)
- Lecturer's common errors: mixing weightings, wrong expiry dates, vacancy, gross vs net rents, rounding
- 5+ years = secure; longer WALE → lower cap rate → higher value. Portfolio WALE is weighted, never a simple average

</details>
