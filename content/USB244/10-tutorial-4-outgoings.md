The Week 7 tutorial, and the one that finally puts numbers under the
`CV = NI / CR` chain. Outgoings are the gap between **gross** income and
**net** income, and net income is what gets capitalised — so every dollar
of unrecovered outgoing is a dollar off net income and roughly
`1 / cap rate` dollars off value.

## 7.1 What Outgoings Are

> **Commercial property outgoings are the operating costs of a commercial
> premises that the landlord incurs and is allowed to recover from the
> tenant under the lease.** They sit **in addition to base rent** and
> cover the day-to-day expenses of owning, running and maintaining the
> property.

The deck gives three equivalent framings from Australian commercial
leasing guidance:

- Operating costs **related to the premises** that a commercial lessor
  **reasonably incurs** and passes on to tenants.
- The landlord's **recoverable operating expenses**, charged **beside**
  rent, where the lease is a **net lease**.
- Statutory charges, operating costs and common-area utilities — but
  **excluding capital expenditure and the landlord's financing costs**.

Three words do all the work: **reasonably**, **incurs** and
**recoverable**. An expense that fails any one of them stays with the
owner.

## 7.2 What Is In, and What Is Out

| ✅ Can be included | ❌ Cannot be included |
|---|---|
| Council rates (general, water, sewerage) | Capital expenditure — roof replacement, major plant upgrades |
| **Land tax** — *except in retail shop leases in QLD* | Depreciation or **sinking fund** contributions |
| Building insurance premiums | The landlord's **interest costs** or loan charges |
| Common-area utilities (electricity, gas, water for shared spaces) | Insurance **excesses** or loss-of-profits insurance |
| Repairs, maintenance and compliance for **shared areas** | Marketing funds or merchant association contributions |
| Cleaning, gardening, waste removal, pest control | |
| Fire services testing, security, HVAC servicing | |
| Property management fees | |

> **The Queensland land tax carve-out is the exam's favourite.** Land tax
> is a recoverable outgoing in most commercial leases, but **not** under a
> **retail shop lease in Queensland** (Retail Shop Leases Act 1994). A
> neighbourhood shopping centre is full of retail shop leases — so its
> land tax sits in the budget and is *not* recoverable from those tenants.
> At Kallangur Fair that is **$59,225 a year, 9.5% of total expenditure**,
> permanently on the landlord's side of the line. Flag it in any retail
> analysis.

> **Learn the exclusions as a principle, not a list.** Everything in the
> right-hand column is either (a) **capital** rather than operating —
> capex, sinking funds, depreciation; (b) the **owner's own financing**
> decision, which has nothing to do with running the premises; or (c) a
> **promotional** cost governed separately by the lease. A tenant pays to
> *operate* the building they occupy, not to *own* or *improve* it.

> **Repairs to shared areas are recoverable; capital works are not — and
> the same physical job can be either.** Servicing the lifts is an
> outgoing. Replacing the lifts is capex. The test is whether you have
> maintained the existing asset or created a new/improved one. The same
> distinction appears again in Topic 5's tax material, for the same
> reason.

## 7.3 The Three Recovery Structures

From the tutorial's own activity — confirm each from the tenancy schedule:

| Structure | How it works | Who carries the risk of a cost rise |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross lease** | Outgoings are **built into the rent**; the tenant pays a single amount | **The landlord** |
| **Net lease** | Tenant pays **rent plus outgoings** — usually budgeted annually and **reconciled** | The tenant |
| **Direct recovery** | Landlord pays the expense, then **invoices the tenant** | The tenant |

> **A "gross" lease does not mean the tenant escapes outgoings — it means
> they are priced in and fixed.** The landlord has taken the escalation
> risk in exchange for a higher face rent. Which is exactly why comparing
> the face rents of a gross lease and a net lease tells you nothing until
> you have put both on the same basis. This is the same face-vs-effective
> problem as Topic 4's incentives.

> **"Budgeted annually and reconciled" is the phrase to remember.** Net
> lease outgoings are *estimated* at the start of the year, charged
> monthly, and then **trued up** against actuals. That reconciliation is
> where disputes live — and it is why a budget-versus-actual column is the
> first thing to look for.

What a reader should actually notice:

- **Two lines are half the budget.** Cleaning (25.9%) and the management
  fee (25.1%) together are **51%** of total expenditure. Everything else
  is rounding by comparison. Any cost-reduction strategy that does not
  address those two is theatre.
- **The management fee is 6.80% of total gross rent** — verified against
  the schedule's $2,306,319. That is a benchmarkable number and it is on
  the high side for a neighbourhood centre.
- **Statutory charges are 25.2%** and are almost entirely outside the
  manager's control. The controllable 74.6% is where a SAM plan lives.
- **Electricity is $49,764 as an expense — and the centre books $67,333
  of "electricity profit" as other income.** The landlord buys in bulk
  and on-charges at retail, netting about **$17,569**. Read the expense
  line alone and you would misjudge the position entirely.

> **Expenses are per square metre of GLA, and the denominator matters.**
> $132.69/m² is total expenditure ÷ **4,714 m² GLA**. Benchmark against
> the PCA series only on the same basis — a figure computed on NLA, or on
> occupied area, is not comparable. This is the same measurement
> discipline as Topic 2 and Topic 3.

### (b) What do you see when you compare to budget?

> **You cannot answer this from the supplied file, and saying so is the
> correct answer.** The Kallangur schedule is headed "**Outgoings
> Estimate**" and carries a **Budget** column only — there is no actuals
> column and no prior-year comparison. A genuine budget-versus-actual
> review needs both, plus the reconciliation statement.

What the document *does* disclose, and what each disclosure should make
you do:

| Disclosure | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "A number of non-recoverable expenses, such as **legals, leasing fees and promotion fund contributions**, have been excluded … determined to be non-recurring items" | The budget is **not** the full cost of running the asset | Add them back before modelling NOI; leasing fees in particular recur every time a lease expires |
| Note (1): the manager has identified **~$63,300 p.a. in management cost savings** not yet incorporated | The budget overstates forward expenditure | Model both cases |
| "Refer to Disclaimer Tab for Important Notes" | Vendor-prepared figures | Verify against rates notices and contracts in due diligence |

The $63,300 is worth doing the arithmetic on, because it shows why
outgoings are a *value* question and not an accounting one:

```
Total expenditure as budgeted               $625,513
Less identified management savings          −$63,300
Revised expenditure                         $562,213

Net operating income as budgeted          $1,904,282
Plus the savings                            +$63,300
Revised NOI                               $1,967,582

Value impact at a 6.0% cap rate:  $63,300 / 0.06  =  $1,055,000
```

> **A $63,300 cost saving is worth over a million dollars of capital
> value.** `CV = NI / CR` runs in both directions: at a 6% cap rate every
> recurring dollar saved is worth $16.67 of value. That is the single
> best argument for why asset management is a value discipline and not an
> administrative one — and it is exactly the kind of quantified
> initiative a Part B SAM plan needs.

### (c) From the tenancy schedule, confirm the lease structures

This is where the budget and the rent roll have to be read together.

| | Area m² | % GLA | Base rent $ p.a. | **Outgoings recoveries $ p.a.** | Recovery $/m² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Major (Woolworths) | 3,247 | 68.9% | 925,000 | **0** | **0.00** |
| Specialties (14) | 1,465 | 31.1% | 1,175,762 | **173,377** | **118.37** |
| ATMs (2) | 2 | 0.0% | 32,180 | **0** | 0.00 |
| **Total** | **4,714** | **100%** | **2,132,942** | **173,377** | **36.78** |

Read against the three structures in §7.3:

- **Woolworths is effectively on a gross basis** — 68.9% of the centre's
  area, $925,000 of rent, and **zero** outgoings recovery. The landlord
  absorbs the operating cost of more than two-thirds of the building.
  (Its rent review is a formula, `BR + TR/3`, which is where the landlord
  gets paid instead.)
- **The specialties are on net leases** at an almost perfectly uniform
  **$118.37/m²** — twelve of the fourteen are on that exact rate, two are
  rounded to $118.00. That uniformity is the signature of a **budgeted
  recovery rate** applied across the specialty mall, not per-tenant
  actuals.
- **The ATMs recover nothing** — licence-style arrangements, immaterial at
  2 m².

Now the number that matters:

```
Total expenditure                     $625,513   = $132.69/m² across the GLA
Total recovered                       $173,377   = 27.72% of expenditure
Landlord's unrecovered burden         $452,137

Specialties' pro-rata share of expenditure   1,465/4,714 × 625,513 = $194,395
Specialties actually recovered                                      $173,377
                                                  → recovering  89.2%

Major's pro-rata share of expenditure        3,247/4,714 × 625,513 = $430,853
Major actually recovered                                                  $0
```

Verified, including the stated 27.72% recovery ratio.

> **Two separate leakages, and only one of them is a problem.**
>
> 1. The **major** contributes nothing against a $430,853 pro-rata share.
>    That is normal — anchor tenants have the bargaining power and are
>    paid for in covenant strength, foot traffic and a turnover-linked
>    review. It is priced, not lost.
> 2. The **specialties** recover at $118.37/m² against an actual cost of
>    **$132.69/m²** — a **$14.32/m² shortfall**, and they are recovering
>    only **89.2%** of even their own share. Part of that is the
>    Queensland retail land tax carve-out (their pro-rata land tax is
>    $18,406). The rest is a recovery rate that has not kept pace with
>    cost, and *that* is an asset management issue with a specific fix:
>    reset the estimate at the next reconciliation.

> **27.72% recovery is a headline that needs its denominator explained.**
> It looks alarming until you see that 68.9% of the area is a major on a
> zero-recovery lease. Quote the ratio **and** the structural reason in
> the same sentence, or a marker will assume you do not know why.

## 7.5 Outgoings, Net Income and Value

Pulling the whole chain together for Kallangur Fair — this is the shape
every Part A and Part B analysis needs:

```
  Base rent (passing)                          $2,132,942
+ Outgoings recoveries                           $173,377
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
= Total passing rental income                  $2,306,319
+ Other income (vending, casual mall leasing,
  electricity profit, media, misc.)              $180,979
+ Rent reviews to 31 Oct 2026                     $42,497
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
= Gross passing income                         $2,529,795
− Total expenditure                             ($625,513)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
= Total passing net operating income           $1,904,282
+ Estimated vacant other income                   $42,000
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
= Estimated fully leased NOI                   $1,946,282
```

Every line verified against the source workbook.

```
Expense ratio = total expenditure / gross passing income
              = 625,513 / 2,529,795 = 24.73%
```

> **The expense ratio is the single most benchmarkable number on this
> page.** It is the Topic 1 operating ratio (`Og / GI`) with real figures.
> 24.7% for a fully leased neighbourhood centre is reasonable; a number
> drifting toward 35% signals either a cost problem or a rent problem,
> and the ratio alone will not tell you which. Decompose it before you
> conclude.

> **Note how much of the income is *not* rent.** Other income plus
> recoveries is $354,356 — **14.0%** of gross passing income. Ancillary
> income is not a rounding error in a neighbourhood centre; it is a
> managed revenue stream, and Part B expects it to be treated as one.

## Checkpoint

1. A landlord replaces the centre's air-conditioning plant for $180,000
   and services it for $11,000. Which is recoverable?
2. A Queensland neighbourhood centre's budget includes $59,225 of land
   tax. How much of that can be recovered from the specialty retail
   tenants?
3. Recoveries are $173,377 against expenditure of $625,513. Is a 27.7%
   recovery rate evidence of poor management?
4. Specialties are charged $118.37/m² against an actual cost of
   $132.69/m². What is the annual shortfall across their 1,465 m², and
   what is it worth in capital value at a 6.5% cap rate?
5. Why can you not answer "how does this compare to budget?" from the
   supplied Kallangur file?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **The $11,000 service is recoverable; the $180,000 replacement is
   not.** Servicing is an operating cost of the premises. Replacing the
   plant is **capital expenditure** — explicitly excluded — and it goes
   to the owner's account (and, per Topic 5, to the cost base and the
   depreciation schedule).
2. **None of it, from tenants on retail shop leases.** Land tax is
   recoverable in most commercial leases but is carved out for
   **Queensland retail shop leases**. Their pro-rata share would have
   been `1,465/4,714 × 59,225 = $18,406` — the landlord wears it.
3. **No — not on that number alone.** The major tenant holds **68.9% of
   the GLA on a zero-recovery lease**, which is a normal commercial
   outcome for an anchor. Strip the major out and the specialties recover
   **89.2%** of *their* pro-rata share — which is the number that
   actually reflects management performance, and it is the one with room
   to improve.
4. ```
   Shortfall = (132.69 − 118.37) × 1,465 = $14.32 × 1,465 ≈ $20,979 p.a.
   Value impact at 6.5%: 20,979 / 0.065 ≈ $322,750
   ```
   Roughly $21,000 a year, worth about **$323,000** of capital value. A
   recovery rate is not an administrative detail.
5. Because the file is an **"Outgoings Estimate"** with a **Budget column
   only** — no actuals, no prior year, no reconciliation statement.
   Saying so, and naming what you would need, is the correct answer. A
   review also needs the excluded non-recurring items (legals, leasing
   fees, promotion fund) added back.
</details>

## Summary

- Outgoings are the landlord's **recoverable operating costs** of the
  premises, charged **in addition to** base rent under a net lease.
- **In:** rates, water, land tax (not QLD retail shop leases), building
  insurance, common-area utilities, shared-area repairs and compliance,
  cleaning, gardening, waste, pest, fire, security, HVAC, management
  fees.
- **Out:** capital expenditure, depreciation and sinking funds, the
  landlord's interest and loan charges, insurance excesses and
  loss-of-profits cover, marketing funds and merchant association
  contributions. Capital, financing or promotional → not an outgoing.
- Three structures: **gross** (built into rent, landlord carries
  escalation risk), **net** (rent plus outgoings, budgeted and
  reconciled), **direct recovery** (landlord pays, then invoices).
- Kallangur Fair, all verified: total expenditure **$625,513** =
  **$132.69/m²** over 4,714 m² GLA; cleaning and management alone are
  **51%**; statutory charges **25.2%**.
- Recoveries **$173,377 = 27.72%** of expenditure — because the major
  (68.9% of area) recovers **nothing**. Specialties recover **89.2%** of
  their own share, at a flat **$118.37/m²** against a **$132.69/m²**
  cost.
- Expense ratio **24.73%** of gross passing income. Other income plus
  recoveries is **14.0%** of gross income.
- `CV = NI / CR` runs both ways: the identified **$63,300** of management
  savings is worth about **$1,055,000** of value at a 6% cap rate.
- The supplied budget has **no actuals column**, and excludes legals,
  leasing fees and promotion fund contributions as "non-recurring". Say
  so rather than inventing a variance.

Carry this into Part B: outgoings recovery rates, the major's zero
recovery, and the $63,300 savings are all quantified initiatives for the
[SAM plan](#/USB244/14-assessment-1-part-b-sam-plan).
