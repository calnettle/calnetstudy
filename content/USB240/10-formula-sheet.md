## Core formulas

### Yield

```
Gross rental yield  =  (weekly rent × 52) / purchase price

Yield (general)     =  Net income / Value
Rearranged:            Net income = Value × Yield
                       Value      = Net income / Yield
```

Worked: $650/week on a $777,500 unit → 650 × 52 = $33,800 / 777,500 = **4.35%**.

### Growth

```
Period growth  =  (this period / last period) − 1

CAGR           =  (end / start)^(1/n) − 1        where n = number of years
```

Worked: $1,441,250 → $1,610,000 = 1,610,000/1,441,250 − 1 = **+11.71%**.

> **Divide the dollar change by the *prior* value, not the current one.**
> A $105,000 rise to $775,000 is 105,000 ÷ **670,000** = 15.7%, not
> 105,000 ÷ 775,000 = 13.5%.

### Effective rent

```
Net effective rent  =  Net face rent × (1 − NET incentive)

Implied discount    =  1 − (effective / face)
```

Worked, Melbourne (net incentive): $503 × (1 − 0.40) = **$301.8**, reported
$301 ✓.
Worked, Brisbane (**gross** incentive): $601 × (1 − 0.36) = $384.6, but
reported effective is **$337** → implied discount **1 − 337/601 = 43.9%**.

### Productivity and occupancy

```
Productivity ($/m²)  =  MAT / GLA
Income per m²        =  (Price × Initial yield) / GLA
```

Worked: Westfield Chermside MAT > $1.2bn / GLA 175,000 m² = **> $6,857/m²**.
Worked: HomeCo Midland $85m × 5.9% = $5,015,000 / 19,958 m² = **$251.28/m²**.

### Workspace

```
NLA required  =  headcount × 10 m²        (PCA Office Building Quality standard)
```

Worked: 240 staff → 2,400 m². At 60% attendance under hybrid working →
1,440 m², a **40% reduction** in requirement.

### Economic base

```
Employment          =  population × (employment : population ratio)
Basic employment    =  employment × basic share
Basic multiplier    =  total employment / basic employment
```

Worked: population 30,000, emp:pop = 1:3 → 10,000 employed; basic:non-basic =
1:1 → 5,000 basic; **multiplier = 2.0**.

### GDP per capita

```
Per-capita growth  =  (1 + GDP growth) / (1 + population growth) − 1
```

Worked: (1.026 / 1.016) − 1 = **0.98% ≈ 1%** against headline 2.6%.

### Descriptive statistics

```
Mean    = sum / count                  =AVERAGE(range)
Median  = middle value when ordered    =MEDIAN(range)
Mode    = most frequent value          =MODE(range)
Range   = max − min                    =MAX(range) − MIN(range)
SD      sample     =STDEV.S(range)     (divides by n−1)
        population =STDEV.P(range)     (divides by n)
```

Property uses the **median** because price distributions are right-skewed —
East Brisbane 2025 houses: median $1,610,000, mean $2,178,858, **35% higher**,
because of one $15m sale in a 74-sale year.

## The classification tables

### Urban growth theories

| Family | Theories | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| **Location** | von Thünen (1826) | **Where** uses sit and what rent they pay |
| **Patterns of growth** | Concentric (Burgess 1925) · Axial/radial · Sector/wedge (Hoyt 1939) · Multiple nuclei (Harris & Ullman 1945) | The **form** growth takes |
| **Growth multiplier** | Economic base · Keynesian · Central place (Christaller 1933) | **Why** a city grows |

| Theory | Growth | Emphasis | Topography | Transport infra. | Redevelopment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concentric (1925) | Inside out | **Distance**, not time | Ignores | Ignores | Ignores except transition zone |
| Axial / radial | Inside out | **Time, distance and cost** | **Accommodates** | **Accommodates** | Ignores |
| Sector / wedge (1939) | Along an axis of similar use | Housing focus (its weakness) | Ignores | **Accommodates** | Ignores |
| Multiple nuclei (1945) | Multi-nuclei | Many areas beyond the CBD | Can accommodate | Can accommodate | Ignores |

| Class | GLA | Anchors | Specialty |
|---|---|---|---|

| Regional | 30,000–50,000 m² | 1 full-line DS, 1 full-line DDS, 1+ supermarket | ~100 |

### Office grades

| Grade | Marker |
|---|---|
| **Premium** | Highest quality; newly constructed; top-of-the-line fixtures, amenities, HVAC and technology |
| **A** | High quality; typically new; sought-after location; may differ on size, floorplate, Green Star/NABERS, finishes |

## Definitions worth memorising verbatim

```
Redevelopment       value of the CLEARED SITE > value in its EXISTING USE

Vacancy rate        % of lettable space currently unoccupied
Net absorption      change in OCCUPIED space over a period
Face rent           advertised / headline rent, BEFORE incentives
Effective rent      rent AFTER incentives are deducted
Incentive           tenant inducement (rent-free, fit-out), as a % of the lease
Yield               annual income ÷ property value

Primary data        you collect it yourself, for this purpose
Secondary data      already collected by others, for another purpose
Quantitative        information about QUANTITIES; measurable difference between points
Qualitative         information about TYPES; can be a name or symbol

Probability sampling      selection probability KNOWN; statistical inference POSSIBLE
Non-probability sampling  selection probability UNKNOWN; inference IMPOSSIBLE;
                          success rests on the LOGIC of the sample

Cross-sectional     many individuals at ONE point in time — a snapshot
Longitudinal        the SAME variables observed repeatedly over time
    trend           a general population, re-sampled
    cohort          a specific subpopulation followed
    panel           THE SAME PEOPLE followed  (HILDA)

Triangulation       different data collection methods in one study, to enhance
                    validity and reliability

Cumulative causation   growth stimulates growth; reversible by negative
                       externalities such as congestion costs
Metropolitan primacy   excessive concentration of population and development in
                       the main city, often to the detriment of others
Urbanisation           rural-to-urban migration for employment
```

## Exam traps

> **1. Movement along ≠ shift.** A price change causes a movement along the
> curve, which is a change in **quantity demanded**. Only a non-price driver
> shifts the curve and changes **demand**.

> **2. Technical recession has no magnitude threshold.** −0.8% then −1.0%
> qualifies. "Two consecutive quarters of *significant* negative growth" is a
> distractor.

> **3. Burgess: LOW income nearest the CBD, and DISTANCE, not time.**
> Both halves are reversed in distractors.

> **4. Axial/radial ACCOMMODATES topography and infrastructure.** It does not
> ignore them. The concentric model ignores both.

> **5. Keynesian is a growth MULTIPLIER theory.** Pattern theories draw maps;
> multiplier theories explain mechanisms.

> **6. Economic base theory** — not "the multiplier theory" — uses the
> **employment-to-population ratio**.

> **7. Immobility is an ECONOMIC characteristic, not physical.** So are large
> economic units, durability, scarcity, and land being unproductive by itself.
> Physical = shape, size, topography, views, improvements. Legal = tenure,
> restrictions at law.

> **8. Redevelopment is cleared-site value versus existing-use value.**
> "Value uplift" is the wrong answer.

> **9. A positive vendor discount is a PREMIUM.** East Brisbane houses went
> from −7.3% (a genuine discount, Q4 2024) to +13.3% (sold above listing,
> Q4 2025). Reading the sign backwards inverts the conclusion.

> **10. A falling yield has two causes.** Value rose faster than rent (strong
> market), or net income fell as costs rose (squeezed investor). PRD says
> Brisbane's case is the second. Say which side of the fraction moved.

> **11. Face rent ≠ effective rent, and you cannot cross a GROSS incentive
> with a NET rent.** Melbourne and Perth (net incentives) reconcile exactly;
> Sydney, Brisbane and Adelaide (gross incentives) do not. Brisbane's 36%
> gross incentive implies a **43.9%** discount off net face.

> **12. GLA narrows a retail classification; the ANCHOR MIX decides it.**
> City Centre and Super Regional both exceed 100,000 m²; City Centre typically
> has **no** supermarket, Super Regional has **two or more**.

> **13. Office grading is self-regulated, sub-market relative, and not
> permanent.** High occupancy is not a grade indicator.

> **14. Primary/secondary and quantitative/qualitative are independent
> axes.** Every one of the four combinations exists. Numerals do not make data
> quantitative — a postcode is a label.

> **15. STDEV.S for a sample, STDEV.P for a population.** Property data is
> almost always a sample.

> **16. Acknowledgement of Country: anyone. Welcome to Country: only a
> Traditional Owner of that land** — and the Welcome conveys consent.

> **17. Queensland Heritage Act 1992 = non-Indigenous. ACH Act 2003 =
> Indigenous.** Two Acts, two years, two departments. The Queensland Heritage
> Register excludes Aboriginal heritage unless the place has **shared** values.

> **18. Burra Charter is disjunctive.** Aesthetic **OR** historic **OR**
> scientific **OR** social — any one is enough.

> **19. Name the geography beside every number.** Brisbane 8.0% (LGA) and
> Greater Brisbane 12.5% (GCCSA) are the same year. Brisbane median household
> income is $2,155 (SA4), $2,068 (LGA) or $1,849 (GCCSA) — a 16.5% spread.

> **20. Name the base of every percentage.** The 2026 pipeline is 33%
> commercial by **value** and 7.7% by **count**. Both are true; only one is
> stated.

> **21. "Pipeline" is not "supply".** Every 2026 project in the postcode 4169
> file is at status **"Possible"** — not approved, tendered or financed. Filter
> by status before you total, and say which statuses you counted.

> **22. Percentage change is measured against the PRIOR value.** 105,000 on a
> current 775,000 is 15.7%, not 13.5%.

## Errors and gaps found in the unit material

> **A. The capital-city 5-year growth claim is wrong on the slide's own

> **B. The "Development Pipeline — East Brisbane (4169)" table is the whole
> postcode, and 98%+ of it is Kangaroo Point.** East Brisbane suburb accounts
> for $1.1m of $189.6m in 2025 (**0.58%**) and $3.6m of $306.5m in 2026

> **D. The "growth" column in every supplied comparison workbook is zero.**
> All years, all suburbs, all files. Compute growth yourself.

> **E. PRD's published median rent of $898 excludes an outlier.** The raw Q4
> 2025 median of 29 East Brisbane house listings is **$895**; excluding a
> $200/week one-bedroom "house" gives $897.5 → $898.

> **F. The Colliers Q1 2026 report contradicts itself twice.** National metro

> **G. The Week 5 office indicator table is Q4 2022 data** — three-plus years

> **H. "25 large format" on the Paddington Central slide is copied from the

> **I. Population figures for "Brisbane" appear as 2,568,927 (2021, Lecture 2)

> **J. The Week 5 tutorial's Brisbane household counts fail a sanity check.**
> Population ÷ households gives 3.75–4.17 persons per household across all

> **K. Admin dates are inconsistent between decks.** The Week 1 deck says the
> A2 report is due 23 May 2026; Lecture 13 and the Week 12 deck say 22 May. The

> lecturer speaking, not from any slide — **confirm on the QUT central exam
> timetable**.

> **L. Lecture 13's revision checklist asks about Week 10 and 11 material that
> the same lecture marks as not examinable.** Chart selection is also taught in
> Week 7, which *is* examinable — learn it from there and you are covered
