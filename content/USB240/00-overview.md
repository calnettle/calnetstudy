## Learning outcomes

1. **Apply urban economic theory** to analyse markets and provide
   recommendations for investment.
2. **Collect, analyse and evaluate property data** to identify creative
   solutions.
3. **Communicate applied urban economic theory**, orally and in writing, to
   a range of audiences in a team environment.
4. **Evaluate the impact** of development, business ethics and cultural and
   historical influences on property market analysis, applying cultural
   awareness and community and professional responsibility.

## Assessment

| # | Assessment | Weight | When | Individual / Group |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Group oral presentation — progress report on one topic | 15% | Tutorials, Weeks 5–10 (marks 7 May 2026) | Group of 4 |
| 2 | Market Analysis Report | 35% | Week 12 — 22 May 2026 (results 3 June) | Group of 4 |
| 3 | Final examination | 50% | Central exam period | Individual |

Assessment 2 is a **25-page report**, excluding executive summary, table of
contents, references and appendices.

**The site both assessments hang off:** 26 Hampton Street, East Brisbane,
QLD 4169 — approximately **1,395 m² of land** with **822 m² of existing
office/warehouse space**, entry from Hampton Street. The task is to analyse
all four sectors (residential, commercial, retail, industrial) and recommend
one sector or a mixed use.

> **Site cover matters when you argue "highest and best use".** 822 m² of
> improvements on 1,395 m² of land is **59% site cover**. That tells you the
> existing improvement is a low-rise shed, not a tower — so the redevelopment
> question is really "does the cleared-site value exceed the value of the
> existing office/warehouse use?" That is the textbook definition of
> redevelopment (see Note 01, §1.9), and it is the frame the exam expects.

### What the lecturer said about each section

**Section A.** Do the **Canvas online quizzes** relentlessly — her exact
words: *"any of the multiple choice questions in those online quizzes, they
might literally pop up in the exam itself."* Technique: read the whole stem
and every option; eliminate the clearly wrong first. Her worked example:

> *If you wanted to collect a representative sample of data from the
> residents of the suburb of Windsor, which primary data collection method
> would you select?* (a) Survey (b) ABS census data (c) Case studies
> (d) On-the-ground observations.
>
> **(b)** is out — the ABS is *secondary*. **(c)** is out — a case study
> compares across cases (Windsor vs East Brisbane vs Kangaroo Point), it does
> not sample within one. **(d)** is primary but you cannot reach every
> resident and the observer introduces bias. **Answer: (a) survey.**

**Section B.** Half a page to a page each. Quality of key points beats
quantity. **Underline** your key points. **Answer every sub-part** — she
said students routinely answer the overarching question and skip parts (a)
and (b). If it asks for five examples, give exactly five: *"the amount of
times that I read last year's paper where it says provide 3 examples and the
student only provides 1 — if I had a dollar for every single one of them,
I'd buy my own car."*

**Section C.** You get data plus a scenario, and you must analyse it, draw a
graph and recommend. 4–6 pages, headings for each sub-part, full sentences,
underlined answers. **Do not start writing.** Read *both* questions' data
first and pick the one you can graph and analyse best. Asking for a second
booklet is normal in this unit.

### A1 → Section B, A2 → Section C

Her biggest single hint: *"reading back to your A1 and A2 group assignment
is a really big clue. A1 relating to section B of the exam, and A2 relating
to section C."* Re-read both, and re-read the markers' comments.

## How these notes are built

Same structure as EFB335 and USB244: concept → formula → worked example with
real numbers → the trap that costs marks, with a checkpoint quiz in a
tap-to-reveal block at the end of each major section.

Every number was recomputed before it was written down. Where the source
material disagrees with itself — and it does, in about a dozen places — the
note shows the arithmetic and says which reading is right rather than
quietly picking one.
