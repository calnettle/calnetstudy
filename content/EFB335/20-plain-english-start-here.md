## The Whole Unit in One Story

You have money and you want it to grow. Everything in EFB335 answers one
of five questions about that money.

| The question | Topics | The short answer |
|---|---|---|
| **How do I measure how it went, and how bumpy it was?** | 1–2 | Return is what you made. Risk is how much it swung. Mixing assets that don't move together cuts the swings for free |
| **What return should I expect for the risk I take?** | 3–4 | Only the risk you can't diversify away gets paid. The CAPM measures it with one number (beta). Factor models use a few |
| **Can anyone beat the market?** | 5 | Mostly no, because prices already include what people know. That's the efficient market hypothesis |
| **So how do the professionals invest?** | 6–9 | Shares (Topic 6), bonds (7), derivatives to protect or adjust a portfolio (8), and alternatives like hedge funds and private equity (9) |
| **How do we judge whether a manager was any good?** | 10 | Return per unit of risk: Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen, information ratio, Sortino |

Hold that story in your head and every topic has a place to go.

sentences*. That's exactly what these notes train.

## How to Use a Plain-English Note

Each note has the same six parts. Work them in this order:

1. **The one-sentence version.** Read it, then say it out loud without looking.
2. **The picture.** An everyday comparison. It's the hook your memory hangs the idea on.
3. **The words you need.** Only the terms the exam uses, each in plain words.
4. **One worked example.** Do it on paper with the note covered, then check.
5. **How the exam asks it.** A model answer shape you can reuse.
6. **Say it back.** Five questions. Answer each out loud or on paper *before* opening the answer.

> **The rule that makes this work: explain it to an imaginary friend.**
> If you can't explain an idea in plain words to someone who has never
> done finance, you don't have it yet. Go back to the picture. That's
> the test, and it's also exactly what a 4-mark written answer is.

## Five Words That Come Up Everywhere

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Return** | What you made, as a percentage of what you put in |
| **Risk (σ, standard deviation)** | How much the return bounces around its average. Bigger σ, bumpier ride |
| **Risk-free rate (Rf)** | What you'd earn with no risk at all, like a government bill. Every other return is judged against it |
| **Beta (β)** | How much an investment moves when the whole market moves. β = 1 moves with the market, β = 2 moves twice as much |
| **Alpha (α)** | Return above what the risk deserved. Positive alpha means the manager added something |

## When You're Stuck

- **Lost in a formula?** Ask "what is this measuring, in words?" Every formula in this unit is either *return*, *risk*, or *return per unit of risk*.
- **Lost in a written question?** Define the term, explain the idea, give one example, say what it means for an investor. That four-step shape gets most of the marks on most questions.
- **Lost in a whole topic?** Go back to its picture and its one sentence. Then do the worked example. Then stop for the day on that topic.
