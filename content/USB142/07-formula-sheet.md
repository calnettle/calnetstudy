# Formula Sheet & Exam Traps

Every formula, measurement rule, definition and statutory reference in
USB142 on one page, followed by the traps that actually cost marks.

Nothing here is new — it is the compressed version of Topics 1 to 5. If a
line does not make sense, the topic file it came from will.

## Measurement — the two areas

```
GROSS BUILDING AREA (GBA)  ≡ IPMS External Floor Area
  Main building footprint, measured to the OUTSIDE of all external walls.
  INCLUDES generally accessible balconies and verandahs (external
  horizontal structure with a balustrade to the open sides).
  EXCLUDES patios and terraces not integral to the structural
  construction.
  Permanent yard improvements may be added separately:
      GBA = external measurement of Building A + Building B

GROSS FLOOR AREA (GFA)     ≡ IPMS Floor Area
  Sum of the floor area of all spaces within a normally horizontal,
  permanent, load-bearing structure, measured to the INTERNAL face
  of the wall, summed over EVERY level.
  INCLUDES walls, columns, stairs, staircase openings, lift shafts,
  atria, corridors, entrance halls, cupboards, wardrobes, skirtings,
  mouldings, cornices.
  EXCLUDES decks, verandahs, carports.
```

**GBA outside, GFA inside.** GBA is a single-level footprint; GFA is
summed across levels. On a two-storey house GFA normally exceeds GBA.

## The three ratios

```
Land to Building Ratio    LBR = GBA ÷ Land Area        →  1 : x    x ≤ 1
Floor Space Ratio         FSR = GFA ÷ Land Area        →  1 : x    x may exceed 1
Site Coverage Ratio       SCR = (GBA + covering improvements) ÷ Land Area
                                                       →  1 : x    x ≤ 1
```

| | Numerator | Can exceed 1? | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LBR** | GBA — the footprint | No | Dwelling footprint against site |
| **FSR** | GFA — all levels | **Yes** | Development intensity |
| **SCR** | GBA + awnings, pools, balconies, decks, patios, pergolas, terraces, verandas, carports | No | Site + ground improvements |

`1 : x` and `x × 100%` are the same number. Quote whichever the question
asks for, and be consistent.

**SCR numerator — in:** structures that cover the site.
**SCR numerator — out:** gardens, landscaping, fencing. They cover
nothing.

## Scale conversion

```
metres → millimetres      × 1000
millimetres → metres      ÷ 1000

1:100   drawn length = real length in mm ÷ 100
1:200   drawn length = real length in mm ÷ 200

Worked:  6.4 m at 1:100  →  6,400 mm ÷ 100  =  64.0 mm
         6.4 m at 1:200  →  6,400 mm ÷ 200  =  32.0 mm
```

A1 requires 1:100 by hand; 1:200 is acceptable for very large dwellings.
**Always mark the scale on the drawing.** Residential measurement is
expressed in **millimetres**.

## IPMS measurement fundamentals — the seven

1. Use the unit of measurement commonly adopted in the jurisdiction.
2. All measurements except height are taken **horizontally**.
3. Support measurement with computer-generated drawings where available;
   prefer **annotated dimensions** over scaling alone.
4. Verify measurements independently on site where possible.
5. Accuracy must be sufficient for the purpose.
6. Measure **level by level**.
7. Where IPMS is silent, extrapolate the principles consistently **and
   explain what you did**.

Underlying principle: **physical measurement is a matter of fact, not an
opinion.**

## The characteristic sets

```
PHYSICAL       Land (shape, size, topography, views, exposure, soil,
               bearing capacity) + Improvements
                 TO the land — drainage, filling, clearing, levelling
                 ON the land — structures, fencing, attachments

INSTITUTIONAL  Rights available to be traded
                 = the tenurial system  LESS  restrictions imposed at law

ECONOMIC       1. Immobility
               2. Large economic units
               3. Durability
               4. Scarcity
               5. Land is unproductive on its own
```

**Land value is the residual** after labour, capital and management are
paid their rewards.

## Legal interests — the short list

**Co-ownership (PLA Part V, ss 33–43; LTA Part 5, ss 56–57)**

```
The four unities (joint tenancy needs ALL four):
   1. Possession   2. Interest   3. Title   4. Time

Tenancy in common needs unity of POSSESSION only.

Right of survivorship:  joint tenancy YES,  tenancy in common NO.
Default where no intention is expressed:  TENANTS IN COMMON  — s35 PLA.
A partnership CANNOT hold property. The partners do — s35(2)(b) PLA.
```

**Easements — the four elements (*Re Ellenborough Park* [1956] Ch 131)**

```
1. A dominant and a servient tenement
2. The easement must accommodate the DOMINANT LAND (not its owner)
3. The tenements must not be held AND occupied by the same person
     — modified in Qld by s86 LTA: registration permitted where the
       benefited and burdened lots have the same registered owner
4. The right must be capable of forming the subject matter of a grant
     — too wide: weather protection, a view, privacy, mere recreation
     — sufficient: enjoyment of a park, general air flow
```

*Hill v Tupper* (1863): a commercial advantage of a business on the land
is a **personal right**, not an easement.

Extinguishment: agreement, abandonment (more than non-use — intention
required), consolidation of the tenements, alteration in use of the
dominant land, or court order under **s181 PLA**. A court may **create**
an easement under **s180 PLA**.

**Mortgages**

```
Mortgagor = borrower, gives the mortgage
Mortgagee = lender, takes the mortgage

Definition: LTA s4 — "includes a charge on a lot for securing money or
money's worth"
Indefeasibility on registration — s184 LTA

Power of sale preconditions — s84 PLA
   default  →  notice specifying default and requiring remedy
            →  default continued 30 days

Mortgagee's duty on sale — s85 PLA: reasonable care to sell at market
value. Damages under s85(3) = market value − sale amount.
s85(1A) (Mortgagor Protection Amendment Act 2008): advertise adequately,
obtain reliable evidence of value, maintain the property, sell by
auction unless another way is appropriate.

Purchaser protection — s87 PLA; s79 and s184 LTA.
First registered mortgage takes priority over the second on default.
```

**Leases**

```
Lease  = exclusive possession, fixed or determinable term
         Radaich v Smith (1959) 101 CLR 109
Licence = personal right, not an interest in land, no exclusive use

Long lease  > 3 years  → must be registered to secure tenure
Short lease ≤ 3 years  → registration not required

Lessee holds the leasehold estate; lessor holds the REVERSION.
Residential form: General Tenancy Agreement (Form 18a),
   Residential Tenancies and Rooming Accommodation Act 2008
```

**Other encumbrances**

- **Caveat** — prohibits registration of an instrument affecting the
  caveator's claimed interest until withdrawn, removed, lapsed or
  cancelled. Buys time. Protects **equitable** interests.
  Caveator lodges; caveatee is the registered proprietor.
- **Covenant** — written agreement to do or refrain from doing something.
  Positive or negative (restrictive). Enforceable only with notice;
  listing on title at acquisition is **constructive notice**.
- **Profit à prendre** — right to enter land and remove part of its
  substance (soil, produce, animals, sand, gravel, timber). May be
  granted to any person, not only another landowner.

**Community title** — Body Corporate and Community Management Act 1997.
Lot / common property / lot entitlement / community management statement
/ body corporate. Body corporate responsibilities: maintain common
property, insurance, financial management, records and information,
conduct of occupiers.

## Market value

> *"Market Value is the estimated amount for which a property should
> exchange on the date of valuation between a willing buyer and a willing
> seller in an arm's length transaction after proper marketing wherein
> the parties had each acted knowledgeably, prudently, and without
> compulsion."* — API / IVSC

**Learn this verbatim.** The slide calls it "the most important definition
in your course," and one of the Week 11 common pitfalls is *not including
the reference for the definition of market value* — so cite API/IVSC when
you quote it.

Five determinants: **estimated** amount (an opinion, never "accurate") ·
at a **specific date** · **willing buyer, willing seller** · **arm's
length** · **prudently, knowledgeably, without compulsion**.

**Spencer v The Commonwealth of Australia** (1907) 5 CLR 418 — the test is
not what a seller could actually have obtained on the day, but what a
person desiring to buy would have had to pay to a vendor **willing to sell
at a fair price but not desirous to sell**, both parties perfectly
acquainted with the land and cognisant of all circumstances affecting its
value.

```
PRICE  = what you pay          — an historical FACT
COST   = inputs to produce     — a calculated fact
VALUE  = what it pays you      — an OPINION, not a fact

Cost ≠ Value ≠ Price.  The asking price may or may not be market value.
```

Other bases of value: insurance, investment, replacement, going concern,
forced sale (liquidation), special value to the owner, heritage.

## Highest and best use

**API:** the use of an asset that maximises its potential and that is
physically possible, legally permissible and financially feasible.
**IVS (2025):** physically possible (where applicable), financially
feasible, legally allowed, and resulting in the highest value.

```
APPLICATION ORDER — this is not the order either definition lists them in

  1. Legal permissibility     ─┐ screens: apply FIRST
  2. Physical possibility     ─┘
  3. Financial feasibility    ─┐ rankings: apply SECOND
  4. Maximum productivity     ─┘
```

- **Legally permissible** — title details, zoning and land use planning,
  uses forbidden by regulation, deed restrictions and covenants,
  easements. Then check the **neighbourhood plan**.
- **Physically possible** — size, shape, topography, load-bearing
  capacity of soil, existing structures, flooding.
- **Financially feasible** — revenue must justify demolition and
  construction costs **plus a developer's profit**. Site analysis +
  demand analysis + supply analysis.
- **Maximum productivity** — highest net return **based on the investment
  required**; formally, the highest **NPV** or **IRR**.

```
REDEVELOPMENT TEST
  Redevelop when:
      value of land as vacant  >  value as improved − demolition costs

  RR = capital value of the building, falling with age (physical
       depreciation + economic obsolescence)
  SS = capital value of the cleared site (assumed constant)
  Before year L: RR > SS → hold.   After year L: SS > RR → redevelop.
```

**Economic life** (API): the total period over which an asset is expected
to generate economic benefits for one or more users. Not the physical
life.

**Most probable use** — where a higher use exists but is not feasible, the
valuer values the most probable use.

## Discounting

```
        n     CF_t
NPV =   Σ  ──────────   −   initial investment
       t=1  (1 + i)^t

i = required return / discount rate      t = number of periods

Worked (i = 4%):  −10,000 now; 2,000 / 3,000 / 3,000 / 4,000 / 4,000
  1,923.08 + 2,773.67 + 2,666.99 + 3,419.22 + 3,287.71 = 14,070.66
  14,070.66 − 10,000 = NPV 4,070.66          undiscounted total: 6,000
```

**IRR** = the discount rate at which NPV = 0. Higher is more desirable.

## The five valuation methodologies

| Method | Alt. name | Process | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market approach** | Direct market evidence | Compare the subject with comparable recent sales | Fundamental basis of value; applicable across all sectors. **Primary method for residential** and the basis for all the others |
| **Summation** | Cost approach | Land value (deduced from sale evidence) + depreciated value of improvements | Less often used; secondary or check method. Insurance/replacement valuations, assets of nominal value |
| **Capitalisation** | Income approach | `CV = NI ÷ Y` | Income-producing assets with stable cash flows. A "static model" — future cash flows assumed equal to current |
| **Hypothetical development** | Reverse feasibility | Backwards analysis from future forecast revenue of a proposed development, less development costs | Preliminary assessment of a redevelopment site. "Back of the beer coaster" — ignores the time value of money |
| **Discounted cash flow** | Cash flow analysis | Spreadsheet tracking forecast income and expenditure over the lifecycle; output is NPV | Multi-tenanted properties, development sites. Gives NPV and IRR. Garbage in, garbage out |

```
CAPITALISATION            Capital Value = Net Income ÷ Yield

                          CV = NI ÷ Y      →      Y = NI ÷ CV
                                           →     NI = CV × Y
```

**Primary and secondary methods.** Every valuation is carried out on a
primary method and **checked** with a secondary method. The secondary
method for one property type may be the primary method for another. The
choice depends on available data and the nature of the property.
**Never average the two methods.**

## Steps in the market approach

```
1. Confirm the property details — legal description, location, rights
2. Collect sales evidence and other data, including economic and market data
3. Inspect the property
4. Compare each sale to the subject — property rights, value drivers
5. Construct a SALES MATRIX and a SALES LINE CONTINUUM
6. Apply the secondary valuation method
7. Explain the analysis in the valuation rationale, including adjustments
8. Adopt a market value in the valuation statement
      — NOT a range
      — NEVER an average of the sales evidence
```

**Direction of adjustment**

```
Sale is BETTER than the subject   →  the subject is worth LESS
Sale is SIMILAR to the subject    →  the value is SIMILAR
Sale is WORSE than the subject    →  the subject is worth MORE
```

Adjusting for date of sale: market rising → increase; market falling →
decrease; market stable → no change.

**Out-of-line sales** — a sale that does not meet the conditions defining
market value. Types: special value to the purchaser, family sales,
adjoining owner sales, intercompany sales, sales of resumed or acquired
properties, forced or mortgagee sales, sales in extreme economic
conditions (bubble or bust).

```
Sufficient other evidence available   →  ignore the out-of-line sale
Insufficient evidence                 →  use it, but ADJUST it
Either way, it may be used to mark the END of a value range (cap/collar)
```

Land comparison points: lot size, surrounding development, area/location,
aspect, topography, frontage, pedestrian and vehicle access, highest and
best use.

## Economic and market analysis

**Macro factors** — GDP (total value of finished goods and services
produced within a country's borders in a period), the **cash rate** (the
interest rate on unsecured overnight loans between banks; direct
relationship with interest rates), **CPI** (household inflation), the
**unemployment rate**, government initiatives.

**Micro factors** — location and general locality; position (noise, views,
climate); access (transport, amenities); services (public utilities); site
characteristics (size, shape, frontage, topography); building
characteristics (bedrooms, bathrooms, garage, pool, condition); and
emotional factors (street appeal, landscaping, neighbours, fashion and
social trends, colours, natural disasters, crime, schools).

The report's economic analysis section works through four indicators in
this order:

```
1. GDP and GSP
2. Cash rate and interest rate
3. CPI and inflation
4. Unemployment rate
```

The market analysis section works from largest geography to smallest:

```
1. Australia   →   2. Queensland   →   3. LGA/Brisbane   →   4. Suburb
```

For both sections the marking question is the same: **is there any
discussion of the implications of the findings, and does the writer lead
the reader to the same conclusions?** A table of statistics with no
interpretation scores nothing.

## The valuation process

```
Valuation instruction  →  Property verification  →  Property inspection
   →  Data sources & collection  →  Sales and market evidence
   →  Economic analysis  →  Market analysis  →  Valuation calculations
   →  Valuation report  →  Valuation fee
```

Instructions must be **in writing** and must record: the interest being
valued; the purpose; the date of valuation; the scope of work; internal or
external inspection; the agreed fee or its basis; information to be
provided by the client; the parties for whom the valuation is prepared;
the timeframe; access arrangements; and, if applicable, the purchase price
and selling agent.

Competency before acceptance: is the valuer qualified, experienced in this
type of work, covered by their level of registration, covered by their
indemnity insurance, and able to access the required data?

Inspection sequence:

```
1. Confirm land and site details
2. External building inspection
3. Internal building inspection
4. Ground and other improvements
```

## Exam traps checklist

Tick these off before you hand anything in.

- [ ] **LBR uses the footprint, FSR uses the GFA.** Swapping them inverts
      both ratios at once. On a two-storey house the FSR should be roughly
      double the LBR — if it isn't, check which area you used.
- [ ] **GBA is measured outside, GFA inside.** Say which you used.
- [ ] **State what went into the SCR numerator.** Structures in,
      landscaping and fencing out. Three published site-coverage figures
      exist for 80 Bertha Street because nobody stated their inclusions.
- [ ] **Round, don't truncate, and pick one convention.** 214.979 ÷ 448 is
      47.99%, not 47.98%. Figures not matching calculations is a listed
      Week 11 pitfall.
- [ ] **Age indicates style and construction, not condition.** Observe and
      record condition separately.
- [ ] **Physical supply ≠ economic supply.** The scarcity answer needs the
      locational-requirements point.
- [ ] **The co-ownership default is tenancy in common** (s35 PLA), and
      **a partnership cannot hold property**.
- [ ] **An easement accommodates the dominant *land*, not its owner.**
- [ ] **HABU application order is legal → physical → financial →
      maximally productive.** Neither the API nor the IVS wording is the
      application order.
- [ ] **Maximum productivity is a return on the investment required.**
      Name the base of any profit percentage — profit ÷ total cost and
      profit ÷ end value give different rankings.
- [ ] **Deduct demolition costs** before comparing land-as-vacant with
      value-as-improved.
- [ ] **NPV is discounted.** The undiscounted sum is not an NPV.
- [ ] **Market value is an *estimated* amount and an *opinion*** — never
      describe a valuation as accurate. Cite API/IVSC when you quote the
      definition.
- [ ] **Adopt a single figure, not a range, and never average** — not the
      sales evidence, and not the primary and secondary methods.
- [ ] **Use two valuation methods.** "Only one valuation method used" is
      on the Week 11 pitfall list.
- [ ] **State direction with radial distance** — "4 km north-east of the
      CBD," not "4 km from the CBD." Also give actual distances, not just
      radial ones.
- [ ] **Put the subject on every location map.** A map with no subject
      marked is a listed pitfall.
- [ ] **Cover every required heading**, number the pages, make the table
      of contents match, and make the sections refer to one another. A
      report assembled from independently written sections reads as one
      and is marked as one.
- [ ] **Interpret, don't just tabulate.** Every economic and market
      statistic needs a sentence saying what it means for this property.
