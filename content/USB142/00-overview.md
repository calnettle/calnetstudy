# USB142 — Unit Overview

QUT · Semester 1 · **Residential Valuation** · Dr Jason Chia

The first valuation unit. It teaches you to walk onto a residential site,
describe what you see in language a court would accept, measure it, work
out what the law lets the owner do with it, find comparable sales, and
write a number down that you are prepared to defend.

## Unit Map

| # | Note | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Topic 1 — Property Characteristics and Measurement | Weeks 1–2. Property economics, the three characteristic sets, sectors and participants, location analysis, describing improvements objectively, GBA/GFA, scale drawing, LBR/FSR/SCR |
| 02 | Topic 2 — Legal Interests in Property | Weeks 3–4. Lot on plan, Torrens title, the bundle of rights, co-ownership, community title, easements, mortgages, leases, caveats, covenants, adverse possession, native title |
| 03 | Topic 3 — Market Value and Highest and Best Use | Week 5 + Tutorial 5. The purchase process, the four HABU tests, economic life, market value, *Spencer*, price vs cost vs value, valuers in court |
| 04 | Topic 4 — The Market Approach | Week 6. Standards bodies, the five methodologies, sales evidence, out-of-line sales, adjustments, sales matrix, sales line continuum, the Bardon worked example |
| 05 | Topic 5 — Economic Analysis and the Valuation Report | Week 8 + Week 11. Capital and user markets, economic and market indicators, the property clock, inspection procedure, the 17-section report, API disclaimers, common pitfalls |
| 06 | Extra Practice Questions | Exam-style questions across all topics |
| 07 | Formula Sheet & Exam Traps | Every formula, definition and trap on one page |

at the end of each deck:

1. Property economics and the characteristics that drive value
2. Objective description and measurement of land and improvements
3. Legal interests, tenure and encumbrances
4. Market value and highest and best use
5. The market approach and sales analysis
6. Economic and market analysis
7. Professional inspection and report writing

## The running case study — 80 Bertha Street, Kalinga

Most worked examples in these notes use one property, because the source
folder contains a complete professional dossier on it:

- **Current title search** (27/03/2025) — Lot 172 on RP19535, fee simple
- **City Plan property holding report** (v32, effective 14/03/2025) — zone,
  neighbourhood plan and 20-odd overlays
- **FloodWise property report** (22/03/2025) — ground levels, AEP flood
  levels, minimum habitable floor level
- **A PropertyPRO short-form mortgage valuation** by WBP Property Group,
  21 June 2021 — market value $1,150,000, with five sales and risk ratings
- **A CoreLogic CMA and property list report** (May 2025) — comparable
  sales, suburb demographics, 20 years of median values
- **Architectural construction drawings** (MR Design + Drafting, May 2021)
  — site area, site coverage, survey levels
- **Cal's own A1 report** — the student answer, complete with the errors
  these notes flag

That is an unusually complete picture: the same property described by a
registered valuer, a council, a titles registry, an architect and a
student, four years apart. Where they disagree, the notes show the
arithmetic and say which reading holds up.

## How these notes are built

Same structure as EFB335 and USB244: concept → principle or formula →
worked example with real numbers → the trap that costs marks, with a
checkpoint quiz in a tap-to-reveal block at the end of each major section.

Every number was recomputed before it was written down. Several figures in
the source material do not reconcile — three different site coverage
ratios for the same house, two different flood models, a suburb median
that appears as $1.85m, $2.16m and $2.25m depending on which document you
open. Each of those is shown with its arithmetic rather than quietly
resolved.

## Source material

Nine lecture decks (Weeks 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 11 and the Week 5
tutorial), the API guidance paper ANZPGP-201 on disclaimers, an Opteon
long-form commercial valuation used as a report exemplar, and the
80 Bertha Street dossier listed above. All live in
`1st Year/Semester 1/USB142/`.

**Gaps.** There are no decks for Weeks 7, 9, 10 or 12. Week 6 forward-refers
to Week 9 for "property inspection" and "data sources & collection," and to
Week 11 for "valuation calculations" — the Week 9 content is missing
entirely, and the summation (cost) approach is only ever described, never
worked through with numbers. If the exam asks you to *calculate* a
summation value, these notes cannot help beyond the method statement in
Topic 5; find the Week 9 and 10 recordings.
