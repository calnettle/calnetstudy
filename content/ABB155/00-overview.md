## Learning outcomes

Straight from the Week 1 deck. Both assessments are written against these,
so use their language in your report conclusions.

1. **Identify** how real property rights, legislation and land use planning
   systems influence the use and development of land.
2. **Explain** how Queensland legislation regulates land tenure, property
   interests, and planning control relevant to land use and development.
3. **Apply** foundational concepts of real property law and land use
   planning to analyse defined property and development scenarios.
4. **Use GIS** to identify and illustrate land use patterns, site
   constraints, and planning controls.
5. **Communicate** property and land use planning concepts using clear
   written explanations and basic visual and spatial representations.

### Where the marks actually are

Assessment 1's biggest single criterion is **Redevelopment Constraints and
Opportunities at 14 of 40 marks** — 35% of the assessment. Tenure and
encumbrances is 10. The executive summary and the structure/referencing
criterion are worth only 4 each. Spend your effort accordingly: a beautiful
executive summary on a thin constraints analysis is a 4, not a 7.

Assessment 2 is flat — six criteria at 10 marks each — so no single set can
be skipped. Note that **Set B is scored on correct numbers**, not on
reasoning: "correct answers provided for all three questions" is the only
route to a 7 on that criterion.

## How these notes are built

Concept → rule or method → worked example on the real site or the real
dataset → the trap that costs marks. Checkpoint questions are hidden in
tap-to-reveal blocks at the end of each major section.

Every figure was recomputed before it was written down. Where the source
material contradicts itself — and it does in several places — the note
shows the arithmetic and says which reading holds up, rather than quietly
picking one. Watch for the amber callouts; they are reserved for things
that change an answer.
