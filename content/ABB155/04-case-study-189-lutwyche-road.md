# CASE STUDY — 189 Lutwyche Road, Windsor

The Assessment 1 site, worked from the primary documents in the Canvas
information pack. Every figure here was recomputed and cross-checked
against the title searches, the survey plans and the Council reports, and
the places where the sources disagree are called out rather than smoothed
over.

**The scenario.** You are a graduate consultant in a property and planning
advisory firm. A new client with limited familiarity with the Queensland
legal, property and planning systems wants advice on the redevelopment
potential and constraints of a brownfield site at 189 Lutwyche Road,
Windsor. Report format, 2,500 words plus a 250-word executive summary,
40% of the unit.

> **The brief's own framing line, worth keeping in front of you:** "This
> assignment is a hypothetical academic exercise and is entirely
> independent of any current or future intentions of the site's existing
> owner." Do not write as though a real proposal is on foot.

## 4.2 Who actually owns it

The brief says the site "is currently held by Queensland Health." Both
current title searches say something more precise:

```
REGISTERED OWNER
Dealing No: 715875123     03/07/2014
METRO NORTH HOSPITAL AND HEALTH SERVICE
```

Metro North Hospital and Health Service is a **statutory body** — one of
the Hospital and Health Services established under the *Hospital and Health
Boards Act 2011* (Qld) — not the Department of Health, and not "the State"
in the way the tunnel lessee is. (That Act is not named anywhere in the
information pack; verify the citation before using it in the report.)

> **Write the registered owner as it appears on the title, then explain the
> relationship.** The Assessment 1 CRA's top band asks you to "precisely
> identify all relevant surface and subsurface tenures." Repeating the
> brief's shorthand when the primary document in front of you says something
> different is exactly the kind of imprecision that costs you the 7. It also
> matters practically: a disposal by a Hospital and Health Service engages a
> different approval pathway from a departmental disposal, and you cannot
> reason about that if you have written down the wrong entity.

Note the historical layer. The **Form 21 to survey plan SP253385**, signed
in 2012, records the then registered owner as **"The State of Queensland
(represented by Department of Health)"**, signed by the Manager, Property,
Health Planning & Infrastructure Division "as delegate for the Minister for
Health." The 03/07/2014 dealing transferred the holding into the Hospital
and Health Service. So the site has moved from department to statutory body
within the documents you are given — a nice, cheap detail for the tenure
section.

Both surface titles are structurally identical:

| Encumbrance 1 | Rights and interests reserved to the Crown by **Deed of Grant No. 40021306** (Lot 2 on RP 18495) | Rights and interests reserved to the Crown by **Deed of Grant No. 40021306** (Lot 8 on RP 72098) |
| Administrative advice | 717328912 — **HERITGE SITE**, *Queensland Heritage Act 1992*, lodged 21/06/2016, CURRENT | 717328913 — same, lodged 21/06/2016, CURRENT |
| Unregistered dealings | NIL | NIL |
| Search date | 02/03/2026 11:27 | 02/03/2026 11:27 |

Three observations that earn marks:

**1. The registered encumbrances are thin.** No mortgages, no easements, no
covenants, no leases registered against either surface title, and no
unregistered dealings. For a 2.4-hectare inner-city holding that is
unusual, and it is a *positive* finding — say so. The constraints on this
site are almost entirely (a) the Crown reservation, (b) the heritage
administrative advice, (c) the separately-tenured volume beneath, and
(d) the planning scheme. None of those is a private third-party interest
that has to be bought out.

**2. "HERITGE SITE" is the Registry's own typo**, reproduced on both
titles. Quote it as it appears if you quote it; do not silently correct a
primary source in a legal report.

**3. The two Deeds of Grant reference *different* parcels.** Lot 2's
reservation traces to Lot 2 on RP18495 itself; Lot 36's traces to **Lot 8
on RP 72098** — an older parcel that Lot 36 descends from. Same Deed of
Grant number (40021306), different original lots. That is ordinary in a
long chain of subdivision, and it is evidence of the path-dependence Topic
2 talks about: the 2013 title still points back to a nineteenth-century
grant.

### The 1916 plan

RP18495 is the historical curiosity of the pack: a hand-drawn 1916 plan
(Cat. No. E5-1916), Parish of **Enoggera**, County of **Stanley**, prepared
for Chambers, McNab & McNab, Adelaide Street, Brisbane, and annotated
"**Commonwealth Acquisition No. 1682-100**" — consistent with the site's
subsequent life as a military hospital. Areas are in imperial units:

```
Resub. 3 (later Lot 2):   1 acre  0 roods  31.88 perches
Part of Resub. 2:         1 acre  0 roods  33.44 perches

1 acre  = 4,046.856 m²        1 rood = 1,011.714 m²
1 perch = 25.29285 m²

Resub. 3  = 4,046.856 + (31.88 × 25.29285) = 4,046.9 + 806.3 = 4,853.2 m²
Resub. 2  = 4,046.856 + (33.44 × 25.29285) = 4,046.9 + 845.8 = 4,892.6 m²
```

> **Do not read old imperial areas as decimals.** "1.0.31 88/100" is one
> acre, zero roods, thirty-one and eighty-eight hundredths perches — not
> 1.031 of anything. Four roods to the acre, forty perches to the rood. Get
> this wrong and you will report a lot as a tenth of its real size.

## 4.4 The Airport Link Tunnel — the subsurface tenure

This is the heart of the assignment and the part most students get
structurally wrong.

### What the tenure is

```
Current STATE TENURE Search — not a Current Title Search
Title Reference:    40069275
Tenure Reference:   PPL 238134
Lease Type:         PERPETUAL
Purpose:            TRANSPORT FACILITY
Registered lessee:  THE STATE OF QUEENSLAND
                    (REPRESENTED BY DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORT AND MAIN ROADS)
Term:               Lease in perpetuity commencing on 05/12/2014
Area:               40.621700 Ha. (ABOUT)
Date created:       19/12/2014
```

Two things follow immediately.

**It is leasehold tenure, not a lease.** Using Topic 3's distinction
(§3.8): a *lease* is granted by a fee simple owner to a tenant; *leasehold
tenure* is a Crown lease over land where no freehold grant has been made.
This is the second kind. The surface freeholder is **not the lessor**, did
not grant it, cannot vary it and cannot terminate it. If your report says
Metro North leased the subsurface to the State, the tenure criterion is
gone.

**It is one title over an enormous number of parcels.** The description of
land fills most of the 27-page search and lists **547 parcels** across
Brisbane — **537 volumetric lots and 10 non-volumetric** — totalling
40.6217 ha. Volumetric Lot 136 on SP253385 is one of the 537.

```
Lot 136 footprint          3,117 m²
PPL 238134 total area     40.6217 ha = 406,217 m²
Lot 136 share             3,117 / 406,217 = 0.77% of the lease
```

### The lease conditions

Condition **A109** has five limbs, and limb (1) is the one to quote:

> "The lessee shall use the leased land for **transport (State toll road)
> purposes, purposes ancillary to transport, and other commercial and
> community purposes, as approved by the Chief Executive administering the
> *Transport Infrastructure Act 1994*.**"

The remaining limbs: (2) duty of care for the leased land; (3) control of
pest plants and animals under the *Land Protection (Pest and Stock
Management) Act 2002*; (4) give the Minister administering the *Land Act
1994* information about the lease on request; (5) comply with all other
statutory obligations of the *Land Act 1994* "and with any other Act which
deals with the construction, maintenance, management and use of a State toll
road **on, above or below the surface of the ground**."

Condition **I66** is a broad indemnity from the lessee to the Minister
administering the *Land Act 1994* and to the State against all claims
arising out of the granting of the lease or the lessee's use and occupation,
except to the extent of the Indemnified parties' own negligence.

> **"On, above or below the surface of the ground" is the phrase that does
> the constraint work.** The lease conditions expressly contemplate
> regulation of activity *above* the tunnel, not merely inside it. That is
> the textual hook for arguing that surface redevelopment over Lot 136
> engages the State's interest — much stronger than an assertion that "the
> tunnel constrains development."

> **The tunnel is not just "State land" — it is a financed, mortgaged,
> operating infrastructure asset with a private operator until 2053.** That

> corridor needs consent from a chain that runs State → sublessee → operator
> → mortgagee, and a mortgagee whose security is a toll-road sublease has
> every incentive to resist works that could interrupt tolling. Saying this
> is worth more than another paragraph on the *Land Act*.

### Where the tunnel actually sits — the survey plan

**SP253385** is a **volumetric** format plan, "Plan of Lots 36 & 136,
Cancelling Lot 8 on SP197113," surveyed by RPS Australia East Pty Ltd,
survey completed 20/02/2012, Parish Enoggera, County Stanley, meridian MGA
Zone 56. The plan's own notes:

- "Lot 36 is a **Standard Format Lot**."
- "Lot 136 is **bounded by vertical planes**."
- "The **footprint of Lot 136 coincides with the external boundaries of the
  base parcel between stations 34-15-3-9**."
- "Vertices of volumetric lot are not marked. **Vertices below ground.**"
- Datum for levels: PM 34676, RL 5.411 AHD.

And the numbers, taken directly off the plan:

```
Footprint area of Lot 136 .......   3,117 m²
Lot 36 ..........................   1.884 ha  = 18,840 m²
Lot 136 volume (3D diagram) .....  95,000 m³
```

**Reconciliation 1 — how much of the site is affected.**

```
Lot 136 footprint / Lot 36        = 3,117 / 18,840  = 16.5%
Lot 136 footprint / whole holding = 3,117 / 23,755  = 13.1%
```

**Reconciliation 2 — does the volume make sense?**

```
Mean height of Lot 136   = 95,000 / 3,117              = 30.5 m
A simple prism from RL +1.409 down to RL −31.886       = 33.295 m tall
Prism volume             = 3,117 × 33.295              = 103,781 m³
Actual / prism           = 95,000 / 103,781            = 0.915
```

The 8.5% shortfall is exactly what you would expect: the three-dimensional
diagram shows a **stepped upper surface**, higher over part of the lot and
lower over the rest, rather than a flat lid. The volume, the footprint and
the reduced levels are mutually consistent — which is a good sign that you
have read all three correctly.

**Reconciliation 3 — how deep is it?** Ground levels surveyed across the
site run **RL 13.9 to RL 17.0 AHD**. Matching each station on the 3D
diagram to the ground level at the same station:

| Station | Ground level | Top of Lot 136 | Cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 34 | RL 16.2 | RL 6.804 | **9.4 m** |
| 33 | RL 16.4 | RL 5.327 | 11.1 m |
| 30 | RL 15.8 | RL 4.652 | 11.1 m |
| 9 | RL 16.3 | RL 4.234 | 12.1 m |
| 31 | RL 13.9 | RL 1.409 | 12.5 m |
| 32 | RL 14.0 | RL 1.409 | 12.6 m |

Base levels run down to about **RL −31.9** at most stations (RL −33.025 at
the deepest labelled point, RL −19.4 at the shallowest), so:

```
Deepest floor below ground at stn 34:  16.2 − (−31.886) = 48.1 m
Shallowest floor below ground:         16.2 − (−19.407) = 35.6 m
```

> **This is the number that makes or breaks the redevelopment argument.**
> The State's volume begins roughly **9 to 13 metres below the surface**.
> That is not "deep enough to ignore" — it is squarely within the depth of
> a two- or three-level basement car park. So the constraint is not
> abstract: over that 3,117 m² footprint, basement excavation, piling and
> deep footings are the specific works at risk, and the design response is
> to put the basement somewhere else. A report that quantifies the cover
> depth is doing site-specific analysis; one that says "the tunnel limits
> excavation" is describing.

### How the volumetric lot was created

The **Form 21** attached to SP253385 carries a handwritten note that is the
single most useful legal fact in the whole pack:

> "The State is purchasing Lot 136 on SP253385 **by agreement for a purpose
> set out in the *Acquisition of Land Act 1967***. Accordingly, this plan is
> exempt from local government approval under **schedules 19 and 26 of the
> *Sustainable Planning Regulation 2009***."

The plan also records the reconfiguration: existing title **50783954, Lot 8
on SP197113 → new Lots 36 & 136**. That is why Lot 36's title search shows
"Previous Title: 50783954."

> **Cite the *Acquisition of Land Act 1967*, and be careful what you claim
> the title search says.** The state tenure search names the *Transport
> Infrastructure Act 1994*, the *Land Act 1994* and the *Land Protection
> (Pest and Stock Management) Act 2002* **inside the lease conditions**, but
> it does **not** state the section under which the lease was granted. If
> you write "the title search notes the lease was granted under s 84C of
> the *Transport Infrastructure Act 1994* and s 17 of the *Land Act 1994*,"
> you are attributing to the document something it does not say. The
> documented mechanism for acquiring the volume is *acquisition by
> agreement under the Acquisition of Land Act 1967*, recorded on the Form
> 21. Cite that, and cite the Acts named in the conditions for the operating
> framework. Confirm the granting provision with your tutor if you want to
> name one.

### The listing

| | |
|---|---|
| Place name | **Rosemount Hospital** |
| Queensland Heritage Register number | **602145** |
| Date of entry on the Register | **24 September 1999** |
| Statute | ***Queensland Heritage Act 1992*** (Qld) |
| City Plan overlay | Heritage overlay — **State heritage place** sub-category *and* **Local heritage place** sub-category |
| Title notation | Administrative advices 717328912 (Lot 2) and 717328913 (Lot 36), lodged **21/06/2016**, status CURRENT |

> **The listing predates the title notation by nearly seventeen years, and
> that ordering is legally significant.** The place was entered on the
> Register in 1999; the administrative advice was lodged on the titles in
> 2016. The statutory effect flows from **entry in the Register under the
> *Queensland Heritage Act 1992***, not from the note on the title — the
> note is a notification device. A purchaser in 2005 who relied on a clean
> title search would still have been bound. Making that point demonstrates
> you understand what an administrative advice is, which is more than
> reciting that the site is heritage listed.

> **Look at the twelfth entry: `L136_SP253385`.** The heritage listing
> extends over the **volumetric tunnel lot**, not just the surface parcels.
> So the two headline constraints on this site are not independent layers
> stacked on different parts of the land — they overlap in the same volume.
> Almost nobody spots this, and it is a genuine "sophisticated,
> site-specific analysis" point for the 14-mark criterion. Confirm it
> against the official Queensland Heritage Register extract under s 33 of
> the Act before you rely on it in the report; the Council page is a
> secondary source.

### What the listing does to redevelopment

Heritage narrows the *legally permissible* set; it does not close it.
Demolition, façade removal, or work diminishing the cultural heritage
significance of the place requires approval and will attract objection.
Adaptive reuse of the existing buildings, with new development placed on
the parts of the holding away from the significant fabric, is the
conventional response and the one the constraints actually support.

Both reports are correct. They answer different questions: a **holding**
report aggregates every zone and overlay touching *any* lot in the holding,
and a **lot** report describes one lot. The lot report for Lot 136 also
demonstrates that a volumetric parcel is a lot in its own right for
planning purposes — it has an address, a zone, a neighbourhood plan
precinct and overlays.

> **Never quote a holding-report zone as though it applies to the whole
> site.** "The site is zoned HDR1, DC2 and SR1" means three different parts
> of the holding carry three different zones — not that any one part enjoys
> all three. Sport and recreation (Local) is a very different development
> envelope from High density residential. The Assessment 1 CRA rewards
> "site-specific" analysis; mapping which zone sits where, and saying so, is
> exactly that. Use the City Plan interactive mapping to work out the split
> before you write the built-form section.

### The overlay stack

Both reports agree on these, and each is a separate assessment trigger:

  development intrudes into an airport's OLS or PANS-OPS, advice from CASA
  should be sought.
- **Heritage** — State heritage place *and* local heritage place
  sub-categories.
- **Critical infrastructure and movement network** — critical infrastructure
  and movement planning area sub-category.
- **Transport air quality corridor** — the overlay's sub-categories include
  a **tunnel ventilation stack** sub-category.
- **Transport noise corridor** — QDC MP4.4 **Noise Category 3**, established
  under Chapter 8B of the *Building Act 1975*.
- **Road hierarchy**, **Bicycle network**, **Streetscape hierarchy**,
  **Community purposes network**, **Industrial amenity** (investigation area
  sub-category), **Potential and actual acid sulfate soils**.
- Holding only: **Flood overlay** (Brisbane River flood planning area 5;

> **Two overlays here are the tunnel wearing a planning hat.** *Critical
> infrastructure and movement network* and the *tunnel ventilation stack*
> sub-category of the transport air quality overlay both exist because of
> the Airport Link. That is the point to make in the constraints section:
> the tunnel constrains the site **twice** — once as a competing tenure in
> the volume, and once as a set of assessment triggers in the planning
> scheme. They are different mechanisms with different decision-makers, and
> conflating them loses the analytical mark.

### Storey and plot-ratio limits

## 4.7 Highest and best use

### The definition to use

The brief adopts the International Valuation Standards Council formulation
and you should quote **the brief's wording**, because that is the standard
you are being marked against:

> "The use that maximises the potential of an asset and produces the
> highest value, subject to the use being physically possible, legally
> permissible, and financially feasible."

Three tests, not four — "maximally productive" is folded into "produces the
highest value." The brief also says you are **not required** to undertake a
detailed financial feasibility analysis, but you **must** demonstrate a
sound understanding of legal permissibility and planning constraints.

> **Do not substitute a different definition.** Other sources — the
> Australian Property Institute, or IVSC exposure drafts for future
> editions — word this differently. The brief tells you which definition
> governs. Swapping it in mid-report, or citing an unreleased exposure
> draft, invites a referencing query and gains nothing.

### Running the three tests on this site

**Physically possible.**

```
Whole holding                        23,755 m²
Lot 136 footprint (State volume)      3,117 m²  = 13.1% of the holding
Unaffected by the volumetric lot     20,638 m²  = 86.9%
Cover over the top of Lot 136          9.4 – 12.6 m
```

So the tunnel sterilises deep works over about an eighth of the holding and
leaves seven eighths structurally unencumbered at depth. The physical
answer is not "no" — it is "**not there**." Deep basements, piling and
excavation go on the 86.9%; anything over the 3,117 m² needs shallow
foundations, a lightweight structure, or open space, and detailed
geotechnical and structural justification either way.

**Legally permissible.** Freehold fee simple gives the widest possible
private rights (Topic 3, §3.6), but three things narrow them: the Crown
reservation on both titles; the *Queensland Heritage Act 1992* listing over
the whole place including the volumetric lot; and the State's perpetual
lease with its private sublessee, operator and mortgagee to 2053. The
planning scheme cuts the other way — HDR1, DC2, the Lutwyche Road corridor
neighbourhood plan and inclusion in the Priority Infrastructure Area all
signal that Council wants intensification here.

**Financially feasible** (at the level the brief asks for). Heritage-led
adaptive reuse works *with* the statutory constraint rather than against
it, avoiding a demolition approval that is unlikely to be granted and
certain to be contested. Concentrating new floorspace off the tunnel
footprint avoids the highest-cost, highest-risk engineering. Both moves
reduce approval risk, which is the dominant feasibility variable on a site
like this.

### The defensible conclusion

A **differentiated, staged, mixed-use redevelopment**:

1. **Adaptive reuse** of the heritage-listed Rosemount buildings for
   compatible non-residential uses — health, community, education — which
   preserves cultural heritage significance and suits the DC2 District
   centre zone.
2. **New higher-density residential and mixed-use floorspace** on the parts
   of the holding zoned HDR1 and DC2 that lie **clear of the Lot 136
   footprint**, where deep basements are achievable.
3. **Low-intensity uses over the tunnel footprint** — landscape, open
   space, at-grade parking, or a lightweight structure with shallow
   foundations.
4. **Early engagement** with the Department of Environment, Tourism,
   Science and Innovation (heritage), the Department of Transport and Main

**Q1. A colleague writes: "The surface owner has leased the subsurface to the State for the Airport Link Tunnel." What is wrong with that sentence?**

It reverses the tenure. The tunnel is held under a **Crown lease** —
leasehold tenure, PPL 238134, granted by the State to itself through the
Department of Transport and Main Roads. A commercial lease is granted by a
fee simple owner to a tenant; Metro North HHS is not the lessor here, did
not grant the interest, and cannot vary or terminate it. The volume was
separated from the surface parcel by a volumetric survey (SP253385) and
acquired by the State by agreement for a purpose under the *Acquisition of
Land Act 1967*.

**Q2. Quantify the tunnel constraint. What proportion of the holding is affected, and at what depth?**

```
Lot 136 footprint             3,117 m²
Holding title area           23,755 m²
Share                    3,117 / 23,755 = 13.1%
(of Lot 36 alone)        3,117 / 18,840 = 16.5%
```

Cover from the ground surface to the top of the volumetric lot ranges from
about **9.4 m** (station 34: GL 16.2 less RL 6.804) to about **12.6 m**
(station 32: GL 14.0 less RL 1.409). The floor sits around RL −31.9, some
**48 m** below the surface at the deepest labelled point.

**Q3. Why does the depth figure change your advice rather than just decorate it?**

Because 9–13 m is basement depth. A two- or three-level basement car park
on an inner-city redevelopment sits exactly in that band, so the constraint
bites on the most ordinary element of the scheme. Had the roof of the
volume been 40 m down, the tunnel would be a due-diligence footnote. Because
it is not, the design response — put the basement on the 86.9% of the
holding that is clear — is a real planning decision that follows from a
number.

**Q4. The title searches show no mortgages, easements or covenants on the surface lots. Is that good news? Explain.**

Yes, and it is worth stating. There are no private third-party interests to
negotiate out, no registered rights of way to design around, and no
unregistered dealings pending. The constraints are all public-law
constraints — the Crown reservation, the heritage listing, the planning
scheme — plus the separately-tenured volume beneath. Public-law constraints
are managed through approval processes rather than bought out, which shapes
the programme and the risk profile quite differently from a site cluttered
with registered easements.

**Q5. Give three parties whose consent or input a scheme over the tunnel footprint would realistically need, and why.**

The **Department of Transport and Main Roads** as registered lessee and the
Chief Executive administering the *Transport Infrastructure Act 1994*, whose
approval condition A109 contemplates. The **tunnel operator** — the
BrisConnections sublease chain, now APL Co / TQ APL Asset Co — which holds
the whole of the land under sublease to 2053 and runs the asset.
**Westpac**, as mortgagee over both subleases, whose security is the
tolling revenue stream. (Also defensible: the heritage regulator under the
*Queensland Heritage Act 1992*, and Council through pre-lodgement advice.)

**Q6. The BCC Property Holding Report says the site is zoned HDR1, DC2 and SR1. Can you design an 8-storey residential building anywhere on the holding on that basis?**

No. A holding report aggregates the zones applying to *any* lot in the
holding; it does not mean every part carries every zone. HDR1 permits up to
8 storeys, but only over the part of the holding actually zoned HDR1 — the
DC2 and SR1 parts have different purposes and envelopes, and SR1 (Sport and
recreation, Local) is not a residential zone at all. You would need the City
Plan interactive mapping to establish which zone applies where before
locating any built form.

**Q7. Why does it matter that the heritage listing was entered on the Register in 1999 but only noted on the titles in 2016?**

Because the statutory effect comes from entry in the Queensland Heritage
Register under the *Queensland Heritage Act 1992*, not from the note on the
title. The administrative advice is a notification device, so a purchaser
between 1999 and 2016 who searched the title and found nothing would still
have been bound by the heritage controls. It is a concrete illustration that
the Torrens register does not capture every constraint on land, and that
title searching alone is not sufficient due diligence.
