## The concepts the unit named

The workbook is useful for one thing: it names, in the unit's own words, the
vocabulary weeks 2–6 were assessed on.

**Week 2 — study design.** Observational study, survey, clinical trial,
experiment, longitudinal study. Sources of bias, including coverage/exclusion
bias and seasonality.

**Week 3 — data ethics.** The **utilitarian lens** (weigh aggregate outcomes)
and the **rights lens** (equal consideration, non-discrimination, honest
disclosure). Applied to a decision, not to a person.

**Weeks 4–5 — interpretation errors.** Five named pitfalls:

- McNamara's fallacy
- Conditional probability
- Correlation vs causation
- Regression to the mean
- Questionable research practices

**Week 6 — the data science pipeline.** Four stages, in order:

1. **Data input and design** — where the data comes from, sampling design
2. **Handling and processing** — what is retained, what is discarded, cleaning
   and de-identification
3. **Analysis and interpretation** — what internal *and* external users must
   keep in mind
4. **Openness and reuse** — open data, the **FAIR** principles (Findable,
   Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) and the **CARE** principles for
   Indigenous data governance

## What Cal submitted

Short version, in case the answers need referring back to:

- **Task 1** — the ABS *Average Weekly Earnings, Australia* survey, classified
  as a biannual survey and therefore an observational study. Strength: it
  captures very large employers, cutting variance. Biases: excluded sectors
  (defence, agriculture/forestry/fishing, private household employees, anyone
  unpaid in the reference week) and seasonality, since irregular payments like
  bonuses and overtime are out of scope.
- **Task 2** — argued Jennifer should *not* present the merged-data analysis.
  The dataset omits very-low-value and unclear-title dwellings, and the 80%
  forecast accuracy is unevenly distributed, improving the average while
  worsening two vulnerable locations. Both lenses were then applied to include
  and exclude in a 2×2 table.
- **Task 3.1** — regression to the mean and questionable research practices.
  A rollout that coincided with an unusually high Q4 makes the Q1 fall look
  causal; the Q2 rebound is just the return to the average, not the system
  failing. Judging success off a single cherry-picked quarter is the QRP.
- **Task 3.2** — conditional probability, in mortgage lending. "Most defaults
  are high-leverage loans" does not make most high-leverage loans defaults;
  the base rate is missing. Viewed through the rights lens, penalising
  borrowers on a group statistic rather than their own risk profile is unjust,
  and the societal implication is delayed home ownership for first home buyers.
- **Task 4** — a multi-suburb supermarket group, walked through all four
  pipeline stages, ending on FAIR, CARE and what the data could be sold for.
