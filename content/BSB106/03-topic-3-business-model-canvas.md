# TOPIC 3 — The Business Model Canvas

The BEC looks outward at the environment. The **Business Model Canvas (BMC)**
looks inward at how one brand actually creates, delivers and captures value.
Assignment 2 is built entirely on it.

## 3.1 The nine blocks

The QUT template prints all nine. Their standard arrangement:

```
┌───────────┬───────────┬─────────────┬──────────────┬───────────┐
│           │    KEY    │             │   CUSTOMER   │           │
│    KEY    │ ACTIVITIES│    VALUE    │RELATIONSHIPS │ CUSTOMER  │
│  PARTNERS ├───────────┤ PROPOSITIONS├──────────────┤ SEGMENTS  │
│           │    KEY    │             │              │           │
│           │ RESOURCES │             │   CHANNELS   │           │
├───────────┴───────────┴──────┬──────┴──────────────┴───────────┤
│      COST STRUCTURE          │        REVENUE STREAMS          │
└──────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Block | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Customer segments** | Who are we creating value for? |
| **Value propositions** | What problem do we solve, and why us? |
| **Channels** | How do we reach and deliver to them? |
| **Customer relationships** | What kind of relationship do they expect? |
| **Revenue streams** | What are they willing to pay for, and how? |
| **Key resources** | What assets does the model require? |
| **Key activities** | What must we do well? |
| **Key partners** | Who do we rely on to do the rest? |
| **Cost structure** | What does all this cost, and where? |

## 3.2 Desirability, feasibility, viability

Assignment 2 does not ask for nine blocks in isolation. It asks how the value
proposition *"is achieved via the business model components of desirability,
feasibility, and viability."* Those three lenses group the blocks:

```
       FEASIBILITY              VALUE               DESIRABILITY
   can we actually build   the proposition     do customers want it
   and deliver this?         at the centre     and can we reach them?

   Key partners                                Customer segments
   Key activities          VALUE PROPOSITIONS  Customer relationships
   Key resources                               Channels

                        VIABILITY
              does the money work over time?
              Cost structure · Revenue streams
```

- **Desirability** — the right-hand side. Is there a customer, do they want
  this, can we reach and keep them?
- **Feasibility** — the left-hand side. Do we have, or can we get, the
  partners, activities and resources to deliver it?
- **Viability** — the bottom. Does revenue exceed cost, durably?

> **Every recommendation must name the blocks it changes and the lens it sits
> under.** "Gillette should launch a subscription" is an idea. "A subscription
> reconfigures **Channels**, **Customer relationships** and **Revenue
> streams** — strengthening desirability through direct engagement and
> viability through recurring revenue" is an analysis. The rubric rewards the
> second, and the difference is about fifteen words.

## 3.3 Worked example — Gillette

Team A's submitted BMC, block by block, with what each demonstrates.

**Customer segments.** Males aged 18 and above interested in shaving and men's
grooming (B2C). Plus a B2B segment: large corporations including hotels
(Hilton), airlines (United Airlines) and hospitals.

**Value proposition.** *"The best a man can get"* — quality, accessibility and
male-targeted branding. The customer problems addressed are **accessibility**
and **comfort when grooming**. Stated strengths: distribution reach, heavy R&D
funding, clear packaging. Stated weakness: **dependence on one product type**.

**Channels.** Supermarkets, convenience stores, pharmacies, specialist
retailers (Target, Shaver Shop), plus Gillette's own online store and Amazon.
Team A framed this through the "place" element of the 4Ps.

**Customer relationships.** Social media service and engagement (Facebook,
Instagram), surveys and feedback collection through the Gillette website.

**Revenue streams.** Sales and subscriptions of shaving and personal-care
products, across online and in-store B2C plus B2B wholesaling. Pricing
approaches: **volume discounting, bundling, and loss leaders**.

**Key resources.** Human resources, branding, marketing, R&D, a multinational
distribution network, and patents/IP giving brand exclusivity.

**Key activities.** Product development through research, safety and
sensitisation testing, extensive marketing and advertising, distribution, and
customer service.

**Key partners.** Retailers, suppliers, e-commerce platforms, advertising
agencies, research institutions.

**Cost structure.** Analysed through **value chain analysis**: raw materials
(inbound logistics), manufacturing (operations), distribution (outbound
logistics), advertising (marketing and sales), plus support activities in R&D
and HR.

### The self-diagnosis inside that canvas

Two entries do a lot of work if you notice them together:

```
Value proposition weakness:  "dependence on one product type"
Revenue streams:             "loss leader" pricing on handles,
                             margin recovered on blade refills
```

That is the razor-and-blades model stated in the canvas. It explains why
Gillette's installed base of handle owners is a **Key Resource** rather than
just a sales history — and why a supply disruption that empties shelves is more
dangerous than a one-off sales loss. If shoppers switch handle systems, the
blade annuity goes with them. Assignment 3 turns on precisely this.

> **A "weakness" written into a Value proposition block is a gift to your own
> recommendations section.** Team A wrote "dependence on one product type" and
> then, three slides later, recommended diversifying into beard care and
> skincare. That is a canvas doing its job. If your BMC contains no tension at
> all, your recommendations will have nothing to attach to.

## 3.4 Supporting frameworks

### Porter's generic strategies

Porter (1985). Two dimensions: source of advantage, and competitive scope.

```
                    COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE
                 Low cost          Differentiation
              ┌──────────────────┬────────────────────┐
       Broad  │  Cost leadership │  Differentiation   │
  S C O P E   ├──────────────────┼────────────────────┤
      Narrow  │   Cost focus     │Differentiation focus│
              └──────────────────┴────────────────────┘
```

> **Both the A2 deck and the A3 report claim Gillette's direct-to-consumer
> subscription "aligns with Porter's Differentiation Focus Strategy". It does
> not.** A *focus* strategy is defined by **narrow scope** — one segment, one
> niche, one geography. Selling a subscription direct to the entire Australian
> male grooming market is the broadest scope Gillette has. The correct label is
> **broad differentiation**. It would only be differentiation *focus* if the
> subscription targeted a deliberately narrow segment — sensitive-skin
> sufferers, say, or a single metropolitan delivery zone. Getting the scope
> wrong inverts the strategy's whole logic, and it is a one-word fix.

### Value chain analysis

Porter's value chain splits a firm's activities into primary and support, so
cost and margin can be located rather than guessed.

| Primary activities | In Gillette's case |
|---|---|
| Inbound logistics | Raw materials — steel strip, plastics, chemicals |
| Operations | Manufacturing and assembly |
| Outbound logistics | Distribution to retailers and, increasingly, consumers |
| Marketing and sales | Advertising, celebrity endorsement, shelf presence |
| Service | Customer support, warranty, social-media response |

| Support activities |
|---|
| Firm infrastructure · Human resource management · Technology development (R&D) · Procurement |

The BMC's **Cost structure** block is where value chain analysis lands. Saying
"costs include manufacturing and marketing" is a list; saying "the value chain
locates the largest controllable cost in outbound logistics, which is exactly
what the port disruption attacks" is analysis.

### The marketing mix — 4Ps

```
Product   what is sold, and its features, quality, range
Price     level, discounts, bundling, loss leaders
Place     where and how it is available
Promotion advertising, endorsement, social media, in-store display
```

Team A used **Place** to justify the Channels block, and Price implicitly
through "volume discounting, bundling and loss leader approaches". The 4Ps map
cleanly onto BMC blocks:

| 4P | BMC block |
|---|---|
| Product | Value propositions |
| Price | Revenue streams |
| Place | Channels |
| Promotion | Customer relationships and Channels |

> **Do not run the 4Ps and the BMC as two separate analyses.** They overlap
> almost completely. Use the 4Ps as a *checklist* to test whether a BMC block is
> thin — if you cannot state Gillette's price architecture, the Revenue streams
> block is under-done — and then write it once, in the BMC.

## 3.5 Connecting the BEC to the BMC

Assignment 2 requires analysing the BMC *"in relation to the business
environment impacts you identified in Assignment 1"*. That connection is the
assessment, not a preamble to it.

The mechanism: **each environmental force lands on specific blocks.**

| BEC finding (A1) | BMC blocks it hits | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation compresses discretionary spending | Revenue streams, Customer segments | Threat |
| Buyer power of supermarkets (49.1% of value) | Channels, Key partners, Cost structure | Threat |
| Consumers seeking sustainable products | Value propositions, Key activities, Key resources | Opportunity |
| E-commerce and digital growth | Channels, Customer relationships, Revenue streams | Opportunity |
| Supply-chain disruption risk | Key partners, Key activities, Cost structure | Threat |
| Rising rivalry from Dollar Shave Club, Harry's, BIC, Schick | Value propositions, Channels, Revenue streams | Threat |

Team A's three recommendations, and what each actually changed:

| Horizon | Recommendation | Blocks changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Short** | Exclusive bundles and limited editions in supermarkets and online; secure premium shelf space and branded displays | Key partners, Channels |
| **Medium** | Direct-to-consumer subscription model | Channels, Revenue streams, Customer relationships |
| **Long** | AI mobile app analysing skin type and shaving habits to personalise routines and recommendations | Value propositions, Key activities, Key resources, Customer relationships |

Note the escalation: the short-term move touches two blocks and needs no new
capability; the long-term move touches four and needs data-science capability
Gillette does not currently list among its Key resources. That escalation is
the point of splitting recommendations by horizon.

> **A recommendation that changes no BMC block is not a business-model
> recommendation.** "Increase advertising spend" changes nothing structural —
> it is more of an existing Key activity. "Shift from retail-mediated to direct
> customer relationships, adding first-party data as a Key resource" is a
> business-model change. Assignment 2 asks for the second kind.

## 3.6 Ethical and legal considerations

Assignment 2 requires recommendations *"noting ethical and legal
considerations"* — a distinct rubric line, not decoration. Anchor each to the
specific recommendation.

| Recommendation | Legal | Ethical |
|---|---|---|
| Bundles and premium shelf space | Australian Consumer Law on honest advertising and transparent pricing; anti-competitive conduct rules on exclusive shelf arrangements | Not using scale to foreclose smaller rivals from shelf space |
| DTC subscription | Australian Consumer Law on refunds and **automatic renewals**; **Privacy Act 1988** obligations on stored customer data | Transparent shipping timelines; genuinely easy opt-out; sustainable delivery |
| AI personalisation app | Privacy Act obligations on biometric and image data; consent for collection | Avoiding manipulative personalisation; being clear what images are used for |
| Sustainability claims | ACL prohibition on misleading environmental claims | **Greenwashing** — P&G already faces litigation over Charmin's environmental claims |

> **Subscription auto-renewal is the specific legal risk of the specific
> recommendation.** Generic sentences ("the company must act ethically and
> comply with the law") earn nothing. Team A's deck got this right, naming
> refund policies and automatic renewal under the DTC recommendation and data
> obligations under the AI app. Tie the law to the mechanism.

## Checkpoint

<details><summary>1. Which BMC blocks sit under desirability, feasibility and viability?</summary>

```
Desirability  Customer segments · Customer relationships · Channels
Feasibility   Key partners · Key activities · Key resources
Viability     Cost structure · Revenue streams
Centre        Value propositions
```

The value proposition is the hinge: desirability asks whether customers want
it, feasibility whether you can deliver it, viability whether the money works.
</details>

<details><summary>2. A team recommends "Gillette should improve its social media presence." Rewrite it as a business-model recommendation.</summary>

As written it changes nothing — social media engagement is already in Team A's
Customer relationships block, so this is "do the existing activity more".

A business-model version: *"Shift customer relationships from broadcast social
engagement to owned, data-generating relationships by launching a first-party
account and subscription platform. This adds first-party customer data as a
**Key resource**, converts **Channels** from wholly retail-mediated to
omnichannel, and adds recurring subscription income to **Revenue streams**."*

Same broad idea; now it names blocks, adds a resource, and changes the revenue
architecture.
</details>

<details><summary>3. Why is a nationwide DTC subscription broad differentiation rather than differentiation focus?</summary>

Because **scope** is the second axis of Porter's generic strategies, and
nationwide sale to the whole male grooming market is broad scope. Focus
strategies serve a deliberately narrow segment.

The advantage source (differentiation — convenience, personalisation, brand)
is right. The scope is wrong. Say **broad differentiation**, or narrow the
recommendation until "focus" becomes true.
</details>

<details><summary>4. Gillette sells handles near cost and makes margin on blades. Which BMC blocks does this involve, and what risk does it create?</summary>

Primarily **Revenue streams** (the pricing architecture: loss leader plus
recurring consumables) and **Key resources** (the installed base of handle
owners, which is what makes the annuity possible). It also shapes **Cost
structure**, since handle production is deliberately run at little or no
margin.

The risk: the model depends on retention. Anything that pushes a customer to
buy a *different* handle system — a stockout, a cheaper subscription rival,
private label — does not cost one sale, it costs the whole future blade
stream from that customer. This is why Team A's canvas listing "dependence on
one product type" as a weakness matters, and why the port disruption in
Assignment 3 is more dangerous than it first appears.
</details>

## Summary

- Nine blocks, grouped into **desirability** (segments, relationships,
  channels), **feasibility** (partners, activities, resources) and
  **viability** (costs, revenue), with the value proposition at the centre.
- Every recommendation names the blocks it changes and the lens it sits under.
- Gillette's model is razor-and-blades: loss-leader handles, margin on refills,
  installed base as the real Key resource, and "dependence on one product type"
  as the stated weakness.
- Porter's generic strategies have **two** axes. Nationwide DTC is broad
  **differentiation**, not differentiation *focus*.
- Value chain analysis populates the Cost structure block; the 4Ps map onto
  Value propositions, Revenue streams and Channels.
- The BEC-to-BMC link is the assessment: name the environmental force, name
  the blocks it lands on, name the direction.
- Tie legal and ethical points to the specific mechanism — auto-renewal to
  subscriptions, Privacy Act to data collection, greenwashing to sustainability
  claims.
