# Framework Sheet and Exam Traps

Everything the unit uses, compressed onto one page, plus the definitions worth
memorising verbatim and the verified numbers from the supplied sources. No
worked examples here — those live in Topics 1 to 5. This is the page to read on

## The two canvases — what goes where

The single most common structural error in the unit is putting an entry on the
wrong canvas.

```
BUSINESS ENVIRONMENT CANVAS          BUSINESS MODEL CANVAS
external — the world acting          internal — how this firm creates,
on the organisation                  delivers and captures value

┌──────────────┬──────────────┐      ┌────┬────┬─────┬────┬────┐
│  KEY TRENDS  │ MACRO-ECON.  │      │ KP │ KA │     │ CR │    │
│              │   FORCES     │      │    ├────┤ VP  ├────┤ CS │
├──────────────┼──────────────┤      │    │ KR │     │ CH │    │
│  INDUSTRY    │   MARKET     │      ├────┴────┴──┬──┴────┴────┤
│   FORCES     │   FORCES     │      │   COST     │  REVENUE   │
└──────────────┴──────────────┘      └────────────┴────────────┘

max 60 words per quadrant            max 60 words per segment
```

The dividing test is **ownership, not topic**. "Retailers hold half the
category's value" is environment. "We are contracted to these retailers" is
business model. If a sentence could only be written by someone with inside
knowledge of the firm, it belongs on the BMC.

## PESTLE

```
P  Political       trade regulation, tariffs, geopolitical exposure
E  Economic        purchasing power, interest rates, cost of living
S  Socio-cultural  values, demographics, media and influence
T  Technological   channels, automation, data, e-commerce
L  Legal           consumer law, privacy, advertising standards
E  Environmental   packaging, waste, climate, supply-chain resilience
```

Each entry: **issue → impact on this organisation → opportunity or threat.**

Economic changes what money is worth. Environmental changes the physical world.

## Macro-economic indicators

| Indicator | Direction that helps the firm |
|---|---|
| GDP growth | Up — room to invest and grow revenue |
| Inflation | Down — raises input, commodity and transport cost; erodes purchasing power |
| Unemployment | Low, within limits — more household income, but adds to inflation |
| Consumer spending | Up, and shifting towards your category |
| Monetary policy | Lower rates — cheaper capital, faster demand |
| Fiscal policy | Expansionary — transfers and spending lift disposable income |

**RBA target: inflation, 2–3% on average over time**, alongside a full
employment objective. ~4.5% is a **NAIRU estimate**, not an unemployment
target.

## Porter's Five Forces

```
                    THREAT OF NEW ENTRANTS
                            │
                            ▼
 BARGAINING POWER  →  RIVALRY AMONG   ←  BARGAINING POWER
   OF SUPPLIERS       EXISTING FIRMS      OF BUYERS
                            ▲
                            │
                  THREAT OF SUBSTITUTES
```

| Force | Strong when |
|---|---|
| New entrants | Low capital needs, weak brands, light regulation, **growing market** |
| Supplier power | Few suppliers, no input substitutes, suppliers not dependent on you |
| Buyer power | Few large buyers, low switching costs, buyers can backward-integrate |
| Substitutes | Cheap, convenient alternatives from **outside** the industry |
| Rivalry | Many similar players, slow growth, low differentiation, high exit costs |

Before any force: **define players, buyers and suppliers.** In packaged goods
the buyers are retailers, not shoppers.

Market growth enters two forces with opposite signs — it **raises** the entrant
threat and **lowers** rivalry.

> **Five Forces is a market framework, not a company framework.** The same firm
> faces different forces in every market it competes in. Name the market before
> you run it, and say why you chose that market.

## Market structures

| Structure | Sellers | Product | Entry | Price power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect competition | Very many | Identical | Free | None — price taker |
| Monopolistic competition | Many | Differentiated | Easy | Some, from branding |
| Oligopoly | Few, each large | Either | Hard | Substantial; rivals react |
| Monopoly | **One** | Unique, no close substitute | Blocked | Full, subject to regulation |

A dominant firm with 60% share in a market with live rivals is a **concentrated
oligopoly with a dominant firm**, not a monopoly.

## The BCG growth–share matrix

```
              HIGH market growth
                     │
      STAR           │       QUESTION MARK
  invest to hold     │      fix, sell or kill
─────────────────────┼─────────────────────
    CASH COW         │           DOG
  milk, fund others  │      divest or reposition
                     │
              LOW market growth

  HIGH relative ←─────────────→ LOW relative
   market share                 market share
```

```
Relative market share = your share ÷ largest competitor's share
                        crossing point 1.0

RMS > 1.0  you lead the market        → "high"
RMS < 1.0  someone else leads         → "low"
```

Vertical axis: market growth rate, conventionally split at 10% a year, lower in
mature consumer markets. **State the cut-off you use.**

| Cell | Cash | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Star | Generates and consumes | Invest to defend leadership; it becomes tomorrow's Cash Cow |
| Cash Cow | Net generator | Milk it; fund Stars and Question Marks; defend share cheaply |
| Question Mark | Consumes | Commit heavily or exit. Do not drip-feed |
| Dog | Roughly neutral | Divest, harvest, or reposition into a niche where RMS > 1.0 |

## Growth arithmetic

```
CAGR = (End value / Start value)^(1 / number of years) − 1
```

The exponent uses **years elapsed**, so 2020 → 2025 is 5, not 6.

Three different growth numbers appear in every industry profile and they are
not interchangeable:

- **single-year growth** — the most recent year only
- **historical CAGR** — over the review period
- **forecast CAGR** — over the forecast period

Quote every rate **with its period** and say which of the three it is.

> **Total growth divided by the number of years is not a CAGR.** It ignores
> compounding and overstates the rate. 21.9% over five years is 4.04% a year,
> not 4.38%.

## Consumer buying behaviour

Stimulus–response: external stimuli and the marketing mix enter the buyer's
**"black box"**, interact with buyer characteristics and decision processes,
and produce a decision (Ramya & Mohamed Ali, 2016).

```
Who is the market, and how much power do they have?
What do they buy?   Why do they buy?   Who is involved in buying?
How do they buy?    When do they buy?  Where do they buy?
```

| Factor group | Components |
|---|---|
| Psychological | Motivation; perception |
| Social | Family; reference groups; roles and status |
| Cultural | Culture; sub-culture; social class |
| Economic | Personal and family income; income expectations; savings; liquid assets; consumer credit |
| Personal | Age; occupation; income; lifestyle |

```
Biogenic needs      arise from physiological tension (thirst, hunger)
Psychogenic needs   arise from psychological tension (recognition, esteem)

Disposable income     = gross income − tax and compulsory deductions
Discretionary income  = disposable income − cost of basic necessities
```

Shopping goods, durables and luxuries are bought out of **discretionary**
income, which compresses faster than disposable income when necessities rise.

under. A recommendation that changes no block is a budget decision, not a
business-model change.

## The Double Diamond

```
              PROBLEM DIAMOND              SOLUTION DIAMOND

                 ◆                              ◆
               ╱   ╲                          ╱   ╲
Disruption ──▶╱     ╲────────────────────────╱     ╲──▶ Market
              ╲     ╱                        ╲     ╱     offering
               ╲   ╱                          ╲   ╱
                 ◆                              ◆

           Problem      Problem          Solution     Solution
         articulation  selection        generation   selection
           DIVERGENT   CONVERGENT        DIVERGENT   CONVERGENT
```

UK Design Council's original names: **Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver.**

Five-step convergence for problem selection:

```
1  Remove non-consequential or insubstantial impacts.
2  Remove impacts the current business model can accommodate.  ← the filter
3  Consolidate inter-related impacts into one description.
4  Rank by how challenging each is to the current model.
5  Identify the most significant impact(s).
```

Three-step shape of the whole disruption task:

```
SITUATE  →  IMPACT  →  RESPOND
in the BEC   on the BMC   and revise the BMC
```

## Christensen's disruptive innovation — what it is and is not

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| An entrant attacking from a **low-end** or **new-market** foothold | Any large or damaging change |
| A different value network and business model the incumbent finds structurally awkward to copy | A supply shock, a natural disaster, or a regulatory change |
| Initially cheaper, simpler or inferior, then moving upmarket | A conventional rival exploiting a temporary capability gap |

## Collaborative learning

```
Individual accountability   the degree to which individual students are held
                            accountable for their contribution   (Slavin 1980)

Positive interdependence    the extent to which one group member's performance
                            depends on the others'              (Johnson 1981)

Construct validity          a measure accurately measuring what it intends to
                            measure                             (Messick 1995)
```

| Misalignment | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Free-riding** | Not performing a fair share of the work |
| **Social loafing** | Investing less effort than when working individually |
| **Sucker effect** | Being taken for a free-ride, so one member does all the work |
| **Performance-orientation** | Optimising the grade rather than the learning |

| Assessment method | Accountability | Interdependence |
|---|---|---|
| Group assessment | Absent | Fostered |
| Individual assessment | Fostered | Absent |
| Group + intra-group peer assessment | Partly restored | Fostered |

Peer rating biases: friendship, peer pressure, harsh marking after conflict,
strategic up- or down-grading, and the **reciprocity effect** (Magin 2001).
Peer assessment appears valid **formatively** but not **summatively**
(Sridharan, Tai & Boud, 2019). No agreed moderation formula exists.

QUT peer evaluation: **1–7 scale; your mark is the average of the scores
others gave you, excluding your own.** No submission = zero for the component.
An average of 1–2 can void the whole assignment.

## Assessment formatting rules

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| BEC | 1 slide, **60 words per quadrant**, legible in A4 landscape, no references |
| BMC | 1 slide, **60 words per segment**, same conditions |
| A1 written summary | **750 words**, supplied template, Times 12pt, 1.5 spacing, 2.54cm margins |
| Team contract | **4 pages max** plus activity log appendix, research-informed, signed |
| A2 deck | **10 slides max** including the BMC slide, **5 minutes**, plus 5 minutes of questions |
| A3 report | **2,000 words** |
| A3 reflection | **500 words** |
| Word-limit tolerance | **±10%**; content beyond the limit is *not considered* |
| Register | Third person, professional. Audience is the case organisation's senior management, including the CEO |
| Referencing | APA, in written documents only |

```
60   words → 66   at +10%
750  words → 675 – 825
2000 words → 1800 – 2200
500  words → 450 – 550
```

> **Content past the limit is ignored, not penalised — which is worse.** A
> conclusion that falls past the cut earns nothing at all. Cut from the middle,
> never from the end.

## Exam traps checklist

Run this list over any answer before submitting it.

**Canvas placement**

1. Is every BEC entry **external**? Capability, culture, brand equity and IP
   are BMC entries.
2. Does every BEC entry state an **impact on this organisation**, plus an
   opportunity/threat verdict?
3. Could the quadrant be pasted into a competitor's canvas unchanged? Then it
   is not analysis.

**Frameworks**

4. Five Forces run on a **named market**, not on a company.
5. Players, buyers and suppliers **defined** before any force is assessed.
6. Buyers = retailers in packaged goods, not shoppers.
7. Substitutes come from **outside** the industry. A rival business model is
   rivalry.
8. Rivals named from the **right** market.
9. Market growth raises the entrant threat and lowers rivalry — not the same
   sign in both.
10. PESTLE's Economic ≠ Environmental.
11. The RBA targets **inflation**, not unemployment.
12. A dominant share is not a **monopoly**.
13. BCG horizontal axis is **relative** market share, crossing at 1.0. If you
    did not divide, you did not use the framework.
14. BCG growth cut-off **stated**.
15. Porter's **focus** strategies are narrow-scope. Nationwide is broad.
16. Christensen is about low-end and new-market footholds, not supply shocks.

**Numbers**

17. Every percentage has a **base** and a stated **population**.
18. Every growth rate carries its **period** and its type (single-year,
    historical CAGR, forecast CAGR).
19. CAGR computed with the root form, not total growth ÷ years.
20. Share tables sum to 100%; component values reconcile to stated totals.
21. Where two supplied sources disagree, the newer one is used **and the
    disagreement is named in one clause**.
22. Currency columns converted at a constant rate carry **no** exchange-rate
    information.

**Business model and disruption**

23. Every recommendation names the **blocks** it changes and the **lens**.
24. At least three responses generated, with limitations, before one is chosen.
25. Impacts ranked by **persistence**, not size.
26. Assumptions about duration, breadth and severity stated in one sentence,
    early.
27. The revised canvas actually **shows** what changed — including Key
    resources, the block most often left unrevised.
28. Legal and ethical points tied to the specific mechanism: auto-renewal to
    subscriptions, Privacy Act to data collection, greenwashing to
    sustainability claims.

**Teamwork**

29. Free-riding, social loafing, the sucker effect and performance-orientation
    used as four distinct terms.
30. The contract's cited research is consistent with the choices ticked in the
    contract.
31. Activity log kept contemporaneously.
32. Peer evaluation submitted — not submitting forfeits the component.

**Format**

33. Word counts inside the ±10% tolerance, checked per box for the canvases.
34. Third person everywhere except the A3 reflection.
35. APA references in written documents; none on the canvases.

> **The traps in this list are the ones that appear in the actual submitted
> work in the source folder, not hypothetical ones.** Every item from 6 to 16
> corresponds to a real error a real team made and had to live with. Reading
> the list once before you start is cheaper than any of them.
