# EFB231 — Unit Overview

QUT · 1st Year, Semester 1 · **Economics**

Introductory micro *and* macro in one semester. Twelve lectures, twelve
tutorials, one written report and a final exam. These notes cover Lectures
3–12, which is everything the sample exam papers draw on.

**Lecture 1 (introduction) and Lecture 2 (demand and supply) are not in the
source folder.** Their content survives only through Tutorials 1 and 2 —
opportunity cost, capital goods versus factors of production, incentives,
micro versus macro, and the four shift questions on the demand/supply
diagram. Topic 2 of these notes reconstructs enough of the demand-and-supply
apparatus to make the surplus and intervention material work, but if the
exam asks for a pure Lecture 2 question (what shifts demand, what shifts
supply) go back to the textbook chapters on demand and supply.

## Learning outcomes

The unit outline is not in the source folder, so the objectives below are
compiled from the per-lecture OBJECTIVES slides. They are what the lecture
material was actually written against:

1. Define and calculate the four elasticities, and explain what influences
   each.
2. Explain consumer and producer surplus, and how the market price system
   produces efficiency and maximises total surplus.
3. Evaluate government intervention in markets — price controls, taxes,
   quotas and externality correction.
4. Compare accounting and economic measures of cost and profit, and derive
   short-run and long-run cost curves.
5. Determine a firm's profit-maximising output under each of the four market
   structures, and compare their efficiency.
6. Define and measure GDP, distinguish real from nominal, and describe the
   limitations of GDP as a living-standards measure.
7. Define the labour market indicators, the types of unemployment, the CPI
   and its biases.
8. Use the AS–AD and Aggregate Expenditure models to explain output,
   the price level and the multiplier.
9. Explain fiscal stimulus and the RBA's monetary policy transmission
   mechanism, and evaluate the effectiveness of each.
10. Identify the gains from international trade and the welfare effects of
    tariffs.

Lectures 4, 8 and 12 additionally list "align and promote economic analysis
with the UN Sustainable Development Goals" as an objective.

### The written report, in one paragraph

The Residential Tenancies Authority hires you as its economic adviser.
Analyse the Brisbane LGA rental crisis from the March quarter 2022 to the
December quarter 2024 using RTA median rents; model it with demand and
supply; analyse the welfare consequences with consumer and producer
surplus; and evaluate a rent ceiling. **Microeconomics only — the task
sheet says in capitals not to apply any macroeconomics.** Only Lectures 2, 3
and 4 are required.

Marks (40 total, weighting to the unit not stated):

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| KS 1.1 — Application of economic theory/concepts | 10 |
| KS 1.2 — Appropriateness of data presentation and explanation | 6 |
| HO 2.1 — Critical analysis: analysis, evaluation and synthesis via evidence | 16 |
| PC 3.1 — Range of relevant scholarly sources | 4 |
| PC 3.1 — Communication and referencing style | 4 |

Presentation rules that lose marks if ignored: Arial 11pt, 1½ spacing,
2.4 cm margins, page numbers bottom-right, no abstract, no executive
summary, no appendix. **All diagrams must be drawn by you** — copying and
pasting a diagram from another document is marked as copying. Do not cite
textbooks, lecture slides, tutorial notes or definitions.

The folder contains the three recommended readings (Hepburn 2006 on
regulation by prices; Laguerodie & Vergara 2008 on Galbraith and price
controls; Schlee 2008 on expected consumer's surplus), plus AHURI Final
Report 416, the Sisson rent-regulation paper, ABS building-activity
workbooks (`87520034.xlsx`, `87520077.xlsx`), the RTA bond statistics
workbook, and a net-interstate-migration CSV. A completed report sits in
`Finished/`.

## How to use these notes

Each topic note runs concept → model → worked example with real numbers →
the trap. Every number in them has been recomputed from the source data;
where the unit's own material disagrees with the arithmetic, the note says
so and shows the algebra. Those disagreements are listed at the top of note
10 so you can take them to a tutor in one go.

The practice questions in note 09 are built from the two sample exam papers
and the tutorial problem sets. Do those before the formula sheet, not after.
