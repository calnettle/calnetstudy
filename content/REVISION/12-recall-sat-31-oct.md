2. Same two assets. Minimum-variance weight in asset 1?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Cov = 0.3 × 20 × 10 = 60. w₁ = (100 − 60) ÷ (400 + 100 − 120) = 40 ÷ 380 = **10.5%**.

**Revision notes**

- **Cov(1,2) = ρ σ₁ σ₂**; **ρ = Cov ÷ (σ₁σ₂)**, from −1 to +1.
- **σp² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2w₁w₂Cov₁₂.** The sheet only gives the double-sum form Σ Σ wᵢwⱼCov(i,j).
- **Minimum-variance weight** w₁ = (σ₂² − Cov) ÷ (σ₁² + σ₂² − 2Cov).
- Portfolio σ is **below the weighted average** whenever ρ < +1. At ρ = −1 risk can be eliminated.
- **Efficient frontier:** highest return for each level of risk. Benefits level off after about 20–30 shares.

</details>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Four reasons for time value: **risk of collection, opportunity cost, deferred consumption, inflation**.
- FV = PV(1 + i)ⁿ · PV = FV(1 + i)⁻ⁿ · **EAR = (1 + j/m)^m − 1**. Keep i and n in the same period.

- NPV > 0 accept; IRR > r accept. Interpolated IRR **overstates** the true IRR.
- Real return ≈ nominal − tax − inflation.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **In-sample vs out-of-sample:** a strategy fitted to past data may fail on new data (**overfitting**).
