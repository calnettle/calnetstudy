## How a Block Works

Every hour runs the same way:

```
Sprint 1    25 min   the first task named in the block
Break        5 min   stand up and move. Water. No phone
Sprint 2    25 min   the second task, then the OUTPUT in its last 10 minutes,
                     written closed book
Break        5 min   off the chair

Between blocks       a 10–15 minute walk. It's part of the plan, not a reward
```

Block 1 of each day starts with **5 minutes of closed-book recall** of
yesterday's outputs, inside Sprint 1.

**Before you sit down:**

- **Phone in another room,** not face down on the desk. Even having it nearby pulls your attention.
- **Timer on, task chosen.** Each sprint below says exactly what to do, so there's nothing to decide when you start.
- **Pen and blank paper.** Every sprint should have you writing, drawing or saying something out loud. Reading on its own is what makes you restless.

**The rules:**

1. **Do the blocks in the order given.** Block 1 is always the hardest
   new material of the day, so it goes first while you are fresh.
2. **The output is the point of the hour.** If Sprint 2's task runs long,
   cut it and still write the output in the last 10 minutes.
3. **When the timer goes, stop, even mid-page.** Take the break. Note
   anything unfinished and pick it up in the next sweep (18 Oct, 25 Oct,
   1 Nov). Don't let one block take the next one's time.
4. **Struggling to start? Commit to ten minutes.** Most days you'll keep
   going. If 25 minutes really won't happen today, do two 15-minute
   sprints and the output, and count the block as done.
5. **When a whole day is lost, don't push the plan back.** Skip it. At
   the next sweep, do the most important block you missed instead of
   the sweep, choosing EFB335 or AYB250 over the property units.
6. **If you already worked a block's material in the first plan,** go
   straight to Sprint 2's self-test or tutorial. If you get most of it,
   spend the rest on the output and the traps, then stop.

## If the Plan Slips

| What happened | Do this |
|---|---|
| Can't face a full sprint today | Do two 15-minute sprints and the output. It counts |
| Five blocks is too many today | Drop Block 5 first, then Block 4. **Never drop the plain-English EFB335 blocks** (usually Blocks 1–2): they're the reason the plan went to five |
| A plain-English note still doesn't click | Stop at its picture and one-sentence version. Explain those out loud, do its worked example, and move on. Ask Trent to explain that one part a different way |
| Lost one day | Skip it. At the next sweep, do its EFB335 or AYB250 block instead of the sweep |
| Lost two or more days in Weeks 1–2 | Drop the USB244 and USB245 blocks in Weeks 1–3, which are only there to keep those units warm, and use them for the missed EFB335/AYB250 blocks. The property units' late blocks stay |
| A deck block takes two hours | Finish it in the next block and push that block's unit to the next sweep. Topics 5–10 are worth the time, since they carry 36 of the sample's 40 marks |
| You feel solid on a unit early | Don't drop its blocks. Turn them into closed-book practice from that unit's question bank |
| New material lands (AYB250 plan construction, USB244 Week 12, USB245 Week 12) | Add it to that week's sweep, then ask Trent to write it up |
