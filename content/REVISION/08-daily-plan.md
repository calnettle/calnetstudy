## How a Block Works

```
Sprint 1    25 min   the first task named in the block
Break        5 min   stand up and move. Water. No phone
Sprint 2    25 min   the second task, then the OUTPUT in its last 10 minutes,
                     written closed book
Break        5 min   off the chair

Between blocks       a 10–15 minute walk. It's part of the plan, not a reward
```

**Every morning, 8:10 to 8:25: the spaced recall.** Before Block 1,
fifteen minutes, closed book, on what you learned **two days ago and
seven days ago** (each day below lists it). For each item write one
line on the main idea and one formula or worked example, then check
the note. For practice blocks, redo one of the misses you listed.
Anything you couldn't do goes to the top of that unit's next block.
Recall runs from Thu 29 Oct to Sun 8 Nov; in exam week the night-before
revision does the same job.

**Before you sit down:** phone in another room, timer on, pen and blank
paper. Every sprint below says exactly what to do, so there's nothing
to decide when you start.

**The rules:**

1. **Do the blocks in order.** The new, hardest learning is first.
2. **The output is the point of the hour.** If Sprint 2 runs long, cut it and still write the output in the last 10 minutes.
3. **When the timer goes, stop, even mid-page.** Note what's unfinished and do it at the start of that unit's next block.
4. **Struggling to start? Commit to ten minutes.** If 25 minutes won't happen, do two 15-minute sprints and the output. It counts.
5. **Too many blocks today?** Drop Block 6, then Block 5. **Never drop Blocks 1–4** (EFB335 and AYB250).
6. **Lost a whole day?** Don't push the plan back. Do that day's Block 1 and Block 2 in place of the next day's Blocks 5 and 6.

> **EFB335: plain English first, then the full depth, then the jargon.**
> Every EFB335 lesson starts with the plain-English note (10–20 minutes)
> so the idea makes sense, then goes through the full material: the
> revision pack and the tutorial attempted before checking (Topics 1–4),
> or the lecture slides section by section (Topics 5–10). It always ends
> in the jargon.
>
> **Understand it in plain English, answer in the jargon.**
> Each [plain-English note](#/EFB335/20-plain-english-start-here) has
> a picture and a worked example to make the idea click, then a
> **Jargon to Learn** table (the term, what it means, an exam sentence
> using it). The exam marks the proper terms, so every EFB335 block
> ends by writing in the jargon.

> **Where the lecturer's files are.** Sprints marked 📁 use files in
> `QUT › 2nd Year › Sem 2 › <unit>`, which are not on the site. Each
> calendar event has download links for them.
