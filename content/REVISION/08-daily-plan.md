# The Daily Plan — 28 September to 8 November

Every day, two lists.

**HIGH PRIORITY — 2 hours. This happens no matter what.** If the day
collapses, this is what survives. It is the only place new content is
ever introduced.

**ADDITIONAL — 1 hour. Droppable without consequence.** Every additional
task is **revision of material you have already completed** — never new
content, never a topic you have not met. Miss it and the plan still
works; do it and every concept gets two or three more touchpoints than
the minimum.

```
3 hours/day  =  15 min retrieval warm-up
              + 1 h 45 primary block        ← HIGH PRIORITY
              + 1 hour revision of completed material  ← ADDITIONAL
```

> **Why the split works.** The high-priority list is sequenced so that
> nothing later depends on an additional task. Additional tasks only
> ever *re-touch* what high priority already taught. So a fortnight of
> missed additional hours costs you retention, not progress — you will
> never arrive at a block unable to start it.

> **One honest caveat about Phase 1.** Three hours a day is a study
> budget, and between 28 September and 21 October you also have four
> assessment deadlines. **Assessment work sits outside this 3 hours** on
> the days marked ⚑. Two 30% group reports cannot be written inside a
> budget that is simultaneously teaching you two units from zero, and
> pretending otherwise is how the learning blocks get eaten.

**The warm-up, every day, closed book, 15 minutes:** 5 minutes
reproducing yesterday's output from memory, 10 minutes on the row you
feel least sure of from roughly a week ago. It is inside the
high-priority block, not on top of it.

## The Rules For This Page

1. **High priority is a contract with yourself.** Two hours. If a day is
   destroyed, the two hours still happen, even at 9pm, even badly.
2. **Additional is genuinely droppable.** It is never a prerequisite.
   Nothing on any later day assumes you did it.
3. **Additional never introduces new content.** Every task on that side
   of the table is material already taught by a high-priority block on
   an earlier day. If you find yourself reading something new in the
   additional hour, you are in the wrong column.
4. **The warm-up is inside high priority**, not the additional hour. It
   survives when everything else is cut.
5. **⚑ days are assessment days.** Study shrinks to the warm-up and the
   assessment owns the rest. There are five of them and they are all in
   October.
6. **Re-plan on Sundays**, after the sweep. The sweep tells you what the
   week actually stuck, which is the only honest input to next week's
   additional column.
