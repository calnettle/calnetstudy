# The Exam Week — 7 to 13 November

Four exams, five days, one free day in the middle. This is the highest
-leverage week of the semester and the one most easily wasted by
improvising.

```
Sat  7 Nov   USB244 second pass
Sun  8 Nov   USB244 — the day before
Mon  9 Nov   USB244 EXAM      →  then USB245
Tue 10 Nov   USB245 EXAM      →  then AYB250
Wed 11 Nov   AYB250 EXAM      →  then EFB335
Thu 12 Nov   EFB335 — a full free day
Fri 13 Nov   EFB335 EXAM      →  done
```

> **The structural problem of this week: you get one evening between
> exams one, two and three, and then a whole day before the fourth.**
> That asymmetry is the plan. USB244, USB245 and AYB250 must arrive at
> Monday morning **already revised** — the evening before each is a
> top-up, not a revision session. Only EFB335 gets real time inside the
> week.

> **Which is why [note 01](#/REVISION/01-week-by-week-schedule)'s Phase
> 3 sequences AYB250 first.** Its exam is on Wednesday but its last
> proper revision is the Monday and Tuesday of the *previous* week. By
> the time you sit it you will have had nine days of spacing — which is
> what makes recall durable rather than fragile.

## Before the week starts

Four things to have done by Friday 6 November:

1. **Confirm every start time and venue.** A 9am exam and a 2pm exam
   need different nights before. Do this now, not on the Sunday.
2. **Confirm what you can take in.** Open book, formula sheet, or
   nothing changes what the last three days are for. If a printed
   formula sheet is permitted, print the site's sheets and **tab them**
   — an untabbed 5,400-word document is not usable under time pressure.
3. **Calculator: batteries, and `2ndF M-CLR 1 =` tested.** The Sharp
   EL-738 is required for USB245 and useful for AYB250 and EFB335. Make
   sure you can clear cash-flow data and set two decimals without
   thinking. [USB245 note 06](#/USB245/06-topic-6-the-financial-calculator)
   §12.2.
4. **Print or download the four formula sheets** so nothing depends on
   wifi.
5. **Walk the four checklists** ([04](#/REVISION/04-topics-usb244),
   [05](#/REVISION/05-topics-usb245), [06](#/REVISION/06-topics-ayb250),
   [07](#/REVISION/07-topics-efb335)) and mark every row you still
   cannot produce. That list — and nothing else — is what the last four
   days are for.

## The Five Rules For This Week

1. **No post-mortems.** Not after any of the four.
2. **Stop by 8pm every night.** Sleep is the highest-yield revision
   activity available in an exam block, and it compounds across five
   days.
3. **The evening after an exam belongs to the next exam** — as a
   top-up, not a rescue. Anything not already revised by Monday morning
   will not get revised.
4. **Formula sheets and practice questions only from 8 November.** No
   topic notes, no new reading.
5. **Eat and move.** Three exams in three days is a physical event. A
   twenty-minute walk after each paper is not time lost.

## If Something Goes Wrong

| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| A paper goes badly | Nothing. Walk away. It is already scored and the next one is not |
| You wake up unprepared for the day's exam | Formula sheet and trap list only, for 45 minutes. Cramming new content on the morning of an exam reliably makes recall *worse* |
| Illness | **Apply for deferred assessment immediately**, before the exam if possible. QUT requires it promptly — do not sit a paper sick and appeal afterwards |
| You run out of time in a paper | Answer every question partially rather than some completely. Method marks are real, especially in USB245 and EFB335 where the working carries the marks |
| A question uses a convention the notes flagged as disputed | State your assumption in one line, then answer. For the three live disputes — USB245's 6.9% IRR and Exercise 5(d), AYB250's income-protection percentage — showing that you know *why* it is ambiguous is worth more than picking right |
