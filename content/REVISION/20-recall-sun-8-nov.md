<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### EFB335 · Topic 6 (1 of 2): passive and active

*Learned Sun 1 Nov · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/24-plain-english-topic-6-equity-strategies)*

**Questions**

1. What's a 130/30 strategy?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**130% long** in favoured shares, **30% short** in disliked ones. The shorts fund extra longs, so research is used both ways.

</details>

2. What did Brinson, Hood and Beebower (1986) find?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Strategic asset allocation explained 93.6%** of the variation in portfolio returns.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Passive:** track an index; judged on **tracking error** = SD of (Rp − Rb). **Active:** beat the benchmark risk-adjusted, net of costs (alpha).
- Index construction: **full replication** (closest, costliest), **sampling**, **optimisation**.
- **ETF** trades on an exchange (can be shorted); an index fund is bought from the manager.
- Active styles: top-down vs bottom-up, **130/30**, contrarian vs momentum, factor-based.
- **Tax cost ratio** = 1 − (1 + TAR) ÷ (1 + PTR). High turnover → higher TCR (active funds).

### AYB250 · Topic 5b: leveraged investing and margin lending

*Learned Sun 1 Nov · [open the lesson](#/AYB250/09-topic-5-leveraged-investing)*

**Questions**

1. Positive vs negative gearing?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Positive:** investment income exceeds costs, so there's taxable income. **Negative:** costs exceed income, creating a loss that (where allowed) offsets other income.

</details>

2. Three ways to meet a margin call, and the deadline?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Deposit cash**, **add security**, or **sell investments**, usually within **24 hours**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Leverage magnifies both gains and losses.
- **Gearing ratio = debt ÷ your capital. LVR = debt ÷ total value.**
- Positive gearing: income > costs (taxable). Negative: costs > income (loss offsets other income, subject to the 2026 changes for residential).
- Margin loans: max LVR per security plus a **5–10% buffer**; a margin call must be met in **24 hours** (cash, more security, or sell).

### USB245 · Topic 5: NPV, IRR and return measures

*Learned Sun 1 Nov · [open the lesson](#/USB245/05-topic-5-return-measurements)*

**Questions**

1. Required return vs expected return: what do you solve for in each?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Required return: **fix the rate, solve for the price** (maximum price payable). Expected return: **fix the price, solve for the rate** (IRR).

</details>

2. Two weaknesses of static return measures?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

They ignore **time value** and **later years** (also costs and growth).

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Static measures: single-year ratios, ignore time value and later years.
- **NPV = Σ CFₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ − CF₀.** Positive = accept.
- **IRR** = the rate making NPV zero; accept if IRR > required return.
- Rankings can conflict (**scale, timing, horizon**); non-conventional cashflows give multiple IRRs. **Decide by NPV.**
- Required return: fix the rate, solve the price. Expected return: fix the price, solve the rate.
