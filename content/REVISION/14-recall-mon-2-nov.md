### EFB335 · Topic 5 (1 of 2): the three forms and weak-form tests

*Learned Sat 31 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/23-plain-english-topic-5-market-efficiency)*

**Questions**

1. A runs test shows far fewer runs than expected. What does that suggest?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Positive dependence (**trends**), which is **inconsistent with weak-form efficiency**.

</details>

2. Momentum vs reversal: horizons and explanations?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Momentum:** winners keep winning over 1–12 months (underreaction, slow information diffusion). **Reversal:** 3–5 year winners become losers (overreaction).

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Weak** = past market data; **semi-strong** = all public information; **strong** = public and private. Each includes the one before.
- Weak-form tests: **autocorrelation**, **runs tests**, calendar patterns, momentum/reversal, **trading-rule tests** (after costs).
- **Runs test:** fewer runs than expected = trends = against weak form.
- **Momentum** (1–12 months, underreaction) vs **reversal** (3–5 years, overreaction).
- **January effect:** tax-loss selling in December; Keim's bid–ask explanation for small stocks.

### AYB250 · Topic 4a: asset classes

*Learned Sat 31 Oct · [open the lesson](#/AYB250/06-topic-4-asset-classes-and-investments)*

**Questions**

1. Nominal cash rate 5%, inflation 3%. Exact real rate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1.05 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = **1.94%**.

</details>

2. A $100,000 90-day bill costs $98,500. Annualised yield?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1,500 ÷ 98,500 × 365 ÷ 90 = **6.18%**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Classes: cash, fixed interest, property, shares, alternatives (direct or via managed funds).
- **Real rate = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1.** Risk-free proxy = 90-day Treasury Note.
- Discount security yield = (FV − price) ÷ price × 365 ÷ days. Coupon bond price = PV(coupons) + PV(face).
- Rates ↑ → bond prices ↓. **P/E** = price per $ of earnings; **dividend yield** = income per $ of price.
- Managed fund risks: market, security, currency, liquidity, gearing, taxation, beta.

### EFB335 · Topic 5 (2 of 2): semi-strong, strong and what it means

*Learned Sat 31 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/18-sample-final-exam/question-1-market-efficiency-topic-5-4-marks)*

**Questions**

1. A stock rises 6% on announcement day; the market rises 2%. AR? What should the CAR look like after day 0 if markets are efficient?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

AR = **4%**. The CAR should be **flat** after day 0.

</details>

2. Is strong-form efficiency supported? Why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**No.** Corporate insiders consistently earn above-average returns, so private information isn't fully in prices.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Abnormal return** ARᵢₜ = Rᵢₜ − Rₘₜ. **Event study:** average AR around day 0, cumulate into the CAR.
- Semi-strong anomalies: **low P/E, size, neglected firms, high B/M (value), post-earnings announcement drift**.
- **Joint hypothesis:** an anomaly may mean the pricing model is wrong, not the market.
- Strong form **rejected**: insiders earn abnormal returns. Analysts show some skill; fund managers mostly don't.
- Without superior analysts: know the client's risk, diversify globally, rebalance, **minimise costs**.

### AYB250 · Topic 4b: risk, return and portfolio theory

*Learned Sat 31 Oct · [open the lesson](#/AYB250/07-topic-4-risk-return-and-portfolio-theory)*

**Questions**

1. Rf 4%, market risk premium 6%, β 1.5. Required return?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

4 + 1.5 × 6 = **13%**.

</details>

2. Rp 10%, Rf 4%, σ 12%. Sharpe ratio?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

6 ÷ 12 = **0.5**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Risk = **standard deviation**. Only property and shares reliably beat inflation long run.
- Portfolio return is a weighted average; **risk is not** (correlation). Total risk = systematic + non-systematic; 10–30 holdings remove most non-systematic.
- **CAPM:** Rᵢ = Rf + βᵢ(Rm − Rf). **Sharpe** = (Rp − Rf) ÷ σp.
- Risk profile = risk **required** + **capacity** + **tolerance**.
- Biases: **loss aversion, herding, overconfidence, biased judgements**.

### USB245 · Topic 4: deriving the discount rate

*Learned Sat 31 Oct · [open the lesson](#/USB245/04-topic-4-deriving-the-discount-rate)*

**Questions**

1. The four methods of deriving a discount rate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Risk premium model**, **market approach**, **benchmarking against alternative asset classes**, **WACC**.

</details>

2. Cap rate 6%, expected growth 2.5%. Implied discount rate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

6 + 2.5 = **8.5%** (Gordon growth).

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Discount rate = required return (opportunity cost, inflation, risk).
- Four methods: **risk premium (rf + rp)**, **market approach**, **benchmarking against other asset classes**, **WACC** (only valid with interest-only debt).
- Cap rate = discount rate **only when income is stabilised**. Otherwise **discount rate = cap rate + growth** (Gordon).
- Requirement side (rf + premium) vs delivery side (cap rate + growth) should land close.

### USB244 · Topic 4: commercial and green leases

*Learned Sat 31 Oct · [open the lesson](#/USB244/04-topic-4-commercial-and-green-leases)*

**Questions**

1. Face rent $600/m², 20% incentive. Effective rent?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

600 × 0.8 = **$480/m²**.

</details>

2. Lease vs licence?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A lease gives **exclusive possession**; a licence doesn't.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- A lease grants **exclusive possession**; a licence doesn't.
- On sale, leases survive: **notice of attornment**; bank guarantees must be reissued.
- Reviews: fixed, CPI, market; modifiers: **ratchet, floor, cap, collar**.
- **Effective rent** = face rent less incentives (16% incentive: $500 → $420).
- Percentage rent natural breakpoint = base rent ÷ rate. Green leases contractualise sustainability.
