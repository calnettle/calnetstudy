### EFB335 · Topic 9 (1 of 2): private equity and venture capital

*Learned Wed 4 Nov · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/27-plain-english-topic-9-alternatives)*

**Questions**

1. Explain the J-curve.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

PE funds show **negative returns in the early years** (fees and costs before any exits), then **rising returns** as companies are sold. Plotted over time, it's a J.

</details>

2. Are VC's high average returns evidence of superior performance?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**No.** Returns are non-normal and very risky (β 1.4–1.7); **risk-adjusted, the mean return is negative** (−7.1% against NASDAQ).

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Alternatives: large minimums, illiquid, barriers to entry, professional investors; usually **limited partnerships**.
- **Private equity:** unlisted ownership, locked up for years. **VC** is the start-up end: seed → Series A → B/C → mezzanine → pre-IPO; exit by IPO.
- **J-curve:** negative early returns (fees, no exits), then strong later returns.
- VC raw returns look high (30–45% in early studies) but betas are 1.4–1.7; **risk-adjusted, the average is negative**.

### EFB335 · Topic 3 in depth: CML, beta and the CAPM

*Learned Fri 30 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/22-plain-english-topics-3-4-capm-and-factors)*

**Questions**

1. Rf 4%, E(RM) 10%, σM 18%. A CML portfolio has σ 27%. Expected return, and is it lending or borrowing?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

4 + 27 × 6 ÷ 18 = **13%**. Weight in M = 27 ÷ 18 = 1.5, so it's a **borrowing** portfolio (−50% in Rf).

</details>

2. CML vs SML in one line each?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**CML:** efficient portfolios, return vs total risk σ. **SML:** any asset, required return vs beta.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **CML (on the sheet):** E(Rp) = RFR + σp × [E(RM) − RFR] ÷ σM. Efficient portfolios, **total risk**.
- **CAPM / SML (not on the sheet):** E(Rᵢ) = Rf + βᵢ[E(RM) − Rf]. Any asset, **systematic risk**.
- **β = Cov(i, M) ÷ σM².** Market β = 1.
- Above the SML = **undervalued** (positive alpha, buy). Below = overvalued.
- **Separation theorem:** everyone holds M; only the lending/borrowing mix differs. Negative weight in Rf = borrowing.

### EFB335 · Topic 4 in depth: APT and factor models

*Learned Fri 30 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/22-plain-english-topics-3-4-capm-and-factors)*

**Questions**

1. The three Fama–French factors and what each captures?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Market** (excess market return), **SMB** (small firms earn more), **HML** (high book-to-market / value firms earn more).

</details>

2. What's an arbitrage opportunity?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A portfolio that **costs nothing, carries no risk and earns a positive return**. In equilibrium none should exist.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **APT:** E(R) = Rf + b₁λ₁ + b₂λ₂ + … (b = factor sensitivity, λ = factor risk premium).
- APT needs fewer assumptions than the CAPM: **no market portfolio, no normal returns, no quadratic utility**. It doesn't name the factors.
- **Arbitrage:** a zero-cost, zero-risk portfolio with a positive return. APT says these are traded away.
- Factors: **macro** (GDP, inflation, rates), **micro / Fama–French** (market, SMB, HML), **statistical** (PCA).

### USB245 · Topic 3: the DCF and its cashflows

*Learned Fri 30 Oct · [open the lesson](#/USB245/03-topic-3-dcf-and-cashflow-components)*

**Questions**

1. The income waterfall from gross potential to NOI?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Gross potential income − vacancy = **gross effective income** − statutory charges and operating expenses = **NOI**.

</details>

2. Sum of the discounted cashflows: is that the NPV?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**No**, it's the **gross present value**. Subtract the outlay to get NPV.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- DCF = time-value tool for **irregular** cashflows; discount period by period.
- Step 1: n, period length, advance or arrears. Period 0 = purchase; **n + 1** prices the exit.
- Waterfall: gross potential − vacancy = gross effective − statutory charges and opex = **NOI**.
- **Acquisition costs are added** to the price. Income and expense growth are separate rates.
- Sale price = year n + 1 NOI ÷ terminal yield, less selling costs. Sum of PVs is **gross** PV; subtract the outlay for NPV.
