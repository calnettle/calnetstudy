### EFB335 · Topic 1: return, risk and margin

*Learned Wed 28 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/21-plain-english-topics-1-2-return-and-risk)*

**Questions**

1. Buy at $40, receive a $2 dividend, sell at $46. HPR and HPY?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

HPR = (46 + 2) ÷ 40 = **1.20**. HPY = **20%**.

</details>

2. Returns of +20% then −10%. Arithmetic and geometric means?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Arithmetic = (20 − 10) ÷ 2 = **5%**. Geometric = √(1.20 × 0.90) − 1 = √1.08 − 1 = **3.92%**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **HPR** = ending value ÷ beginning value (e.g. 1.14). **HPY** = HPR − 1 = (P₁ − P₀ + D) ÷ P₀.
- **Geometric mean ≤ arithmetic mean.** Use the geometric mean for how money actually grew; the arithmetic mean for a single future period.
- **E(R) = Σ pᵢRᵢ**; **σ = √Σ pᵢ(Rᵢ − E(R))²**. σ measures total risk.
- **Margin-call price (long)** = P₀ × (1 − initial margin) ÷ (1 − maintenance margin).

### EFB335 · Topic 1 in depth: Revision Pack 1

*Learned Wed 28 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/13-revision-pack-topic-1-and-tutorial-1/part-a-concepts-taught-from-scratch)*

**Questions**

1. $10,000 grows to $14,000 over 3 years. Annual HPY?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1.4^(1/3) − 1 = **11.87%**.

</details>

2. Short sell at $80, initial margin 50%, maintenance margin 30%. Margin-call price?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

80 × 1.5 ÷ 1.3 = **$92.31**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Annualised HPY** = HPR^(1/n) − 1.
- **Coefficient of variation** = σ ÷ E(R): risk per unit of return, **lower is better**.
- **Order types:** market (now, at the best price), limit (only at your price or better), stop-loss (becomes a market sell when the price falls to the stop).
- **Short sale:** sell borrowed shares, profit if the price falls. Margin-call price (short) = P₀ × (1 + initial margin) ÷ (1 + maintenance margin).
- **Trap:** historical variance from a sample divides by n − 1; check which the question wants.

### AYB250 · Topic 2a: personal financial statements and ratios

*Learned Wed 28 Oct · [open the lesson](#/AYB250/02-topic-2-personal-financial-statements-and-ratios)*

**Questions**

1. Assets $800,000, liabilities $200,000. Net worth and net worth ratio?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Net worth **$600,000**; ratio = 600 ÷ 800 = **75%**.

</details>

2. Which of the four ratios is better when lower?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The **debt service ratio**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Cash flow budget** = a flow over a period. **Balance sheet** = a stock at a point. Assets = liabilities + net worth.
- **Net worth ratio** = net worth ÷ total assets (higher better).
- **Liquidity ratio** = liquid assets ÷ current debts, where current debt = **12 months of repayments** (higher better).
- **Savings ratio** = savings (add back other saving) ÷ net income (higher better). **Debt service ratio** = repayments ÷ net income (**lower** better).
- Every ratio needs a written interpretation.

</details>

2. The six investment objectives?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Return preference, risk tolerance, investment horizon, liquidity, amount, tax**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Return = **income + capital**; express as a % of **purchase price**; annualise with the geometric mean.

- **NOI = gross income − operating expenses**, before interest, tax and depreciation.
- **CV = NI ÷ Y** (always net income).
- Time value: PV of an annuity, PV of a lump sum, sinking-fund PMT; NPV generalises them.
