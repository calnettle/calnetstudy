### EFB335 · Topic 8 (1 of 2): options and futures in a portfolio

*Learned Tue 3 Nov · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/26-plain-english-topic-8-derivatives)*

**Questions**

1. Put strike $25, premium $1.50, share ends at $21. Payoff and profit to the holder?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Payoff = 25 − 21 = **$4.00**. Profit = 4.00 − 1.50 = **$2.50**.

</details>

2. How is a collar built, and what does it cost the investor?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Buy an **out-of-the-money put**, sell an **out-of-the-money call**. Little or no upfront cost; the price is **giving up gains above the call strike**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Call payoff = max(S_T − X, 0); put payoff = max(X − S_T, 0)** (on the sheet). Profit deducts the premium.
- Call value ↑ with S, T, σ, r; ↓ with X, dividends. Put value ↑ with X, T, σ, dividends; ↓ with S, r.
- **Hedge:** own shares + sell index futures. **Protective put:** shares + long put. **Covered call:** shares + short call.
- **Collar:** long OTM put + short OTM call, often zero cost (a range forward).
- **Cash equitisation:** long index futures for uninvested cash.

### EFB335 · Topic 2 in depth (1 of 2): portfolio risk

*Learned Thu 29 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/21-plain-english-topics-1-2-return-and-risk)*

**Questions**

1. Why isn't portfolio σ a weighted average of the assets' σs?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Because of the **covariance term**. Unless correlation is +1, the assets' movements partly offset, so portfolio risk is lower.

</details>

2. ρ = −1, σ₁ = 20%, σ₂ = 10%. What weights give zero risk?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

w₁ = σ₂ ÷ (σ₁ + σ₂) = 10 ÷ 30 = **33.3%** in asset 1, 66.7% in asset 2.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Cov(1,2) = ρ σ₁ σ₂**; **ρ = Cov ÷ (σ₁σ₂)**, from −1 to +1.
- **σp² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2w₁w₂Cov₁₂.** The sheet only gives the double-sum form Σ Σ wᵢwⱼCov(i,j).
- **Minimum-variance weight** w₁ = (σ₂² − Cov) ÷ (σ₁² + σ₂² − 2Cov).
- Portfolio σ is **below the weighted average** whenever ρ < +1. At ρ = −1 risk can be eliminated.
- **Efficient frontier:** highest return for each level of risk. Benefits level off after about 20–30 shares.

### EFB335 · Topic 2 in depth (2 of 2): Tutorial 2 and utility

*Learned Thu 29 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/14-revision-pack-topic-2-and-tutorial-2/part-b-tutorial-2-fully-worked)*

**Questions**

1. A = 6. X: E 10%, σ 15%. Y: E 14%, σ 25%. Which does the investor choose?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

U(X) = 0.10 − 3(0.0225) = **0.0325**. U(Y) = 0.14 − 3(0.0625) = **−0.0475**. Choose **X**.

</details>

2. Why test a strategy out-of-sample?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In-sample results can be **overfitted** to past noise. Out-of-sample testing shows whether the strategy works on data it wasn't built on.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **U = E(r) − ½Aσ².** A = risk aversion. Choose the portfolio with the highest U.
- **Indifference curves:** risk/return combinations with equal utility. Steeper for more risk-averse investors.
- The **risk tolerance questionnaire** turns attitude into A (roughly 7 conservative to 1 aggressive).
- **In-sample vs out-of-sample:** a strategy fitted to past data may fail on new data (**overfitting**).
