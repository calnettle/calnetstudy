## Topic 3 — The DCF and Cashflow Components

[Note 03](#/USB245/03-topic-3-dcf-and-cashflow-components) — **the
examinable core**

**Memorise the seven steps verbatim:**

```
1. Set up cashflows over period/time
2. Determine the Net Income for each period
3. Calculate the discount factor for each period
4. Calculate the PV of each period
5. Add up all the PVs
6. Adjust for any acquisition costs
7. Answer = Maximum price payable OR Expected Return
```

| # | Sub-topic | Own it |
|---|---|---|
| 6.3 | Step 1 — the time frame | Holding period, periodicity, and the consequences of monthly vs annual |
| 6.4 | **Step 2 — net income** | Every line of the waterfall. This is where the marks are lost |
| 6.5 | **Step 2b — sale proceeds and the `n+1` year** | The terminal value capitalises the year *after* the holding period ends |
| 6.6 | Steps 3–5 — discounting | Discount factor, PV per period, and the sum |
| 6.7 | **Step 6 — acquisition costs** | The algebra, including that costs come out of **equity, not the loan** |
| 6.8 | **Step 7 — the two answers** | Leave price unknown → maximum price payable. Fix price, solve the rate → expected return (IRR). Be able to do both on one property |

## Topic 9 — Discounting After-Tax Cashflows

[Note 16](#/USB245/16-topic-9-discounting-after-tax-cashflows) — the best
exam framing in the unit

**The four cashflow definitions. Draw this grid from memory:**

| | Property only | With borrowings |
|---|---|---|
| **Before tax** | 1. Net rent + resale proceeds | 2. Net rent − loan payments; proceeds − outstanding loan |
| **After tax** | 3. After depreciation and income tax; proceeds after CGT | 4. Both — and remember **mortgage duty and establishment fees** in period 0 |

Then: one property, four IRRs, and the interpretation checklist (§19.3),
the criticisms of after-tax models (§19.4), and how to present the
analysis (§19.5).

## Traps — the recurring seven

1. **`NPV()` excludes period 0; the calculator includes it.**
2. **Period `n+1`** — the terminal value capitalises the year *after* the
   holding period ends.
3. **Periodic vs annual rates**, and the Sharp/Excel disagreement.
4. **Only interest is deductible; principal never is.**
5. **Depreciation reduces tax, never cashflow. Principal reduces
   cashflow, never tax.** Each belongs to exactly one bottom line.
6. **Apply carried-forward losses before taxing the final year.**
7. **Acquisition costs come out of equity, not the loan.**
