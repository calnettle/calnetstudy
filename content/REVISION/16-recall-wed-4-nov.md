### EFB335 · Topic 7 (1 of 2): price, duration and convexity

*Learned Mon 2 Nov · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/25-plain-english-topic-7-bonds)*

**Questions**

1. ModD 4, convexity 30, yields rise 0.5%. Price change?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

−4 × 0.005 + ½ × 30 × 0.000025 = −2.00% + 0.04% = **−1.96%**.

</details>

2. Duration of a 5-year zero-coupon bond? Which is more rate-sensitive: a high or low coupon bond?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**5 years.** The **low-coupon** bond.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Yield ↑ → price ↓. Yield below coupon = premium; above = discount.
- More sensitive: **longer maturity, lower coupon, lower yield**. Price rises from a fall exceed falls from an equal rise.
- **Macaulay D** = PV-weighted average time to cash flows (years). Zero-coupon D = maturity. **ModD = D ÷ (1 + i).**
- **%ΔP ≈ −ModD × Δy + ½ × Convexity × (Δy)²** (not on the sheet).
- Portfolio duration = weighted average of bond durations.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Active management stops and the portfolio is **immunised** to lock in the minimum required return.

### EFB335 · Topic 1: return, risk and margin

*Learned Wed 28 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/21-plain-english-topics-1-2-return-and-risk)*

**Questions**

1. You buy at $50 with 60% initial margin; maintenance margin is 30%. Margin-call price?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

50 × 0.40 ÷ 0.70 = **$28.57**.

</details>

2. Probability 0.3 of 20%, 0.7 of 5%. E(R) and σ?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

E(R) = 6 + 3.5 = **9.5%**. Variance = 0.3(10.5²) + 0.7(4.5²) = 33.075 + 14.175 = 47.25 → σ = **6.87%**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **HPR** = ending value ÷ beginning value (e.g. 1.14). **HPY** = HPR − 1 = (P₁ − P₀ + D) ÷ P₀.
- **Geometric mean ≤ arithmetic mean.** Use the geometric mean for how money actually grew; the arithmetic mean for a single future period.
- **E(R) = Σ pᵢRᵢ**; **σ = √Σ pᵢ(Rᵢ − E(R))²**. σ measures total risk.
- **Margin-call price (long)** = P₀ × (1 − initial margin) ÷ (1 − maintenance margin).

### EFB335 · Topic 1 in depth: Revision Pack 1

</details>

2. Limit order vs stop-loss order?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A **limit** order trades only at your stated price or better. A **stop-loss** becomes a market sell order once the price falls to the stop, capping the loss.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Short sale:** sell borrowed shares, profit if the price falls. Margin-call price (short) = P₀ × (1 + initial margin) ÷ (1 + maintenance margin).
- **Trap:** historical variance from a sample divides by n − 1; check which the question wants.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Twelve months of debt repayments**, not the whole loan balance.

</details>

2. Savings ratio: what's the trap in the numerator and the denominator?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Numerator: **add back other forms of saving** (e.g. extra super). Denominator: **net (after-tax) income**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Cash flow budget** = a flow over a period. **Balance sheet** = a stock at a point. Assets = liabilities + net worth.

- **Savings ratio** = savings (add back other saving) ÷ net income (higher better). **Debt service ratio** = repayments ÷ net income (**lower** better).
- Every ratio needs a written interpretation.
