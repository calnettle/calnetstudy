### EFB335 · Topic 10 (1 of 2): the five measures

*Learned Thu 5 Nov · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/28-plain-english-topic-10-performance)*

**Questions**

1. Rp 14%, Rf 4%, σ 20%, β 1.25, Rm 11%. Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sharpe = 10 ÷ 20 = **0.50**. Treynor = 10 ÷ 1.25 = **8.0**. Jensen = 10 − 1.25 × 7 = **+1.25%**.

</details>

2. When can Sharpe and Treynor rank funds differently?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

When a fund is **poorly diversified**: its unsystematic risk lowers Sharpe but doesn't affect Treynor.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Sharpe** = (Rp − Rf) ÷ σp (total risk). **Treynor** = (Rp − Rf) ÷ βp (systematic).
- **Jensen's α** = (Rp − Rf) − βp(Rm − Rf). **IR** = (Rp − Rb) ÷ tracking error. **Sortino** = (Rp − τ) ÷ downside risk (not on the sheet).
- Sharpe for whole wealth; Treynor for one part of a diversified whole; IR for active managers.
- They **disagree when a fund is poorly diversified**: Sharpe punishes the unsystematic risk, Treynor ignores it.

### EFB335 · Topic 10 (2 of 2): attribution and evidence

*Learned Thu 5 Nov · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/18-sample-final-exam/question-9-risk-adjusted-performance-topic-10-4-marks)*

**Questions**

1. Shares: Wp 0.5, Wb 0.6, Rp 9%, Rb 8%. Bonds: Wp 0.5, Wb 0.4, Rp 4%, Rb 5%. Allocation and selection?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Allocation = (−0.1)(8) + (0.1)(5) = **−0.3**. Selection = 0.5(1) + 0.5(−1) = **0**. Check: Rp 6.5% − Rb 6.8% = −0.3 ✓.

</details>

2. Name four required characteristics of a benchmark.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Any four: **unambiguous, investable, measurable, appropriate, reflective of current investment opinion, specified in advance**.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Allocation** = Σ (W_pi − W_bi) × R_bi. **Selection** = Σ W_pi × (R_pi − R_bi). They sum to the excess return (sheet form).
- A good manager: superior **timing or selection**, and **full diversification**.
- A benchmark must be **unambiguous, investable, measurable, appropriate, reflect current opinion, specified in advance**.
- Evidence: funds underperform on average (Jensen 1968: α −1.1%); poor performers persist more than good ones.

### USB245 · Weeks 10–11: risk and portfolio analysis

**Questions**

1. The three general techniques for managing property investment risk, with one example each?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Avoid** (e.g. don't buy in flood areas), **transfer** (insurance, long leases with escalation clauses), **reduce** (lower LVR, better price, diversification).

</details>

2. Sensitivity vs scenario analysis?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Sensitivity** changes one variable at a time to see its effect. **Scenario** changes several together to model a whole outcome (e.g. a downturn).

</details>

**Revision notes**

- Risk = chance of not receiving the expected or required return. Categories: **market, financial, property**; systemic vs non-systemic.
- Manage risk: **avoid/eliminate**, **transfer/shift** (insurance, long leases with escalations, fixed-price contracts), **reduce** (loan terms, purchase price, diversification).
- Qualitative: **PEST**, **SWOT**. Quantitative levels: basic feasibility (DCR, LVR) → DCF most likely → **sensitivity** → **scenario**.
- Diversification means across sectors and locations, not just many assets (five Brisbane offices isn't diversified).

### EFB335 · Topic 5 (1 of 2): the three forms and weak-form tests

*Learned Sat 31 Oct · [open the lesson](#/EFB335/23-plain-english-topic-5-market-efficiency)*

**Questions**

1. Which form of the EMH says technical analysis can't earn abnormal returns?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Weak form**: prices already reflect all past market data.

</details>

2. Give two explanations of the January effect.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Tax-loss selling** in December depresses prices, which bounce in January; **microstructure** (last December trades at the bid, Keim 1989), strongest for small stocks.

</details>

**Revision notes**

- **Weak** = past market data; **semi-strong** = all public information; **strong** = public and private. Each includes the one before.
- Weak-form tests: **autocorrelation**, **runs tests**, calendar patterns, momentum/reversal, **trading-rule tests** (after costs).
- **Runs test:** fewer runs than expected = trends = against weak form.
- **Momentum** (1–12 months, underreaction) vs **reversal** (3–5 years, overreaction).
- **January effect:** tax-loss selling in December; Keim's bid–ask explanation for small stocks.

### EFB335 · Topic 5 (2 of 2): semi-strong, strong and what it means

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Determine the client's risk preference, build the matching portfolio, **diversify globally**, **rebalance** to keep the risk level, **minimise transaction costs**.

</details>
