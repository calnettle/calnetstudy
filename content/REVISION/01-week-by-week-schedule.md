# Week-by-Week Schedule

28 September to 8 November. Six weeks, three phases. The exam week
itself is [note 03](#/REVISION/03-the-exam-week).

Blocks are **2 hours** unless marked otherwise. A weekday carries 1–2
blocks around everything else; a weekend day carries 3. That is the
budget the plan is built on — if yours is different, scale the phase
boundaries, not the order.

```
PHASE 1   28 Sep – 21 Oct   Assessment first, maintenance revision only
PHASE 2   22 Oct –  1 Nov   Full revision, first pass, AYB250-weighted
PHASE 3    2 Nov –  8 Nov   Second pass and practice questions, exam-ordered
EXAMS      9 Nov – 13 Nov   See note 03
```

---

## Phase 1 — Assessment First (28 Sep – 21 Oct)

Three deadlines. Revision in this phase is **maintenance**: keep the
material warm, capture new decks into the notes, and let the two
property assessments do double duty as revision.

> **The single most useful habit in this phase: write the assessments
> with the relevant formula sheet open.** USB245 A2 and USB244 Part B
> between them exercise most of both units' examinable calculation
> content. If you look each formula up while you use it, you arrive at
> 22 October with two units already half-revised.

## Phase 2 — First Pass (22 Oct – 1 Nov)

Eleven days. One complete pass through all four units, weighted by
priority. Goal: **every note read once, every worked example worked
once, every formula sheet annotated.**

Order is deliberately *not* exam order — AYB250 first because it is
60% and the largest, USB244 last-but-one because it is on the Monday
and wants to be recent.

| **Fri 23** | Notes 04–05 (income tax, investment tax) | Notes 06–07 (asset classes, risk/return) | — |

## Phase 3 — Second Pass and Practice (2 – 8 Nov)

Seven days. **No new reading.** Closed-book practice questions, the trap
lists, and the worked tutorials rebuilt from scratch. Sequenced so each
unit peaks close to its exam.

| Day | Block 1 | Block 2 | Block 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon 2 Nov** | AYB250 note 23 practice questions — closed book | AYB250 traps 1–18 | — |
| **Tue 3** | AYB250 traps 19–36 | AYB250 tutorials 5–8 re-worked | — |
| **Wed 4** | EFB335 note 10 practice questions — closed book | EFB335 tutorials 1–4 re-worked | — |
| **Thu 5** | EFB335 formula sheet + any Topic 5+ material | USB245 notes 19–21 practice questions | — |
| **Fri 6** | USB245 tutorials 13–15 re-worked (finance, depreciation, after-tax) | USB245 formula sheet quick-reference blocks | — |
| **Sat 7** | USB244 note 11 practice questions — closed book | USB244 tutorials 07–10 re-worked | USB244 traps + formula sheet |
| **Sun 8** | **USB244 full formula sheet, closed book recall** | USB244 quick-reference blocks | Light: exam logistics, sleep early |

> **Phase 3's rule is "produce, don't consume".** Every block should
> generate written output — a solved question, a recalled formula list,
> a trap explained in your own words. If you find yourself re-reading a
> topic note in November, you have reverted to Phase 2 behaviour and it
> is costing you.

> **Note the deliberate asymmetry: AYB250 gets Phase 3's first two days
> even though its exam is third.** It is the 60% paper and the biggest
> body of rules, so it needs the most spacing between the second pass
> and the exam — you want recall to be *durable*, not fresh. USB244
> gets the last two days because its exam is on Monday and it is the
> unit where freshness helps most.

## The Weekly Re-Plan

Twenty minutes, Sunday evening. Four questions:

1. **What new material landed this week?** Get it into the notes. This
   matters most for EFB335 (Topics 5+) and USB245/AYB250 (Weeks 10+).
2. **What slipped?** Move it forward; do not silently drop it.
3. **What did the closed-book output show?** If you could not produce
   something, it goes back into next week at a *specific* time.
4. **Do the phase boundaries still hold?** The two that matter are 21
   October (assessments done) and 1 November (first pass done). If
   either is going to slip, cut scope somewhere — and cut *reading*,
   never practice questions.

## Contingency — If You Lose a Week

It happens. In priority order, what to cut:

| Cut first | Why it is safe |
|---|---|
| Second reading of any topic note | Reading has the lowest retention per minute |
| USB245's Phase 2 allocation | A2 has already covered most of it |
| USB244's Phase 2 day | It gets a full Phase 3 pass on 7–8 Nov |
| EFB335 topic notes (keep the revision packs) | The packs already contain them |

| Never cut | Why |
|---|---|
| **AYB250's Phase 2 block** | 60% of the unit, no assessment left to prop it up |
| **Any closed-book practice question set** | The only activity that reliably predicts exam performance |
| **The day before each exam** | See note 03 |
| **The formula sheets** | They are the map; without them the other work is unordered |
