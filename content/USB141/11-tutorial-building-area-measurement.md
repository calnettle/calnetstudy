# Tutorial — Building Area Measurement (Worked Solutions)

The four Assessment 1 lease plans, worked end to end. This is also the model
for the exam's measurement calculation, which the revision deck lists as one
of the five predictable formats.

## The rules the assessment sets

From the Week 2–5 tutorial decks. These are marked, so follow them exactly:

```
All measurements on the plans are in MILLIMETRES (mm)
Use METRES (m) in the calculations
Use TWO DECIMALS
Present ALL workings
Round the final result to the nearest whole square metre

e.g. the final result is 123.45 m2, rounded 123 m2
```

Marks per plan:

| Task | Marks |
|---|---|
| Write the method of measurement to be used for each tenancy | 1 |
| Shade/colour boundaries and hatch the leasable area neatly in coloured pencil, readable after scanning | 1 |
| Calculate the leasable area for each tenancy, to the nearest square metre | 3 |

Shading must run **from boundary to boundary** — the tutorial shows a red
example (acceptable, hatching fills the whole tenancy up to the measured line)
and a blue one (not acceptable, hatching stops short).

> **The one-mark "state the method" is free and people lose it.** Write the
> full name and the application, not the acronym: *"Gross Lettable Area (GLA)
> — applies to warehouses and industrial buildings"*. Two seconds, one mark,
> and it also forces you to commit to the right measurement line before you
> start.

## The method-selection decision tree

```
What is the building?

  Warehouse / industrial / free-standing supermarket / showroom
        -> GLA    measure to the OUTSIDE face of the dominant portion

  Shopping centre / commercial building / strip, free-standing,
  semi-detached or terrace shop
        -> GLAR   measure from the INSIDE face; to the MALL LINE

  Office building / office and business park
        -> NLA    measure from the INSIDE face; to the CENTRELINE of
                  inter-tenancy partitions

  Mixed use (e.g. warehouse with attached office)
        -> APPORTIONMENT (PCA recommended):
           office on NLA + warehouse on GLA, then add
```

And for every method, before you measure any external wall:

```
Is the glass area greater than the solid wall area on THIS wall?
   YES -> the window is the dominant portion -> measure to the glass
   NO  -> the wall is dominant -> measure to the finished wall surface
```

## Plan 1 of 4 — Industrial (GLA)

**The plan.** A single-tenancy warehouse. Overall dimensions given: **26,000
wide** and **17,000 deep** (the vertical dimension string reads 3,000 + 4,000
+ 3,000 + 4,000 + 3,000, and 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 17). The external wall is
**300 thick**. Inside: an **enclosed loading dock** 4,000 deep with two roller
doors 5,000 wide, and a **toilet** 2,800 × 2,000 in the far corner formed by
200-thick partitions.

**Step 1 — method.**

> Gross Lettable Area (GLA). GLA applies to warehouses and industrial
> buildings. Single tenancy, so measure to the **external face of the dominant
> portion** of the external walls all round. Internal structural columns are
> included; protruding external columns would be excluded.

**Step 2 — confirm the 26,000 and 17,000 are outside-to-outside.** They are —
both dimension strings terminate on the outer face of the wall, and the 300 is
dimensioned separately from the outer face to the inner face. That is exactly
what GLA wants, so no adjustment is needed.

**Step 3 — calculate.**

```
Depth   3.00 + 4.00 + 3.00 + 4.00 + 3.00   =  17.00 m
Width                                       =  26.00 m

GLA = 26.00 x 17.00                         = 442.00 m2

                                    GLA = 442 m2
```

**Step 4 — the enclosed loading dock and the toilet.** The loading dock is
**enclosed and inside the envelope**, so it is part of the tenancy and stays
in. The toilet is the one to think about.

> **The toilet: 442 m² or 436 m²?** The PCA exclusion list in the lecture is
> headed *"To exclude (in a multiple tenanted building)"* — and lift lobbies,
> stairs and **toilets** sit under that heading. This is a **single-tenancy**
> building, so there are no common areas: the whole envelope is the one
> tenancy and **the toilet is not deducted**. The single-tenancy slide
> confirms it, listing only "measure to the external face of the dominant
> portion, include internal structural columns, exclude protruding external
> columns" — no exclusions at all.
>
> The alternative reading deducts the toilet:
>
> ```
> Toilet   2.80 x 2.00              =   5.60 m2
> GLA      442.00 - 5.60            = 436.40 m2  ->  436 m2
> ```
>
> **Answer 442 m², and add one line stating that toilets are excluded only in
> a multiple-tenanted building.** That earns the mark and shows you read the
> qualifier. Worth confirming with your tutor which reading they mark.

**Cross-check.** 26 × 17 = 442. If you instead measured to the inside faces
(the NLA/GLAR line) you would get (26.0 − 0.6) × (17.0 − 0.6) = 25.4 × 16.4 =
**416.56 m²** — 5.8% smaller. That gap is the whole reason the method matters.

## Plan 2 of 4 — Retail (GLAR)

**The plan.** Three tenancies side by side in a shopping centre. Overall depth
dimension **17,000**, external wall **500** thick at the top, **inter-tenancy
walls 500** thick, each tenancy **8,000** clear internal width, a **mall line**
along the bottom. Lease Retail (1) has a shopfront set back from the mall
line; Lease Retail (2) is set back further; Lease Retail (3) has a bay that
**projects past the mall line**, 6,000 clear × 1,750 + 200 deep, with 500-thick
return walls.

**Step 1 — method.**

> Gross Lettable Area – Retail (GLAR). GLAR applies to shopping centres.
> Measure from the **internal surface** of the dominant portion of external
> walls, **to the centreline** of inter-tenancy walls, **to the mall line
> regardless of a setback**, and **to the external surface only if the front
> extends beyond** the mall or shop line.

**Step 2 — the common depth.** All three tenancies run from the internal face
of the top external wall to the mall line:

```
Depth = 17.00 - 0.50 (external wall)      = 16.50 m
```

This is the same for all three **regardless of where their shopfronts
actually sit**. That is the setback rule doing its work.

**Step 3 — the widths.**

```
Tenancy 1   external wall on one side, inter-tenancy wall on the other
            8.00 (clear) + 0.25 (half the 500 inter-tenancy wall)   = 8.25 m

Tenancy 2   inter-tenancy walls both sides
            0.25 + 8.00 + 0.25                                      = 8.50 m

Tenancy 3   inter-tenancy walls both sides
            0.25 + 8.00 + 0.25                                      = 8.50 m
```

**Step 4 — the areas.**

```
Tenancy 1   16.50 x 8.25  = 136.125 m2   ->  136 m2
Tenancy 2   16.50 x 8.50  = 140.250 m2   ->  140 m2
Tenancy 3   16.50 x 8.50  = 140.250 m2   (main body, before the projection)
```

**Step 5 — Tenancy 3's projecting bay.** The front extends **beyond** the mall
line, so this part is measured **to the external surface**:

```
Projection width   6.00 (clear) + 0.50 + 0.50 (return walls)  = 7.00 m
Projection depth   1.75 + 0.20                                 = 1.95 m

Projection area    7.00 x 1.95                                 =  13.65 m2
```

```
Tenancy 3   140.25 + 13.65 = 153.90 m2   ->  154 m2
```

**Answers.**

| Tenancy | Area | Rounded |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lease Retail (1) | 136.125 m² | **136 m²** |
| Lease Retail (2) | 140.250 m² | **140 m²** |
| Lease Retail (3) | 153.900 m² | **154 m²** |
| Total | 430.275 m² | 430 m² |

> **Tenancy 1 is narrower than Tenancy 2 by exactly 250 mm, and that is not a
> mistake.** Tenancy 1 has an **external wall** on one side (measured to the
> internal face — it gets none of that wall's thickness) and an
> **inter-tenancy wall** on the other (measured to the centreline — it gets
> half). Tenancies 2 and 3 have inter-tenancy walls on both sides, so each
> gets half of both. 8.25 versus 8.50. The end tenancy of any retail row is
> always the smallest for this reason.

> **Do not use the "16000" and "15000" dimensions printed inside Tenancies 1
> and 2.** They are the tenancies' *actual internal depths* to their recessed
> shopfronts — decoys. GLAR measures to the mall line, so both are **16,500**.
> Using 16,000 for Tenancy 1 gives 16.00 × 8.25 = 132 m², four square metres
> short, and it is the trap the plan is built around.

> **Check for double counting on Tenancy 3.** The main body is measured to
> the mall line and the projection is measured *beyond* the mall line, so the
> two areas do not overlap. The projection is 7.00 m wide while the tenancy is
> 8.50 m wide — the projection sits inside that width, below the line. Add
> them; do not intersect them.

## Plans 3 and 4 of 4 — Office (NLA)

**Step 1 — method.**

> Net Lettable Area (NLA). NLA applies to office buildings and office and
> business parks. Measure **from the internal surface** of the dominant
> portion of external walls, firewalls and service areas, and **to the
> centreline** of inter-tenancy walls or partitions. Where a partition adjoins
> a public area (lobby or corridor), measure **to the line of the dominant
> portion of the public-area face**.

### The method for any NLA plan

NLA floor plates are never a single rectangle. The reliable procedure:

```
1. Establish the INTERNAL perimeter.
   Subtract the external wall thickness from every overall dimension.

2. Decompose the internal area into RECTANGLES.
   Cut along the lines the plan already gives you (wall faces, core edges).
   Number them. Tabulate length x width.

3. Sum the rectangles -> gross internal area.

4. Subtract every EXCLUSION:
      lift shafts and lift lobbies
      stairs
      services / risers / ducts
      toilets
      foyers and atria (public space)

5. Round to the nearest square metre and state the method.
```

Two disciplines that save marks:

- **Tabulate.** Write every rectangle as a row: `No. | length | width | area`.
  The marker can follow it, and you can check your own total.
- **Subtract the exclusions as a separate list at the bottom**, each labelled.
  If you deduct as you go you will double-count something.

### Worked illustrative example

*Not from the assessment — a clean plate to show the arithmetic. Use this
shape to rehearse the method, then apply it to the real plans.*

A single-tenancy office floor. External envelope **34,000 × 22,000**, external
walls **300** thick. Central core **12,000 × 7,000** containing lifts, stairs
and services. Two toilet blocks each **6,000 × 3,000**. One riser
**1,800 × 1,200**.

```
Internal envelope
   Length   34.00 - 0.30 - 0.30              = 33.40 m
   Width    22.00 - 0.30 - 0.30              = 21.40 m
   Gross internal   33.40 x 21.40            = 714.76 m2

Exclusions
   Core             12.00 x 7.00             =  84.00 m2
   Toilets (2)      2 x (6.00 x 3.00)        =  36.00 m2
   Riser            1.80 x 1.20              =   2.16 m2
                                              ---------
   Total exclusions                          = 122.16 m2

NLA = 714.76 - 122.16                        = 592.60 m2

                                       NLA = 593 m2
```

**Sense-check with the efficiency test from Topic 3:**

```
Gross (to inner faces)  714.76 m2
Net                     592.60 m2
Efficiency = 592.60 / 714.76 = 82.9%
```

Between the thresholds (<75% poor, >85% good) — plausible for a plate with a
central core and two toilet blocks. **If your NLA comes out above 90% or below
70% of the internal area, you have missed an exclusion or double-counted
one.** That ratio is the fastest error check available on an office plan.

### The Assessment 1 office plans

**Office (3/4)** is a whole-floor tenancy. **Office (4/4)** is the same
building sub-divided into **Lease Office (B) 1** and **Lease Office (B) 2**,
which is why it exists — it tests the sub-divided floor rule.

What the plans give you:

- Overall width **28,000** (15,000 + 3,000 + 10,000 across the top; 12,000 +
  3,000 + 13,000 across the bottom — both sum to 28,000)
- Two wings, each **16,000** deep externally, with the right-hand wing offset
  **1,500** from the left
- External walls **500** thick, so each wing is **15,000** internally
- A central core **6,000 × 5,000** containing **two stairs, eight lifts, a
  lobby and two services cupboards**
- Two toilet blocks, one dimensioned **6,000** long × **1,500** deep (6,500
  overall), the other **6,000** long
- A **1,500 × 1,500 duct** in the right-hand wing
- Glazed sections in the external walls, marked by multiple parallel lines,
  and a run of external columns along the top and bottom walls

On plan 4/4 the two tenancies are separated by the lobby and core, with the
sub-divided-floor rule in play: tenancy-to-tenancy partitions at the
**centreline**, tenancy-to-lobby partitions at the **public-area face**.

> **Cal's submitted answer for Office (3/4) was 330 m², built like this.**
> The rectangles were `12.0 × 14.0 = 168`, `11.0 × 3.0 = 33`,
> `4.0 × 10.5 = 42`, `13.5 × 9.0 = 121.5`, `12.0 × 1.5 = 18`, summing to
> **382.5 m²**; then exclusions of toilet 9, toilet 32.5, lift 9 and duct 2.25
> = **52.75 m²**, giving **329.75 → 330 m²**.
>
> The arithmetic is internally consistent — every product and the two sums
> check out — and the efficiency implied (330 / 382.5 = 86%) is in a
> believable range. But **this is a student submission, not a marked answer
> key**, and the rectangle decomposition cannot be independently verified from
> the plan without re-measuring it. Treat it as a cross-check on your own
> working, and check it against the returned assignment feedback before you
> rely on it.

> **On the office plans, the external columns along the walls are IN.**
> Structural columns or piers and **engaged perimeter columns** are both on
> the NLA include list. Only externally *protruding* columns are excluded, and
> that rule belongs to **GLA**, not NLA. Do not deduct the column boxes.

> **On plan 4/4, the lobby is out of both tenancies.** It is a public area
> not for the exclusive use of either tenant. Measure each tenancy **to the
> lobby-side face** of its partition, not to the centreline — the centreline
> rule is only for the wall *between two tenancies*.

## Mixed use — the apportionment method

Not on the Assessment 1 plans, but explicitly flagged in both the Week 1
lecture and the Week 12 revision deck, so it is exam-live.

**The rule:**

```
Mixed-use premises (e.g. a warehouse complex with an office)

  Option 1  Dominant use
  Option 2  Apportionment      <- PCA RECOMMENDS THIS

  Apportionment:  office area  measured on NLA
                  warehouse    measured on GLA
                  Total = NLA + GLA
```

**Worked example.** A tenancy comprises a warehouse and an attached office
under one roof. The warehouse is **32,000 × 25,000** outside-to-outside with
**200** thick walls. The office occupies a **12,000 × 10,000** portion inside
the same envelope, with **150** thick internal walls, and contains a **2,400 ×
1,800** toilet and a **900 × 700** riser.

**Step 1 — office on NLA** (internal faces, exclusions out):

```
Internal   (12.00 - 0.15 - 0.15) x (10.00 - 0.15 - 0.15)
         =  11.70 x 9.70                    = 113.49 m2
Less toilet   2.40 x 1.80                   =   4.32 m2
Less riser    0.90 x 0.70                   =   0.63 m2
                                             ---------
Office NLA                                  = 108.54 m2
```

**Step 2 — warehouse on GLA** (outside faces of the dominant portion, less the
office footprint, which has been measured separately):

```
Whole envelope   32.00 x 25.00              = 800.00 m2
Less office footprint (as drawn)  12.00 x 10.00 = 120.00 m2
                                             ---------
Warehouse GLA                               = 680.00 m2
```

**Step 3 — total lettable area:**

```
Total = 680.00 (GLA) + 108.54 (NLA)         = 788.54 m2

                                       Total = 789 m2
```

> **The two methods measure to different lines, so the parts will never sum
> to the whole envelope — and that is correct.** 800.00 m² of envelope
> produces 788.54 m² of lettable area, because the office's internal walls,
> toilet and riser are stripped out under NLA while the warehouse keeps its
> full outside-face measurement. Do not "fix" the difference. State that the
> office is measured on NLA and the warehouse on GLA per the PCA's
> recommendation, and show them as separate lines.

> **Do not apply the office's exclusions to the warehouse.** The warehouse is
> measured on GLA to the outside face; in a single tenancy nothing is deducted
> from it. Only the office portion loses its toilet and riser.

## Common errors that cost marks

> **1. Wrong method.** GLA is the only one that measures to the **outside**.
> For the same envelope: GLA > GLAR ≈ NLA. On the industrial plan the gap
> between the two lines was **442 m² and 416.56 m²** — nearly 6%.

> **2. Millimetres left in the calculation.** Every plan is dimensioned in mm
> and the answer must be in m². `26000 × 17000 = 442,000,000` is
> 442 **million** square millimetres. Convert first, then multiply.

> **3. Half a wall on the wrong side.** Inter-tenancy wall → **centreline**
> (half each). External wall under NLA/GLAR → **internal face** (none of it).
> External wall under GLA → **external face** (all of it). Partition to a
> public corridor under NLA → **the corridor face** (all of it, to the
> tenant's disadvantage — the landlord keeps the corridor).

> **4. Measuring a retail tenancy to its shopfront.** GLAR goes **to the mall
> line regardless if the front is set back**, and **to the external surface
> only if the front extends beyond it**.

> **5. Deducting columns.** Structural columns, piers, engaged perimeter
> columns, window mullions and window frames are **included** in all three
> methods. The only column ever excluded is an **externally protruding** one
> under GLA.

> **6. Rounding too early.** Carry two decimals through the working and round
> **only the final answer**. Rounding each rectangle to whole metres before
> summing can shift the total by several square metres on a plate with five or
> six rectangles.

> **7. Not stating the method.** One mark per plan, unconditionally
> available.

<summary>1. A single-tenancy warehouse measures 40,000 × 22,000 outside-to-outside with 250 thick walls, and contains a 3,000 × 2,500 amenities block. What is the GLA?</summary>

```
GLA = 40.00 x 22.00 = 880.00 m2   ->  880 m2
```

Measure to the **outside face** — the 250 wall thickness is not deducted
because it is already inside the 40,000 and 22,000 dimensions. The amenities
block is **not deducted**: the exclusion list (which includes toilets) applies
**in a multiple tenanted building**, and this is a single tenancy. Note the
alternative reading (880 − 7.5 = 872.5 → 873 m²) and state your assumption.
</details>

<summary>2. A retail tenancy has an external wall on the left, an inter-tenancy wall on the right (500 thick), 9,500 clear internal width, an overall depth dimension of 19,000 to the mall line, and a 400 thick external wall at the rear. ```
Width  = 9.50 + 0.25                = 9.75 m

<details>
<summary>5. A warehouse of 28,000 × 20,000 (outside faces) contains a 10,000 × 8,000 office with 150 thick internal walls, a 2,000 × 1,500 toilet and a 1,000 × 800 duct. What is the total lettable area, and by what method?</summary>

**Apportionment — office on NLA, warehouse on GLA, then added** (the PCA's
recommended approach for mixed-use premises).

```
Office NLA
   Internal   (10.00 - 0.30) x (8.00 - 0.30)  =  9.70 x 7.70 = 74.69 m2
   Less toilet  2.00 x 1.50                                  =  3.00 m2
   Less duct    1.00 x 0.80                                  =  0.80 m2
   Office NLA                                                = 70.89 m2

Warehouse GLA
   Envelope     28.00 x 20.00                                = 560.00 m2
   Less office footprint  10.00 x 8.00                       =  80.00 m2
   Warehouse GLA                                             = 480.00 m2

Total = 480.00 + 70.89                                       = 550.89 m2
                                                        ->    551 m2
```
</details>

<details>
<summary>6. Why is the end tenancy in a retail row always narrower than a middle tenancy with the same clear width?</summary>

Because GLAR measures **to the internal face** of an external wall (the
tenancy gets none of that wall's thickness) but **to the centreline** of an
inter-tenancy wall (the tenancy gets half). An end tenancy has one external
wall and one inter-tenancy wall, so it picks up half a wall; a middle tenancy
has two inter-tenancy walls and picks up a full wall's worth. With 500 thick
walls the difference is exactly 250 mm of width.
</details>

## Summary

| Method | Applies to | Measure to |
|---|---|---|
| **GLA** | Warehouses, industrial, free-standing supermarkets, showrooms | **Outside** face of the dominant portion; **centreline** of inter-tenancy/common walls |
| **GLAR** | Shopping centres, commercial buildings, strip/free-standing/semi-detached/terrace shops | **Internal** surface of the dominant portion; **centreline** of inter-tenancy walls; **the mall line regardless of a setback**; the **external surface** if the front projects beyond it |
| **NLA** | Office buildings, office and business parks | **Internal** surface of the dominant portion; **centreline** of inter-tenancy partitions; **the public-area face** where a partition adjoins a lobby or corridor |
| **Apportionment** | Mixed-use premises | Office on **NLA** + warehouse on **GLA**, added |

|---|---|---|
| 1/4 Industrial — warehouse | GLA, single tenancy | **442 m²** (436 m² if toilets are deducted — state the assumption) |
| 2/4 Retail — Lease Retail (1) | GLAR | **136 m²** |
| 2/4 Retail — Lease Retail (2) | GLAR | **140 m²** |
| 2/4 Retail — Lease Retail (3) | GLAR, with projecting bay | **154 m²** |
| 3/4 and 4/4 Office | NLA (whole floor; then sub-divided) | Work it with the rectangle method above and check efficiency lands between 75% and 90% |

**Always:** mm → m first · two decimals through the working · round only at
the end · state the method · tabulate the rectangles · list the exclusions
separately · sense-check the efficiency ratio.
