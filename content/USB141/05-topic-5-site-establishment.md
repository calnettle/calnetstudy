# Topic 5 — Site Establishment and Site Management

Week 5. The works required to prepare a site before construction starts, the
four zones, the checklist, the two tower crane types, project documentation
and construction safety.

## What site establishment is

```
Site establishment = the works required to prepare a site for the
                     commencement of construction.
```

- Good site establishment, planning and organisation should lead to a
  **smooth-flowing project**.
- A site should be **researched extensively** so it can facilitate proper
  set-out.
- Site establishment can be anything from **site layout and amenities** to
  the **"construction team"**.
- **Quality staffing is essential** to maximise efficiency on a project.

## The three site types

Correct site layout is essential, and every site is different — plant and
construction methods differ, materials handling differs, access and traffic
control differ.

| Type | Characteristics | Typical of |
|---|---|---|
| **Open site** | Construction is limited to a **small portion of the entire site**. Ample space for materials, plant and labour accommodation | **Industrial and civil** projects |
| **Long and narrow site** | **Restricted in width**. Substantial length with **narrow road (access) frontage**. Limited access creates **material handling difficulties** | **Inner-city** projects |
| **Restricted site** | Actual construction occupies **most (if not all) of the site**. On-site access is **very restricted** | **Inner-city high-rise** projects |

> **The distinguishing variable is how much of the site the building
> occupies, not how big the site is.** An open site has spare ground for
> laydown; a restricted site has none. A long and narrow site has ground but
> cannot get to it because the frontage is too narrow. A question describing
> a "1.2 ha site with a 6,000 sqm warehouse footprint and a wide frontage" is
> an **open site**; a CBD tower built to its own title boundary is a
> **restricted site**.

## Site layout considerations

**Boundary conditions**

- Access for offloading
- **Plant overswing** (e.g. cranes)
- Acoustic attenuation, noise

**Plant and equipment access**

- Maintenance of access during construction
- Plant and equipment location
- Construction sequence
- Access routes (e.g. worker access)
- Any permits required from Council

### Access

**Off-site**

- Surrounding roads and bridges adequate to withstand heavy, tall or wide
  loads
- Restrictions on surrounding roads and bridges for heavy plant
- Restrictions on the use of local roads
- One-way streets, low rail bridges
- Clearways — no parking zones, bus lanes, bus stops
- Waterfront access

**On-site**

- Overhead obstructions
- Ground level obstructions
- Underground obstructions or services
- **Contaminated waste or ground** (arsenic, lead) from petrol stations,
  tanneries and similar
- Buildings and vegetation that must remain
- Old foundations, basements, in-ground tanks and services

> **Crane overswing is a legal problem, not just a geometric one.** A jib that
> swings over a neighbour's air space needs their permission. That is why the
> luffing crane exists, and why it appears in the crane comparison below.

## The four zones of a construction site

The revision deck lists these on their own slide. Learn all four with a line
of content each.

| # | Zone | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Construction area** | The part of the site to be built upon. **Unavailable for use**, at least in the early stages |
| **2** | **Site accommodation** | Site offices, labour lunchrooms, toilet facilities, small equipment storage, first aid |
| **3** | **Materials handling and storage area** | Hard stand areas for equipment and materials handling |
| **4** | **Access corridors** | Access and entry gates (vehicles and workers); loading and unloading; clear access maintained for workers and evacuation; time required (short or long term, continually changing); people access via covered walkways / gantries to footpaths |

> **Access corridors are the zone people forget, and they carry the most
> content.** They are not just a gate — they cover loading and unloading,
> keeping evacuation routes clear, the fact that they change constantly as
> the build progresses, and covered gantries protecting the public footpath.
> If a 10-mark question asks about the four zones, that is where the extra
> points are.

### What a site layout / map must include

- Building/structure under construction
- Main equipment
- Auxiliary plant, yards, workshops
- Stores and deposits
- Site management offices
- Sanitary/welfare rooms and buildings
- Temporary roads
- Electric power supply
- Water supply
- Storm water drainage

## Site investigation

**What you should know about the site**

- Geology of the site
- Weather conditions
- Traffic conditions and restrictions
- Other buildings in the area and neighbourhood
- Local authority requirements — working hours, permits for plant movement
- Previous site use — contamination
- Emergency response and evacuation plans
- In-ground and above-ground services

**Other investigations**

- *Local authority*: restrictions on working hours; permits for plant
  movement; noise
- *Environmental*: dust and vapours (e.g. adjacent industry); stormwater
  runoff (catchment area, flows to and from adjacent sites); **asbestos —
  bonded/non-friable and unbonded/friable**

**Services to establish:** water · electricity · telephone and internet ·
sewage · storm water

> **Bonded versus friable asbestos is a two-mark definition.** *Bonded /
> non-friable* asbestos is held in a matrix such as cement sheeting and
> cannot be crushed by hand when dry. *Unbonded / friable* asbestos can be
> crumbled by hand when dry and releases fibres far more readily, so it
> demands much more stringent removal controls. It reappears in Topic 9 as a
> **barrier to retrofitting** — "asbestos adds enormous costs, reputation".

## Site establishment checklist

Six questions, and the exam can ask any of them as "list the checks you would
make".

**Is the site secure?**

- Fencing (**non-climbable**)
- Hoarding
- Signs
- Lockable gates

**Are the right amenities provided for the workers (by Code of Practice)?**

- Ablution block and changing room
- Lunchroom with heaters, air-conditioning, refrigerator, microwave oven or
  pie warmer, tables and chairs
- Cool drinking water, ice maker
- First aid

**Are the first aid and emergency plans in place?**

- Qualified first aid person
- Well-maintained first aid kit
- Emergency phone numbers — ambulance, police, fire services, doctor
- Emergency evacuation procedures (accident, flood, fire, earthquakes)
- Clear directions to guide emergency services

**Have the existing grounds and building been checked?**

- Asbestos and soil contamination
- Dangerous goods or hazardous substances
- Underground and above-ground services
- Live electrical wiring
- Strength of structural members
- Adjacent building uses

**Have the right signs and notices been posted?**

- Visitors are to report to the office and sign in
- Protective equipment — safety helmets, hearing protection, safety glasses,
  breathing masks, safety footwear
- First aid, workplace health and safety officer
- Statutory signs
- Site layout and emergency muster points

**Are the right registers and forms on site?**

- Sign-in book
- Record of any workplace incident or injury
- Reporting all notifiable incidents to **WorkCover**
- **Safe work method statements (SWMS)**
- **Safety data sheets (SDS)**

## SWMS and SDS

The two acronyms the revision deck calls out by name.

**Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS)**

Purpose: to help supervisors, workers and any other persons at the workplace
**understand the requirements established to carry out high-risk construction
work in a safe and healthy manner**.

A SWMS:

- Sets out the work activities in **logical sequences**
- **Identifies hazards**
- **Describes control measures**

The aim of a SWMS:

- Describe the activity or task to be undertaken
- Identify the resources, manpower and skills associated with the task
- Assess and select control measures as appropriate
- Systematically plan the activity so it can be completed efficiently and
  effectively

Record-keeping: the SWMS must be **kept and available for inspection until at
least the high-risk construction work is completed**. Where a SWMS is
revised, **all versions should be kept**. It may be kept at the workplace
where the high-risk work is carried out; if that is not possible it should be
kept somewhere it can be **delivered to the workplace promptly**.

**Safety Data Sheet (SDS)**

An SDS includes important information to help **reduce the risks of using
hazardous chemicals and dangerous goods** in your place of work.

> **SWMS is about a task; SDS is about a substance.** SWMS = high-risk *work
> activity* — sequence, hazards, controls. SDS = *hazardous chemical* — what
> it is, what it does to you, how to handle and store it, what to do in an
> emergency. Confusing the two is a standard MCQ distractor.

## Tower cranes

Two types, and the revision deck asks explicitly for **components** and
**differences**.

### Luffing tower crane

- Designed for **urban use in confined spaces**
- Good reach requiring **little room for motions**
- Allows the **jib and the load to always stay within the construction site
  boundaries**, ensuring the safety of the public
- Allows **many cranes to work at the same time** on high-rise buildings
- Must perform **more movements mechanically** to raise the object to the
  desired height and radius

### Hammerhead tower crane

- **Versatile** cranes with **heavy load capacity**
- Stand out for **simple assembly, maintenance and handling**
- **Efficient and quick**
- Built to lift heavy loads efficiently and safely
- Requires **more space**, but reaches **long distances**

### The comparison table

| Feature | Hammerhead | Luffing |
|---|---|---|
| Jib | Fixed horizontal jib; the load moves along it on a trolley | Jib **luffs** — raises and lowers to change the radius |
| Space needed | **More** — the jib swings a full circle | **Less** — designed for confined urban sites |
| Site boundary | The jib will overswing neighbouring property | **Jib and load stay within the site boundary** |
| Multiple cranes | Harder to run several together | **Several can work simultaneously** on a high-rise |
| Movements | Fewer mechanical movements | **More** movements to position a load |
| Reach and capacity | Long reach, heavy capacity | Good reach in a small footprint |
| Assembly and maintenance | **Simple** | More complex |

> **The luffing crane's whole selling point is the site boundary.** Confined
> urban site → luffing. Open site with room to swing → hammerhead. If a
> question describes a restricted inner-city high-rise, the answer is a
> luffing crane, and the reason is public safety and neighbour airspace — not
> lifting capacity.

**Crane radius drawing.** A site plan should show the crane's swept radius so
that overswing, laydown areas and the public exclusion zone can be checked.

## Project documentation — the daily report

Also called the job diary, daily journal or daily log. **Prepared by the
project manager (superintendent)** and **completed daily at the end of the
day**.

**What it is** — four purposes:

1. **Legal record** of daily job site activity
2. **Change order backup** documentation
3. **Back charge and claim** documentation
4. **Historical record of schedule progress**

**What it contains:**

- Project manager's name
- Job name and reference number
- Date and time
- Weather conditions
- Manpower (including subcontractors)
- Routing or distribution of copies
- Hindrance to normal progress
- Deliveries received
- Visitors to the site
- Equipment on site
- Accidents
- Inspections

> **The daily report is written for a dispute you have not had yet.** Weather
> and "hindrance to normal progress" are the two entries that decide
> extension-of-time claims months later. That is why it is a *legal* record,
> completed *daily*, by the *project manager* — three facts that make a
> complete short answer.

## Safety in construction

Statistics from **Safe Work Australia**.

**Work-related fatalities by industry, 2024.** The construction industry
recorded **37 fatalities**, below transport, postal and warehousing (**54**)
and agriculture, forestry and fishing (**44**).

Serious claims — claims involving **one working week of time off work or
more** — are reported by industry and by occupation for **2023–24**.

> **Construction is third, not first.** The deck is making the point that the
> intuitive answer is wrong: transport, postal and warehousing (54) and
> agriculture, forestry and fishing (44) both exceed construction (37). If an
> MCQ asks which industry recorded the most work-related fatalities in 2024,
> the answer is **transport, postal and warehousing**.

### Safety management

**Two major aspects of project site safety:**

1. Safety of **persons working on the site**
2. Safety of the **public who may be near the site**

**The primary causes of injuries** — five, which makes a clean five-point
answer:

1. Falling from an elevation
2. Being struck by something
3. Trenching and excavation cave-in
4. Being caught between two objects
5. Electric shock

**Principles:**

- Safety management is an important part of project management
- A safe working environment is a function of the **physical conditions** of
  the working environment **and the behaviour or working attitude** of
  individuals on the site
- A safe working environment **increases productivity and reduces risks**
- Accidents are **costly and always cause personal trauma**
- **PPE** — Personal Protective Equipment

> **"Physical conditions AND behaviour" is the marked phrase.** Safety is not
> only guardrails and PPE; it is also attitude and behaviour. An answer that
> lists only physical controls has covered half the definition.

Additional reading nominated for this week: Chapter 1 (Introduction) of
Schaufelberger and Holm, *Management of Construction Projects: A
Constructor's Perspective* (2017).

## Summary

- Site establishment is the works required to prepare a site for the
  commencement of construction; researched extensively, well staffed, well
  laid out.
- **Three site types** — open (industrial/civil), long and narrow (inner
  city), restricted (inner-city high-rise) — classified by how much of the
  site the building occupies and how access is constrained.
- **Four zones** — construction area, site accommodation, materials handling
  and storage, access corridors.
- Access splits off-site (roads, bridges, clearways, load limits) from on-site
  (overhead, ground and underground obstructions, contamination, retained
  buildings and vegetation).
- The checklist runs security (non-climbable fence, hoarding, signs, lockable
  gates), amenities, first aid and emergency plans, existing grounds and
  building checks, signage, and registers — including **SWMS** and **SDS**.
- **Luffing crane** = confined urban site, jib and load stay inside the
  boundary, several can work together, more movements.
  **Hammerhead** = more space, long reach, heavy capacity, simple to assemble.
- The **daily report** is the project manager's legal record: change orders,
  back charges, claims, schedule history.
- Safety covers **workers and the public**; the five primary injury causes are
  falls, being struck, trench cave-in, being caught between objects and
  electric shock; and a safe environment depends on **physical conditions and
  behaviour** alike.
