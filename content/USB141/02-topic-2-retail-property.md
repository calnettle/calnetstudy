# Topic 2 — Retail Property

Week 2. Shopping centre classifications, how retail is built, what retail
management actually involves, the market, and GLAR.

## The sector

Retail property is used **exclusively to market and sell consumer goods and
services**.

The Urban Land Institute definition of a shopping centre, quoted verbatim in
the lecture (*Dollars and Cents of Shopping Centres*, 1990):

> A group of commercial establishments planned, developed, owned and managed
> as a unit related in location, size and type of shops to the trade area it
> serves; it provides on-site parking in definite relationship to the types
> and sizes of the stores.

Four things are doing work in that definition: **planned, developed, owned
and managed as a unit**; **related to the trade area**; and **on-site parking
in definite relationship** to the stores. A strip of unrelated shops with
kerbside parking is not a shopping centre.

## History (Australia)

- The first modern shopping centre in Australia opened at **Chermside,
  Brisbane, in May 1957** with **25 retailers**, including a department store.
- A local newspaper described the new American phenomenon as *"an island of
  retailing in a lake of parking"*.
- Pioneer developers and major retailers realised it was more sensible to go
  to the suburbs where customers lived than to expect customers to keep coming
  to the CBD.
- Centres combined major and small retailers to deliver a **'one-stop'
  shopping experience**, usually in air-conditioned comfort, under one roof.
- On average about **22 shopping centres have opened each year in Australia
  since 1957**, and existing centres have expanded through redevelopment.

**Reinvention of the format** added: supermarkets, discount department
stores, fresh food, entertainment, leisure facilities, restaurants. And
working with local and state government brought in public facilities: bus
interchanges, libraries, child care, community facilities.

The counter-trend the lecture shows is the dead American mall (Rolling Acres,
Akron, Ohio) — the format is not immune.

## Shopping centre classifications

Two families. Learn the six core ones in order, and know that there are four
specialist ones.

**Core classifications:** City Centre · Super Regional · Major Regional ·
Regional · Sub Regional · Neighbourhood

**Specialist classifications:** Bulky Goods · Themed Centres · Market ·
Outlet Centres

### The core six

| Class | Composition | Key features | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **City Centre** | Retail premises within an arcade or mall development owned by one company, firm or person and promoted as an entity within a major CBD | Dominated by specialty shops; likely frontage on a mall or major CBD road; **generally no supermarkets**; often co-exist with large department stores | Wintergarden |
| **Super Regional** | **Two** full-line department stores, one or more full-line discount department stores, **two supermarkets**, around **250+** specialty shops | One-stop shopping for all needs; comprehensive coverage; may include entertainment and leisure; broad shopper facilities | Westfield Carindale |
| **Major Regional** | **At least one** full-line department store, one or more full-line DDS, one or more supermarkets, around **150+** specialty shops | One-stop shopping; extensive coverage; typically include entertainment and leisure | Orion Springfield Central |
| **Regional** | **One** full-line department store, a full-line DDS, one or more supermarkets, around **100+** specialty shops | Extensive coverage; broad range of shopper facilities | Westfield Helensvale |
| **Sub Regional** | At least one full-line **discount** department store, a major supermarket, approximately **40+** specialty shops | Broad range of sub-regional retail needs; typically dominated by a full-line DDS or major supermarket | Inala Plaza |
| **Neighbourhood** | A supermarket and approximately **35** specialty shops | Located in residential areas; service the immediate neighbourhood; usually extended trading hours; basic day-to-day needs | Greenslopes Mall |

### The specialist four

| Class | Definition | Key features | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bulky Goods** | Medium to large centre dominated by bulky goods retailers occupying large areas to display merchandise; typically a small number of specialty shops | Generally adjacent to large regional centres or in non-traditional retail locations; purpose designed, built and operated, generally laid out around a central landscaped area with an overall design and colour theme | Homemaker The Valley |
| **Themed Centre** | Specialty centre located primarily in **resort areas** to cater for specialist tourist needs; does not normally include a supermarket | Resort or tourist style development; **size is not a determining factor**; mainly specialty shops with food courts | Harbour Town Gold Coast |
| **Market** | A covered centre of **at least 5,000 sqm** dominated by food retailing with **at least 50 stalls or outlets**; permanent or irregular basis | Includes areas with and without refrigeration and air conditioning | Queen Victoria Market, Melbourne |
| **Outlet Centre** | Medium to large centre which does **not** normally include a department store, discount department store or supermarket | Specialty shops often selling stock at discounted prices — samples, seconds, discontinued lines | DFO Brisbane |

> **The core classifications step down by anchor count, not by floor area.**
> Super Regional = 2 department stores + 2 supermarkets + ~250 specialties.
> Major Regional = at least 1 department store + ~150. Regional = 1
> department store + ~100. Sub Regional = **no full-line department store**,
> a **discount** DDS instead + ~40. Neighbourhood = supermarket only + ~35.
> The exam question is usually "a centre has one full-line DDS, a major
> supermarket and 45 specialty shops — classify it" (Sub Regional). Count the
> anchors first.

> **Market is the only class with a hard numeric threshold.** At least
> **5,000 sqm** and at least **50 stalls**. Themed Centre is the opposite:
> size is explicitly *not* a determining factor. Do not swap them.

> **Two classes have no supermarket by definition** — City Centre ("generally
> do not include supermarkets") and Themed Centre ("does not normally include
> a supermarket"). Outlet Centre also excludes department store, DDS **and**
> supermarket. That is the fastest way to eliminate MCQ options.

## Retail methods and materials

**Shell**

- Reinforced concrete slab
- Structural steel frame

**Façade**

- Tilt-up panel
- Blockwork
- Glazing

**Basement carparks**

- Clear heights — **2,300 mm minimum**, low overhead services, height signs
- Waterproofing
- Slippery surfaces
- Finishes to basement walls and floors

**Floors**

- Generally reinforced concrete — **post-tensioned**
- Waterproofing
- Malls — ramping gradient **1:14 is the maximum**
- Floor finishes chosen for cleaning and maintainability; **food court
  flooring to be non-slip**

**Walls**

- External walls generally **tilt-up or blockwork**
- Internal walls lightweight partitions, with **minimal column supports for
  flexibility**
- Finishes — **malls by the Lessor (owner), tenancies by the Lessees
  (tenants)**

**Ceilings and roofs**

- High ceilings are difficult to maintain and clean
- Roof carparks — check waterproofing
- Metal roofs — provide **maintenance walkways** to prevent damage to roofing
  and water ingress
- Plant — position on grade where possible
- Insulate under concrete slabs for elevated floors

**Parking** — four numbers worth memorising:

```
Car parking       5 spaces per 100 sqm of floor area
Aisle width       6.2 m to 7.0 m minimum
Car bay           2.6 m x 5.4 m
Trolley parking   1 bay per 500 sqm
```

Plus: allow for dedicated pedestrian walkways.

> **Worked example — parking demand.** A neighbourhood centre with 6,500 sqm
> of floor area:
>
> ```
> Car spaces   = 6,500 / 100 x 5      = 325 spaces
> Trolley bays = 6,500 / 500 x 1      = 13 bays
> Bay area     = 2.6 x 5.4            = 14.04 sqm per bay
> Bays only    = 325 x 14.04          = 4,563 sqm
> ```
>
> Bay area alone is already **70% of the lettable area** before aisles,
> ramps or walkways — which is why the 1957 newspaper called it a lake of
> parking, and why roof and basement decks exist.

## Retail management

Large-scale centres are **the most complex property investments**. Three
framing points from the deck: strategic and operational issues; diversity of
tenants; **the centre as a whole must be successful**.

- Each centre has a unique combination of **location, identity, tenancy mix
  and competition**.
- Design and layout must meet the specific demands of location, customers and
  retailers.
- The centre must be **affordable to all**: customers, tenants and asset
  holders.
- **Maximum space — minimised costs — the best combination.**

Skills the manager needs: marketing concepts, tenancy mix, area demographics,
local competition, new developments, legislation and the economy as a whole,
security and safety, logistics and transportation, and technical
understanding of buildings, structures, utilities and technology.

### Assessment and performance

The measures the lecture lists:

- Capital value per net lettable area
- Rental level achieved over the year
- Gross lettable area – retail
- Vacancies (size, number, type)
- Customer and tenant surveys
- The potential to expand
- A level of flexibility and adaptability
- Tenure and lease conditions

> **Note the deliberate mismatch in that list.** It measures capital value
> per **NLA** but reports area on **GLAR**. Those are different bases, so
> $/sqm figures struck on one cannot be compared with the other. If you use
> this list in a report, state which basis each metric is on.

  **+6.5%**.

**Investment**

- Q3-25 volumes **$2.25 bn**, bringing 2025 YTD to **$7.61 bn** — already

> **The Week 2 slide's "+1.0% and +1.6%" for Brisbane and Perth are
> year-on-year, not quarterly.** The slide reads "average gross face rents for
> Regional Centres had steady increases showcasing a +0.3% uplift national for
> the quarter, with Brisbane and Perth being the standouts with +1.0% and
> +1.6% respectively YoY." Two different periods in one sentence. Quote 0.3%
> as the *quarterly* national figure and 1.0% / 1.6% as *annual* city figures
> — mixing them up understates or overstates growth by a factor of four.

> **Yield compression is good news for owners, and it sounds like bad news.**
> Value = net income / yield, so a *falling* yield means a *rising* capital
> value. "Yields are expected to compress further" is a forecast of capital
> growth, not decline.

## Gross Lettable Area – Retail (GLAR)

**Applications:** shopping centres; commercial buildings; strip shops,
free-standing shops, semi-detached or terrace shops in suburban streets.

Specific lease legislation may contain provisions that over-ride this in some
states and territories.

**Method:** GLAR for each tenancy is measured in square metres —

- from the **internal surface** of the dominant portion of external walls,
  firewalls and service areas
- to the **centreline** of inter-tenancy walls
- **to the mall or shop line — regardless if the front is set back**
- to the **external surface — only IF the front extends beyond** the mall or
  shop line

**Include:** window mullions; window frames; structural columns or piers;
**engaged perimeter columns**; **fire hose reels attached to a wall**;
additional facilities for individual tenants.

**Exclude:**

- Areas set aside for the provision of services (ducts, air-conditioning
  risers, fire hose reel **cupboards**, plant or motor rooms)
- Areas dedicated as public spaces not for the exclusive use of tenants
  (toilets, tea rooms)
- Lift lobbies, lift shafts, stairs and **escalators**

Areas with **less than 1.5 m clearance** above floor level should be
**measured and recorded separately** (for example, space under stairs).

> **The setback rule is the single most examinable line in GLAR.** A tenancy
> whose shopfront is recessed *behind* the mall line is still measured **to
> the mall line** — the tenant pays for the recess. A tenancy whose shopfront
> **projects past** the mall line is measured to the **external surface** of
> the projection. Recessed → mall line. Projecting → outside face. Both rules
> favour the landlord, which is how you remember them.

> **A fire hose reel on a wall is IN; a fire hose reel cupboard is OUT.** The
> reel is a fitting hanging in the tenancy. The cupboard is a service area.
> The same word appears on both lists — read which one the question uses.

> **Escalators are named in the GLAR exclusions and not in the GLA ones.**
> Retail is the sector that has escalators, so the list mentions them there.
> The safest general statement is that vertical transportation and its
> lobbies are excluded from all three methods.

## Checkpoint

<details>
<summary>1. Classify: one full-line department store, one full-line discount department store, two supermarkets, about 120 specialty shops.</summary>

**Regional.** One full-line department store + a full-line DDS + one or more
supermarkets + around 100 or more specialties. Major Regional would need
"at least one" department store and around 150+ specialties; Super Regional
would need two department stores, two supermarkets and 250+.
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Which classification has a stated minimum floor area, and what is it?</summary>

**Market** — a covered centre of **at least 5,000 sqm** dominated by food
retailing with **at least 50 stalls or outlets**. Every other classification
is defined by anchor composition rather than area, and Themed Centre says
outright that size is not a determining factor.
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. A shopping centre has 8,000 sqm of floor area. How many car spaces and trolley bays should it provide, and what floor area do the bays alone consume?</summary>

```
Spaces   = 8,000 / 100 x 5   = 400
Trolleys = 8,000 / 500 x 1   = 16 bays
Bay area = 2.6 x 5.4         = 14.04 sqm
Total    = 400 x 14.04       = 5,616 sqm
```

5,616 sqm of bays for 8,000 sqm of shops — before aisles at 6.2–7.0 m,
ramps and walkways.
</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Who finishes the mall, and who finishes the tenancies?</summary>

**Malls by the Lessor (owner); tenancies by the Lessees (tenants).** The
same split applies in office buildings — common areas by the lessor, tenancy
fit-outs by the lessee.
</details>

<details>
<summary>5. A shop's glazed front is set back 1.5 m from the mall line. Another shop's front projects 1.2 m past it. Where does GLAR measure to in each case?</summary>

Set back: **to the mall line** — the 1.5 m recess is inside the measured
area. Projecting: **to the external surface** of the projecting front — the
1.2 m projection is added. GLAR never measures to a recessed shopfront line.
</details>

<details>
<summary>6. Under GLAR, is a structural column inside the tenancy included?</summary>

**Yes.** Structural columns or piers and engaged perimeter columns are both
on the include list. The tenant pays for the floor area the column stands on.
The only columns ever excluded are externally *protruding* columns, and that
rule sits in **GLA**, not GLAR.
</details>

## Summary

- A shopping centre is planned, developed, owned **and managed as a unit**,
  related to its trade area, with on-site parking in definite relationship to
  the stores.
- Australia's first modern centre: **Chermside, May 1957, 25 retailers**;
  about 22 new centres a year since.
- Six core classifications (City Centre, Super Regional, Major Regional,
  Regional, Sub Regional, Neighbourhood) step down by **anchor composition**;
  four specialist ones (Bulky Goods, Themed, Market, Outlet).
- Construction: RC slab and steel frame shell, tilt-up/blockwork/glazing
  façade, post-tensioned RC floors, 2,300 mm minimum basement clearance,
  1:14 maximum mall ramp gradient, non-slip food court flooring.
- Parking: **5 spaces per 100 sqm**, 2.6 × 5.4 m bays, 6.2–7.0 m aisles,
  1 trolley bay per 500 sqm.
- Market at Q3 2025: household spending +5.0% YoY, prime occupancy ~99%,
  national gross face rents +0.3% QoQ, Brisbane Regional yields 5.00–6.50%
  with 24% incentives, $7.61 bn of YTD transactions.
- **GLAR** measures from the **internal** face of external walls, to the
  centreline of inter-tenancy walls, and **to the mall line regardless of a
  setback** — but to the **external surface** where the front projects
  beyond it.
