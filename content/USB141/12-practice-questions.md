# Practice Questions

Written in the real exam format: **Section A, 20 multiple choice at 1 mark
each; Section B, 6 short answers at 10 marks each; 80 marks total, 2 hours
plus 10 minutes perusal.** All questions compulsory.

Every question is drawn from the USB141 lecture and tutorial decks. Answers
are hidden — attempt first.

**Timing.** 20 marks of MCQ in about 20 minutes leaves 100 minutes for 60
marks of short answer, i.e. roughly **16 minutes per short answer**. At two
marks per point, that is five points in sixteen minutes. Do not write essays.

## Section A — Multiple choice (20 marks)

**1.** Under the PCA method, Gross Lettable Area (GLA) is measured to:

- (a) the internal surface of the dominant portion of external walls
- (b) the outside faces of the dominant portion of external walls
- (c) the mall or shop line
- (d) the centreline of all walls

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b)** GLA is the only one of the three methods that measures to the
**outside**. Inter-tenancy or common walls are still taken to the centreline.
</details>

**2.** Which measurement method applies to a free-standing supermarket?

- (a) NLA
- (b) GLAR
- (c) GLA
- (d) Apportionment

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) GLA.** The GLA applications are warehouses, industrial buildings,
**free-standing supermarkets** and showrooms. A supermarket *inside a shopping
centre* would be GLAR — the free-standing qualifier is doing the work.
</details>

**3.** In Australia, high-rise residential is defined as projects of:

- (a) three storeys or greater
- (b) four storeys or greater
- (c) six storeys or greater
- (d) ten storeys or greater

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) four storeys or greater.**
</details>

**4.** Bearing capacity is:

- (a) the pressure on the sub-soil caused by the building load
- (b) the load which the sub-soil can carry
- (c) the downward movement of a structure under load
- (d) the expansion of soil due to climatic factors

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b).** (a) is bearing *pressure*, (c) is settlement, (d) is heave.
</details>

**5.** Consolidation differs from compaction because consolidation:

- (a) is produced by impact forces such as a heavy roller
- (b) happens immediately
- (c) expels water and air from the sub-soil under sustained load
- (d) applies only to granular soils

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(c).** Compaction is mechanical, immediate and applied to a material;
consolidation is caused by self-weight and constant external loading, happens
over time, and works by expelling water and air from the sub-soil.
</details>

**6.** Which industry recorded the most work-related fatalities in Australia
in 2024?

- (a) Construction
- (b) Agriculture, forestry and fishing
- (c) Transport, postal and warehousing
- (d) Manufacturing

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Transport, postal and warehousing (54)**, ahead of agriculture, forestry
and fishing (44) and construction (37).
</details>

**7.** In a portal frame, girts:

- (a) support roof sheeting and run perpendicular to rafters
- (b) support wall cladding and run perpendicular to columns and mullions
- (c) resist the moment of wind at the knee
- (d) increase the bending resistance of the rafter

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b).** (a) is purlins, (c) is the knee joint, (d) is the haunch.
</details>

**8.** The core material of an aluminium composite panel matters because it:

- (a) determines the panel's colour range
- (b) determines the panel's fire performance
- (c) determines whether the panel can be recycled
- (d) determines the panel's acoustic rating

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b).** Higher-proportion mineral cores perform better in fire than
polyethylene cores — the Grenfell Tower failure mode. Panels with different
cores can look outwardly identical.
</details>

**9.** "All modular construction is prefabricated, but not all prefabricated
construction is modular." This is because:

- (a) modular buildings are always larger
- (b) prefabrication refers to components made off-site; modular refers to
  buildings assembled from those components with little extra site work
- (c) prefabrication is a newer term
- (d) modular construction never uses a factory

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b).** Prefabrication is the superset — window panels, walls, staircases,
floors, bathroom pods. Modular is the subset where those pieces form
volumetric units installed on site with minimal further construction.
</details>

**10.** A roof described as having "less than 3 degrees fall" is a:

- (a) skillion roof
- (b) clerestory roof
- (c) flat roof
- (d) jerkin head roof

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) flat roof** — easier to construct than a hip or gable, and defined by

</details>

**11.** Plasterboard on 600 mm stud spacings should be:

- (a) 6 mm thick
- (b) 10 mm thick

- (d) 16 mm thick

- (a) three years

**(c) twelve months.** The annual model ensures the rating represents current

- (a) $500,000

**16.** Rawlinsons $/m² building rates are based on:

- (a) net lettable area
- (b) gross lettable area
- (c) total floor area of all levels measured between the inner faces of

- (d) site area

<summary>Answer</summary>

**(c).** And the rates **exclude parking areas** — basement and open parking

**17.** The capitalisation rate is calculated as:

- (a) Capital value ÷ Net income
- (b) Net income ÷ Capital value
- (c) Gross income ÷ Outgoings
- (d) Outgoings ÷ Gross income

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Net income ÷ Capital value.** Rearranged, Capital value = Net income ÷

- (a) Value management
- (b) Life cycle costing
- (c) Building upgrades
- (d) Strategic Asset Management

**(c) Building upgrades.** The four aspects are building occupancy, building

**19.** Under GLAR, a shopfront that is set back from the mall line is
measured:

- (a) to the shopfront glass
- (b) to the mall line
- (c) to the centreline of the setback
- (d) to the external face of the projecting return

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) to the mall line — regardless if the front is set back.** Only a front
that **extends beyond** the mall line is measured to the external surface.
</details>

**20.** A luffing tower crane is preferred over a hammerhead crane on an
inner-city high-rise site principally because:

- (a) it lifts heavier loads
- (b) it is simpler to assemble and maintain
- (c) the jib and load stay within the site boundaries
- (d) it requires fewer mechanical movements

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(c).** Keeping the jib and load inside the site boundary protects the public
and avoids overswing of neighbouring airspace, and allows several cranes to
work together on a high-rise. (a), (b) and (d) all describe the hammerhead.
</details>

## Question 21 — Building area measurement (10 marks)

**(a)** A single-tenancy warehouse measures **32,000 × 19,000** outside to
outside, with **250 mm** external walls. State the method of measurement and
calculate the lettable area. **(4 marks)**

**(b)** A retail tenancy in a shopping centre has an **overall depth of
15,500** to the mall line, a **400 mm** external wall at the rear, an
inter-tenancy wall (**500 mm**) on one side and an external wall on the other,
and a clear internal width of **7,200**. Its shopfront is recessed 1,800 from
the mall line. State the method and calculate the lettable area. **(4 marks)**

**(c)** Explain why the two tenancies above are measured to different lines.
**(2 marks)**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Gross Lettable Area (GLA)** — applies to warehouses and industrial
buildings; single tenancy so measure to the **external face of the dominant
portion** of the external walls.

```
GLA = 32.00 x 19.00 = 608.00 m2   ->  608 m2
```

The 250 mm wall thickness is **not** deducted — it is already inside the
32,000 and 19,000 dimensions, and GLA measures to the outside.

**(b) Gross Lettable Area – Retail (GLAR)** — applies to shopping centres;
measure from the **internal surface** of the dominant portion of external
walls, to the **centreline** of the inter-tenancy wall, and **to the mall
line regardless of the setback**.

```
Depth = 15.50 - 0.40                     = 15.10 m
Width = 7.20 + 0.25 (half of 500 mm)     =  7.45 m
GLAR  = 15.10 x 7.45                     = 112.495 m2  ->  112 m2
```

The 1,800 recess is inside the measured area.

**(c)** GLA measures to the **outside** of the envelope because the tenant of
an industrial building occupies the whole structure and the rent is struck on
the building footprint. GLAR measures from the **internal surface** and to the
**mall line** because in a shopping centre the landlord retains and finishes
the mall and the external structure, so the tenant pays only for the space it
can trade from — but it does pay to the mall line even if it chooses to recess
its shopfront.
</details>

## Question 22 — Foundations and retaining walls (10 marks)

**(a)** Define **bearing capacity**, **settlement** and **subsidence**, and
state the essential difference between the last two. **(4 marks)**

**(b)** Name the three basic types of residential footing and give one member
of each. **(3 marks)**

**(c)** A retaining wall is proposed to a 2.4 m cut on a site with a high
water table. Explain the two ways such a wall fails and describe three
measures you would specify. **(3 marks)**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(a)**

- **Bearing capacity** — the load which the sub-soil can carry.
- **Settlement** — downward movement of the ground, or any structure on it,
  due to **soil consolidation as a result of the load applied by the
  structure** of the building.
- **Subsidence** — downward movement of the ground, or any building on it,
  due to **changes within the sub-soil**.
- **Difference:** both are downward movement, but settlement is **caused by
  the building's own load**, whereas subsidence is caused by **changes in the
  sub-soil** that would occur whether or not the building was there.

**(b)**

| Type | Members |
|---|---|
| **Isolated** | Pads, piers, piles |
| **Continuous** | Strip, beam |
| **Integrated** | Raft slab, pier and beam |

**(c)** Failure modes: **overturning** (the pressure acting on the wall is so
great the wall is pushed over) and **shear** (the pressure is not evenly
distributed or restrained and part of the wall shears — common in
**unreinforced** walls).

Three measures:

1. **Free-draining, well-graded gravel backfill** — free draining to allow
   unimpeded water movement to drainage points, well graded to limit ingress
   of soil fines that would clog the system.
2. **An agricultural drain at the base/heel of the footing**, covered with
   **geofabric**, piped to a disposal point — and/or weep holes near the base
   discharging to an open drain (not possible in a basement, where the wall
   must instead be waterproofed).
3. **Crushed rock as the filling** between the natural bank and the wall, to
   minimise the pressure on the wall, plus **reinforcement** to resist shear.
</details>

## Question 24 — Site management (10 marks)

**(a)** Name and briefly describe the four zones of a construction site.
**(4 marks)**

**(b)** A project is a 32-storey residential tower on an inner-city site where
construction occupies the entire title. Identify the site type and recommend a
crane type, justifying both. **(4 marks)**

**(c)** What is a SWMS, and how long must it be kept? **(2 marks)**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(a)**

1. **Construction area** — the part of the site to be built upon; unavailable
   for use, at least in the early stages.
2. **Site accommodation** — site offices, labour lunchrooms, toilet
   facilities, small equipment storage, first aid.
3. **Materials handling and storage area** — hard stand areas for equipment
   and materials handling.
4. **Access corridors** — entry gates for vehicles and workers, loading and
   unloading, clear access maintained for workers and evacuation, covered
   walkways or gantries protecting the public footpath; they change
   continually as the build progresses.

**(b) Restricted site** — the actual construction occupies most or all of the
site and on-site access is very restricted; typical of inner-city high-rise
projects. Consequences: no laydown area, so materials must be delivered
just-in-time; access corridors and hoardings must protect the public footpath.

**Luffing tower crane** — designed for urban use in confined spaces, with good
reach requiring little room for motions; critically, the **jib and load stay
within the site boundaries**, ensuring public safety and avoiding overswing of
neighbouring airspace, and **several luffing cranes can work simultaneously**
on a high-rise. The trade-off is more mechanical movements to position each
load.

**(c)** A **Safe Work Method Statement** helps supervisors, workers and others
understand the requirements established to carry out **high-risk construction
work** safely: it sets out the work in **logical sequences**, **identifies
hazards** and **describes control measures**. It must be **kept and available
for inspection until at least the high-risk construction work is completed**,
and **all revised versions must be kept**.
</details>

## Question 25 — Building costing (10 marks)

A developer proposes **60 basic-standard apartments** in Brisbane, averaging
**77 m²** each, with a **9 m² reinforced concrete balcony** per apartment and
**one level of underground parking** for 60 cars.

Rawlinsons Brisbane rates: apartments basic standard **$3,395–3,660/m²**;
individual unit balcony, RC, basic standard **$890–1,525/m²**; underground
parking one level **$72,700–78,400 per car**.

**(a)** Estimate the total construction cost as a range, showing all working.
**(6 marks)**

**(b)** State two adjustments that would still be required to this estimate.
**(2 marks)**

**(c)** The elemental building rate table shows Mechanical services at
**$52.75/m²** for the basic standard and **$552.00/m²** for the high standard,
against a basic-standard total of **$3,445.00/m²**. Calculate the adjusted
rate if ducted air-conditioning is substituted, and state one caution about
using that figure in Brisbane. **(2 marks)**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(a)**

```
Building
   GFA          60 x 77                 =   4,620 m2
   Low          4,620 x 3,395           = $15,684,900
   High         4,620 x 3,660           = $16,909,200

Balconies  (excluded from the base rate)
   Area         60 x 9                  =     540 m2
   Low          540 x 890               =    $480,600
   High         540 x 1,525             =    $823,500

Parking    (excluded from the base rate)
   Low          60 x 72,700             =  $4,362,000
   High         60 x 78,400             =  $4,704,000

TOTAL
   Low     15,684,900 + 480,600 + 4,362,000  = $20,527,500
   High    16,909,200 + 823,500 + 4,704,000  = $22,436,700
```

**Approximately $20.53 m to $22.44 m.**

**(b)** Any two of: **sloping site**; **ground conditions**; **unusual shape**;
**ESD initiatives**; **external works and services** (explicitly excluded from
the apartment rate); shoring for the basement excavation ($300–1,000/m² extra).

**(c)**

```
3,445.00 - 52.75 + 552.00 = $3,944.25 /m2      (a 14.5% uplift)
```

**Caution:** the elemental table total is the **mean cost for SYDNEY**, not
Brisbane. Brisbane's basic-standard midpoint is (3,395 + 3,660)/2 =
**$3,527.50/m²**, about 2.4% above the Sydney elemental total, so the adjusted
rate must have the city relativity applied — or the assumption stated.
</details>

## Question 26 — Property management (10 marks)

**(a)** State the RICS definition of Strategic Asset Management. **(2 marks)**

**(b)** A commercial property produces gross income of **$1,450,000** with
outgoings of **$410,000**. Comparable assets trade on a **6.0%**
capitalisation rate. Calculate the capital value. **(2 marks)**

**(c)** The asset manager identifies **$45,000** of non-recoverable outgoings
that can be removed through individual metering and a preventive maintenance
regime. Calculate the increase in capital value. **(2 marks)**

**(d)** Explain why the asset manager cannot control the market cap rate but
can still influence the rate applied to this asset, and give four specific
strategies. **(4 marks)**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**(a)** *"The process which aligns business and property asset strategies,
ensuring the optimisation of an organisation's property assets in a way which
best supports its key business goals and objectives."*

**(b)**

```
Net income    = 1,450,000 - 410,000    = $1,040,000
Capital value = 1,040,000 / 0.06       = $17,333,333
```

**(c)**

```
Value per $1 pa of net income = 1 / 0.06 = 16.67
Increase = 45,000 x 16.67                = $750,000
```

Check: (1,040,000 + 45,000) / 0.06 = $18,083,333, an increase of $750,000. ✓
State the assumption that the outgoing is **non-recoverable** — a recoverable
outgoing is paid by the tenant, so the saving flows to the tenant.

**(d)** The **cap rate range is market driven** — it reflects the required
return investors demand for that asset class, location and point in the cycle,
which no single owner sets. But **the quality of the asset is manageable**, and
quality determines where within the market range this asset trades. Four
strategies:

1. **Attractive WALE** — longer weighted average lease expiry means more
   certain cash flow and lower risk.
2. **Good quality tenants** — stronger covenants reduce default risk.
3. **Historical low vacancies and limited arrears** — demonstrable income
   reliability.
4. **Well maintained, with few disputes** and **frequent, favourable market
   review terms**.

Worked illustration: moving from the weak end of a market range (6.75%) to the
strong end (5.75%) on $1,040,000 of net income moves the value from
$15,407,407 to $18,086,957 — roughly **$2.68 million created with no change to
income at all**.
</details>

## Extra practice — mixed drills

Short items to run under time pressure.

<details>
<summary>D1. A shopping centre has 9,500 sqm of floor area. How many car spaces and trolley bays, and what floor area do the bays consume?</summary>

```
Spaces   = 9,500 / 100 x 5      = 475 spaces
Trolleys = 9,500 / 500 x 1      = 19 bays
Bay area = 2.6 x 5.4            = 14.04 m2
Bays     = 475 x 14.04          = 6,669 m2
```

Before aisles (6.2–7.0 m), ramps and dedicated pedestrian walkways.
</details>

<details>
<summary>D2. Classify: at least one full-line discount department store, a major supermarket, 45 specialty shops.</summary>

**Sub Regional.** No full-line department store, a DDS anchor, ~40 or more
specialties. Add a full-line department store and take it past 100
specialties and it becomes Regional.
</details>

<details>
<summary>D3. An office floor is 26,000 × 16,000 externally with 300 mm walls, a 9,000 × 5,000 core, two toilets of 4,500 × 2,400 and a 1,200 × 1,000 riser. NLA and efficiency?</summary>

```
Internal   (26.00 - 0.60) x (16.00 - 0.60) = 25.40 x 15.40 = 391.16 m2

Exclusions  core     9.00 x 5.00           =  45.00
            toilets  2 x (4.50 x 2.40)     =  21.60
            riser    1.20 x 1.00           =   1.20
                                            -------
                                            =  67.80

NLA = 391.16 - 67.80                        = 323.36 m2  ->  323 m2
Efficiency = 323.36 / 391.16                = 82.7%
```

Between the poor (<75%) and good (>85%) thresholds, so the working is
plausible.
</details>

<details>
<summary>D4. A building spends $320,000 a year on cooling at a 21 °C setpoint. Estimate the saving from moving to 24 °C.</summary>

```
Each 1 degree ~ 10% of running cost
320,000 x 0.9^3 = $233,280
Saving          =  $86,720 per year
```

At a 6% cap rate, if non-recoverable, that is 86,720 / 0.06 =
**$1,445,333** of capital value.
</details>

<details>
<summary>D5. Name the six types of obsolescence.</summary>

**Physical, economic, functional, technological, social, legal.**
</details>

<details>
<summary>D6. A stair rises 3,600 mm in one flight with 190 mm risers. Is it compliant?</summary>

```
3,600 / 190 = 18.9 risers
```

Not with a whole number of 190 mm risers, and 19 risers would exceed the
**maximum of 18 per flight** in any case. Either reduce the riser height so 18
risers fit (3,600 / 18 = 200 mm each — still one flight, and compliant provided
the riser and going are consistent), or split into two flights with a landing.
</details>

<details>
<summary>D7. Give the four purposes of the site daily report.</summary>

**Legal record** of daily job site activity; **change order backup**
documentation; **back charge and claim** documentation; **historical record of
schedule progress**. Prepared by the project manager (superintendent),
completed daily at the end of the day.
</details>

<details>
<summary>D8. What are the three ways to demolish, and which is used at Queen's Wharf Brisbane?</summary>

**Implosion** (big/high buildings), **giant wrecking balls** (large but lower;
not common now) and **munching** (smaller/lower). Queen's Wharf was
**munched** — asbestos check, internal demolition, scaffold held high, debris
tipped into the lift cores and chuted to trucks on William Street, then
crushed and reused as fill (one destination was Brisbane Airport).
</details>

<details>
<summary>D9. Give the three key site ingredients (of four) for a data centre and the main challenge.</summary>

**High voltage power in proximity; excellent connectivity (fibre providers);
low risk — not near a railway or in a flood zone; locations likely to receive
planning permission or a building permit.** Main challenge: **power** — energy
per unit of floor space is **10–50 times** that of a typical commercial office
building.
</details>

<details>
<summary>D10. Under GLAR, are the following in or out: engaged perimeter column, fire hose reel attached to a wall, fire hose reel cupboard, escalator, window mullion, space under a stair with 1.2 m clearance?</summary>

**In:** engaged perimeter column; fire hose reel attached to a wall; window
mullion. **Out:** fire hose reel cupboard (a service area); escalator. The
space under the stair (**less than 1.5 m clearance**) must be **measured and
recorded separately**.
</details>

## Section B answer template

Use this shape for every 10-mark question:

```
(a)  [term / list asked for]
     - point 1 (2 marks)
     - point 2 (2 marks)
     ...

(b)  Method:  state it in one line
     Working: set out the arithmetic in a block, two decimals
     Answer:  rounded, with units

(c)  Reason / comparison — a table if it is "differences" or "similarities"
```

Rules of thumb from the lecturer's own advice:

- **Two marks per point** → a 10-mark part wants about five points.
- **Tables are accepted** for differences and similarities, and they are
  faster.
- **Add a labelled sketch** where it helps — roof elements, portal frame, a
  lease plan, a footing section.
- **If the question is unclear, state an assumption in one line and answer
  it.** Dr Chia confirmed this is acceptable.
- **Do not write paragraphs.** Short labelled points score the same and save
  time you need elsewhere.
