Lawrence Seeto.

## What the unit is

> Building Construction develops the construction concepts and applies them
> to industrial property, retail centres, commercial and residential
> property. The unit provides the construction and design background that
> defines good quality building materials, design, layout and construction.

The point of it: construction type and quality drive market demand and value
from a **development, valuation and investment** perspective. Every technical
topic in the unit — footings, cladding, roof types, fit-out — eventually gets
pointed back at the same question: *which building will stay desirable to
tenants and need the least capital expenditure?*

## Learning outcomes

Straight from the Week 1 deck. Assessment is written against these:

1. **Articulate and analyse** the concepts, principles and sustainable
   construction procedures related to the construction of residential,
   commercial, retail and industrial buildings.
2. **Calculate and analyse building areas** following the accepted
   measurement standards for various building types.
3. **Estimate** building and construction costs.
4. **Critically evaluate and compare** building design, construction,
   facility services, market position and sustainability of selected
   properties to make critical investment decisions within a team
   environment.
5. **Prepare and present** information using professional oral and written
   communication skills.

Outcomes 2 and 3 are the two that produce *calculation* questions. Everything
else produces definitions, comparisons and label-the-diagram questions.

## Assessment

| Assessment | Weight | Individual / Group |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Building Calculations | 20% | Individual |
| 2 (Part A) — Presentation | 10% | Group |
| 2 (Part B) — Report | 30% | Group |
| Final Exam | 40% | Individual |

**Assessment 1** is four A3 lease plans (industrial, retail, office A, office
B) at 1:100. For each plan you: state the method of measurement (1 mark),
shade the boundary and hatch the lettable area neatly in coloured pencil
(1 mark), and calculate the lettable area to the nearest square metre
(3 marks). All plan dimensions are in **millimetres**; do the arithmetic in
**metres**, to two decimals, show all workings, then round the final answer
to the nearest whole square metre.

**Assessment 2** is a professional building construction report comparing
**two buildings in the same sector, in Brisbane, both completed** (not under
construction), in a group of five. The scenario: report the condition of the
buildings to an investor who plans to purchase one of them. The report
structure is fixed — see Topic 10 for the section list and the
compare-and-contrast method the unit wants.

**All twenty MCQs and all six short answers are compulsory.** There is no
choice in either section, so no topic can be dropped. 80 marks scaled to 40%
of the unit means each raw mark is worth half a percent of your grade.

> **The lecturer's own advice, worth acting on.** The Week 12 deck says
> everything examinable is in the lecture slides or the additional readings —
> so revise slide-driven, not textbook-driven. Short answers are sub-divided
> (A/B/C) and run roughly two marks per point, so a 10-mark question wants
> about **five solid points**, not five paragraphs. Tables are accepted for
> "differences/similarities" answers, and they are faster to write. If a
> question is ambiguous, state your assumption in one line and answer it.

> **Verify the exam details on the QUT website.** The Week 12 slide itself
> says the QUT official page takes precedence if there is any difference. The
> date above (18 June 2026) is what the deck states; decks in this unit are
> otherwise current for 2026, but exam logistics are the one thing worth
> double-checking.

## Where the exam marks actually sit

Counting the revision deck's own emphasis, five formats recur:

1. **Label-the-diagram** — roof types and roof elements, portal frame
   components, crane components, a lease plan.
2. **"Give two/three main differences between X and Y"** — build the tables
   before the exam. They are in the formula sheet note.
3. **Definitions** — bearing capacity, consolidation, build-to-rent, WALE,
   cap rate, GLA/NLA/GLAR, DfMA, retrofit.
4. **Measurement calculation** — area from a plan (GLA, GLAR, NLA, or a
   mixed-use apportionment).
5. **Costing calculation** — $/m² × area, and the elemental build-up idea.

## How these notes are built

Concept → formula → worked example with real numbers → the trap that costs
marks, with a checkpoint quiz in a tap-to-reveal block at the end of each
major section.

Every number was recomputed before it was written down. Where the unit's own
material contradicts itself — and it does, in three places in the costing
deck alone — the note shows the arithmetic and says which reading is right
rather than quietly picking one.
