## PCA Measurement — the one table to memorise

| | **GLA** | **GLAR** | **NLA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full name | Gross Lettable Area | Gross Lettable Area – Retail | Nett Lettable Area |
| Applies to | Warehouses, industrial buildings, **free-standing** supermarkets, showrooms | Shopping centres, commercial buildings, strip / free-standing / semi-detached / terrace shops | Office buildings, office and business parks |
| External walls | **OUTSIDE** face of the dominant portion | **INTERNAL** surface of the dominant portion | **INTERNAL** surface of the dominant portion |
| Inter-tenancy walls | Centreline | Centreline | Centreline |
| Shop / mall line | — | **To the mall line regardless of setback**; **external surface if the front projects beyond** | — |
| Partition to public area | — | — | **To the dominant portion of the public-area face** |
| Window mullions and frames | Included | Included | Included |
| Structural columns / piers | Included | Included | Included |
| Engaged perimeter columns | — | Included | Included |
| Fire hose reel **on a wall** | — | Included | Included |
| Fire hose reel **cupboard** | Excluded (service area) | Excluded | Excluded |
| Additional tenant facilities | — | Included | Included |
| Services / ducts / risers / plant rooms | Excluded | Excluded | Excluded |
| Foyers, atria, access ways | Excluded | Excluded (public space) | Excluded |
| Lift lobbies, shafts, stairs | Excluded | Excluded | Excluded |
| Escalators | — | **Excluded (named)** | Excluded |
| Toilets | Excluded *(in a multiple tenanted building)* | Excluded (public space) | **Excluded (named)** |
| Protruding external columns | **Not included** | — | — |
| Under 1.5 m clearance | — | **Measure and record separately** | — |
| Relative size, same envelope | **Largest** | Smaller | Smaller |

**Dominant portion** = the portion occupying the most surface area of a
vertical wall. Tested **per wall**:

```
Glass area  >  solid wall area  ->  window dominant -> measure to the GLASS
Glass area  <  solid wall area  ->  wall dominant   -> measure to the WALL
```

**Mixed use** (e.g. warehouse with an office):

```
Option 1  Dominant use
Option 2  APPORTIONMENT   <- PCA recommends

   Office   on NLA
   Warehouse on GLA
   Total = NLA + GLA
```

**Assessment 1 conventions:** plans in **mm**, calculate in **m**, **two
decimals**, show all workings, round the final answer to the nearest **whole
m²**. State the method (1 mark), shade boundary to boundary (1 mark),
calculate (3 marks).

## Property management formulas

```
Capitalisation rate (CR)  =  Net income / Capital value
Capital value (CV)        =  Net income / Capitalisation rate
Net income (NI)           =  Gross income - Outgoings
```

**Value of $1 pa of recurring net income** = `1 / CR`:

| Cap rate | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 5.0% | 20.00 |
| 5.5% | 18.18 |
| 6.0% | 16.67 |
| 6.25% | 16.00 |
| 6.5% | 15.38 |
| 7.0% | 14.29 |

So cutting $25,000 of **non-recoverable** outgoings at a 6% cap rate creates
`25,000 × 16.67 = $416,750` of capital value.

**The four value components to manage:** gross rent · outgoings · cap rate
(risk rating) · capital value.

## Building costing

```
$/m2 method   Estimated cost = building rate ($/m2) x area (m2)

Area basis    TOTAL FLOOR AREA OF ALL LEVELS, measured between the
              INNER FACES OF EXTERNAL WALLS  (not NLA, GLA or GLAR)

Rates EXCLUDE parking. Price parking separately and add.
Rates assume a TYPICAL BUILDING ON A FLAT SITE — adjust for sloping sites,
ground conditions, unusual shape, ESD initiatives, external works.
```

**Three estimating levels:** $/m² Building Rates · Elemental Building Rates ·
Elemental Comparative Rates.

**Rawlinsons:** *Construction Cost Guide* = projects **up to $1.5 million**,
smaller builders and consultants. *Australian Construction Handbook* = every
stage of a project, large scale.

## Pre-built comparison tables

Build these before the exam. Any "give two/three main differences" question is
one of them.

**Office vs Residential high-rise**

| Feature | Office | Residential |
|---|---|---|
| Façade | Curtain walling | Balconies |
| Floor to floor | 3,600 mm (more in-ceiling services) | 3,000 mm |
| Fit-out | By the tenant | By the builder/developer |
| Location driver | Close to the CBD | Aspect and view |
| Structure | Post-tensioned RC (occasionally steel) | RC; steel rarely |
| Public space finishes | Differ in level and type | Differ in level and type |

**GLA vs GLAR vs NLA** — see the measurement table above.

**Compaction vs Consolidation**

| | Compaction | Consolidation |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Impact force (roller) | Self-weight and constant external load |
| Timing | Immediate | Over time |
| Mechanism | Densifies a material | Expels water and air from the sub-soil |

**Settlement vs Subsidence**

| | Settlement | Subsidence |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Down | Down |
| Cause | Consolidation **under the building's load** | **Changes within the sub-soil** |
| Would it happen without the building? | No | Yes |

**Replacement vs Displacement piles**

| | Replacement (bored) | Displacement (driven) |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Auger the soil out, replace with RC | Drive the pile in, push soil aside |
| Ground | General | Granular — compact sands and gravels |
| Arisings | Yes | **Little or none** |
| Drawback | Spoil handling | **Noise and vibration** |

**Masonry vs Cast in-situ retaining wall**

| | Masonry | Cast in-situ |
|---|---|---|
| Formwork | **Not required** | Required |
| Construction time | More | Less |
| Scaffolding | More | Less |
| Expansion joints | More | Less |
| Wall heights | Limited | **Greater height possible** |

**Soldier pile vs Contiguous pile vs Sheet pile**

| | Soldier pile and panel | Contiguous pile | Sheet pile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permanence | **Temporary** | **More permanent** | Both |
| Water | **Fails in high water table** without dewatering | Needs groundwater **below** dig depth | **The water system** — wharfs, cofferdams, river banks |
| Method | Piles spaced apart, soil retained by lagging/panels/shotcrete | Piles touching or nearly touching | Interlocking prefabricated sections driven or vibrated in |
| Cost / speed | Cheap, fast, versatile, no advanced technique | — | — |

**Tilt-up vs Masonry**

| | Tilt-up | Masonry |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Speed | Faster | Slower |
| Scaffolding | Minimal | Needed as height increases |
| Form | Cast flat then tilted; generally pre-cast | Laid unit by unit |

**Prefabrication vs Modular**

| | Prefabrication | Modular |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Components made off-site | Volumetric units largely complete off-site |
| Rule | Not all prefab is modular | **All modular is prefab** |
| On-site work | Significant assembly | Minimal — units craned in |
| Maximum form | — | **Volumetric** |

**Hip vs Gable roof**

| | Hip | Gable |
|---|---|---|
| Ends | Slopes on all sides | Vertical gable end that must be clad |
| Gutter | Continuous | Interrupted at the gable ends |
| Wall heights | One height | Vary |
| Covering material | Similar amount | Similar amount |
| Cost | Cheap to construct | Extra cost is the gable cladding |

**Hammerhead vs Luffing crane**

| | Hammerhead | Luffing |
|---|---|---|
| Jib | Fixed horizontal | Luffs up and down |
| Space | More | Less — confined urban sites |
| Boundary | Overswings | **Stays within the site boundary** |
| Multiple cranes | Harder | Several together on a high-rise |
| Movements | Fewer | More |
| Assembly | Simple | More complex |

**Renovation vs Retrofit**

| | Renovation | Retrofit |
|---|---|---|
| Core act | Fixing or replacing existing parts | **Adding components it did not have** |
| Target | Original state **or better** | Modernisation and improvement |
| Driver | Ageing stock, indoor climate, energy prices | Degradation + energy efficiency, appeal, IEQ |
| Framing | The building and its users | **"For the benefit of the community"** |

**NABERS vs Green Star**

| | NABERS | Green Star |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | **Operational** performance as metered | **Design and construction** |
| Validity | **12 months**, renewed annually | Certification |
| Reach | Australian, sector-benchmarked | Internationally recognised |
| Tools | Sector ratings — offices, hotels, centres, apartments, data centres | **Four rating tools**; Green Star Buildings has 8 categories |

**Rawlinsons Cost Guide vs Handbook**

| | Cost Guide | Handbook |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | Smaller builder/consultant; **projects up to $1.5 m** | Every stage of a project, large scale |
| Parking | Excluded from rates — separate section | Separate section |

## Exam traps

> **1. GLA measures to the OUTSIDE; GLAR and NLA measure to the INSIDE.** For
> the same envelope, GLA is always the largest. Never compare a $/sqm rate
> struck on one basis with one struck on another.

> **2. The GLA exclusion list is written "in a multiple tenanted building".**
> In a single-tenancy warehouse there are no common areas, so the toilet is
> not deducted. Read the tenancy arrangement before deducting anything.

> **3. GLAR goes to the mall line regardless of a setback**, and to the
> **external surface only if the front projects beyond it**. Both rules favour
> the landlord — that is how to remember which way round they go.

> **4. Under NLA, a partition adjoining a public corridor is measured to the
> corridor face, not the centreline.** Centreline is only for a wall between
> two tenancies.

> **5. Structural columns, engaged perimeter columns, mullions and frames are
> all INCLUDED.** The only column ever excluded is an externally
> **protruding** one, and only under GLA.

> **6. Fire hose reel on a wall = IN. Fire hose reel cupboard = OUT.** Same
> words, opposite lists.

> **7. Dominant portion is a per-wall test.** One tenancy can be measured to
> glass on one elevation and to plaster on another.

> **8. Convert mm to m before you multiply.** 26000 × 17000 is 442 million
> square millimetres, not 442 m².

> **9. Round only the final answer.** Carry two decimals through every
> rectangle.

> **10. Costing area is gross floor area between the inner faces of external
> walls — not NLA.** Using a lettable area in a $/m² estimate under-states the
> cost.

> **11. Rawlinsons rates exclude parking, always.** Price the building, then
> parking, then add. And every parking rate implies 32 m² per car, so the
> per-car and per-m² routes must agree.

> **12. The elemental table total is the SYDNEY mean.** Verified: the basic
> column sums to exactly $3,445.00 = (3,315 + 3,575)/2, Sydney's range.
> Brisbane's midpoint is $3,527.50 — about 2.4% higher. Apply the city
> relativity or state the assumption.

> **13. The Medium standard's stated 110 m² does not reconcile with its
> per-apartment figures.** Every city implies about **101 m²**
> (367,500 / 3,640 = 101.0 for Brisbane). Basic (77), High (168) and Prestige
> (277) all reconcile perfectly. Use the **$/m² rate and your own area**, and
> flag the discrepancy. Confirm with Dr Chia.

> **14. Adelaide's Prestige per-apartment figure reads $11,450,000 on the
> slide.** The upper bound ($1,563,000) is smaller than the lower bound, and
> 1,450,000 / 5,235 = 277.0 m², which matches. It is a typo for **$1,450,000**.

> **15. Cap rate is in the denominator — lower is better.** A cap rate
> "improving" from 6% to 7% is backwards. Compression raises value.

> **16. Only NON-RECOVERABLE outgoings capitalise to the owner.** If the
> tenant pays the outgoing, cutting it benefits the tenant. This is the same
> split-incentive problem that blocks energy retrofits.

> **17. Two Brisbane industrial vacancy rates are both correct.** Knight Frank
> 5.1% (Q2 2025) and CBRE 3.1% (2H25). Different researchers, different dates.
> **Always name the source and the date.**

> **18. Face rent is not effective rent.** Brisbane prime office face rents
> grew 6.7% in 2025 with **37.75% incentives**. A face rent quoted without the
> incentive is half the story.

> **19. Percentage point ≠ percent.** Rent at 33.4% of income against a 29.2%
> average is **4.2 percentage points** higher, or 14.4% higher in relative
> terms. Writing "4.2% higher" is a marked error.

> **20. "30% lower" and "40% higher" describe different things.** Apartments
> are ~30% cheaper than houses in total price; super high-rise is ~40% dearer
> per m². To back out the base from a premium you **divide** by 1.40, not
> multiply by 0.60.

> **21. Purlins are roof, girts are wall.** Both run perpendicular to their
> supports — purlins on rafters, girts on columns and mullions.

> **22. Glulam is beams and columns; CLT is floors, lift shafts and escape
> stairs.** Linear versus planar.

> **23. Transport, postal and warehousing (54) beat construction (37) on 2024
> fatalities.** The intuitive answer is wrong.

> **24. Insurance-style thinking on cranes:** confined urban site → luffing,
> because the jib and load stay inside the boundary. Open site → hammerhead.

> **25. The Week 3 and Week 4 decks disagree on office floor-to-floor
> height** — 3,500 mm versus 3,600 mm. Use **3,600 vs 3,000** for the
> office/residential comparison question because that is the slide it comes
> from; note the 3,500–3,600 range elsewhere. Worth confirming with the
> lecturer.

> **26. NABERS and Green Star both use stars, so "6 Star" means nothing on its
> own.** 25 King Street holds 6 Star Green Star *and* 6 Star NABERS Energy —
> two different achievements. 80 Ann Street is 6 Star **Green Star
> Buildings**; 111 Eagle Street is 6 Star **Green Star As Built**; Queen's
> Wharf is 6 Star **Green Star Communities**. Name the tool.

> **27. Yield compression is good news for owners.** Falling cap rate = rising
> capital value. "Yields are expected to compress" forecasts capital growth.

> **28. A falling percentage in the elemental table does not mean a falling
> cost.** Substructure goes from $62.50 (1.8%) to $70.00 (1.3%) — the dollars
> rose; the share fell because the total rose faster.

> **29. All Section B questions are compulsory and sub-divided.** You cannot
> drop a topic, and each sub-part's marks tell you how many points it wants.

> **30. State an assumption rather than stalling.** If a question is
> ambiguous, write one line naming the assumption and answer it — Dr Chia
> confirmed this is acceptable, and it is worth more than a blank.
