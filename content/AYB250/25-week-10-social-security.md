## The One-Sentence Version

**The age pension is a government safety net, and how much you get
depends on two means tests, assets and income. You run both and the
one giving the LOWER pension is the one that applies.**

## The Picture

Think of the full pension as a full glass of water. The **assets test**
pours some out for every $1,000 you own above a limit. The **income
test** pours some out for every dollar of income above a limit. You do
both pours separately, and the glass you're left with is **whichever
pour left less water**. A planner's job is to find ways (legally) to
make that pour smaller.

## The System in Brief (slides 3–13)

- **Legislation:** the *Social Security Act 1991*. **Equity** is the central principle. It's funded from general tax (PAYG), not from contributions.
- **Eligibility** is usually age plus residency, and most payments are **means tested** so help goes to those most in need.
- **Four kinds of payment** (slide 6):

| Type | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Pensions** | Long-term income support | Age Pension, Disability Support Pension |
| **Payments** | Income support for people in need | JobSeeker, Parenting Payment |
| **Allowances** | Short-term or conditional support | Youth Allowance, Farm Household Allowance |
| **Supplements** | Extra amounts on top for specific needs | Energy Supplement |

**The other payments named in the deck** (fortnightly unless stated):

## The Age Pension

**Background (slides 14–16).** Introduced in **1909**, when men
qualified at 65 with a life expectancy of 55. About **2.6 million**
people receive it now, **31% on a part pension**. It's the largest
welfare cost (about **41%** of welfare, around **2.3% of GDP**).

**Qualifying age (slide 17).** 65 for men and women from 1 July 2013,
then up 6 months every two years from 1 July 2017, reaching **67 on
1 July 2023**.

**Rates per fortnight (slide 18):**

| | Single | Couple (each) | Couple combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum basic rate | $1,135.40 | $855.90 | $1,711.80 |
| Pension supplement | $88.20 | $66.50 | $133.00 |
| Energy supplement | $14.10 | $10.60 | $21.20 |
| **Total maximum** | **$1,237.70** | **$933.00** | **$1,866.00** |

**Is it enough? (slide 19, ASFA Retirement Standard, ages 65–84):** a
single needs $56,166 a year for a *comfortable* lifestyle and $36,548
for a *modest* one. The full age pension is $31,223. Couples: $78,998
comfortable, $52,690 modest, pension $47,070. So the pension alone
falls short even of "modest".

## Means Testing (slide 21)

**Two tests. Apply both. The one producing the LOWER pension is the
applicable test.** Knowing which test binds matters, because it
changes what investment advice helps the client.

### The assets test (slides 22–30)

**Counted:** bank accounts, managed funds, shares, **super if over
pension age**, annuities and income streams, loans made (including to
family), bonds, bullion, investment property and holiday homes,
cars, boats, caravans, **home contents**, businesses and farms, the
surrender value of life insurance, and gifts over the gifting limits.
Assets are valued at **market value** (what they'd sell for).

**Exempt:** the **principal home** (plus up to 2 hectares on the same
title), **super if under pension age**, a cemetery plot and prepaid
funeral, and some income streams.

**Limits (slide 26):**

| | Full pension up to: homeowner | non-homeowner | Part pension cut-out: homeowner | non-homeowner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single | $333,000 | $600,000 | $745,750 | $1,012,750 |
| Couple (combined) | $499,000 | $766,000 | $1,121,000 | $1,388,000 |

**The taper:** the pension falls by **$3.00 a fortnight for every
$1,000** of assets over the full-pension limit (single, or couple
combined).

```
Assets test pension = maximum pension − (assessable assets − limit) / 1,000 × $3
```

### The income test (slides 31–37)

**Counted:** earnings, **deemed income** from financial investments
(including super once over pension age), business and trust income,
overseas pensions, income streams, salary sacrifice, and net rental
losses.

**Exempt:** most Centrelink payments (including rent assistance),
compensation for property loss, payments for a dependent child, free
board, regular payments from a close relative, emergency relief, NDIS
payments, reimbursements.

**Deeming (slides 33–36).** Instead of using what your investments
actually earn, Centrelink **assumes** a set return on all financial
assets, whatever they really make:

| | Lower rate 1.75% on the first… | Upper rate 3.75% on the rest |
|---|---|---|
| Single | $66,800 | above $66,800 |
| Couple (at least one on pension, combined) | $110,600 | above $110,600 |

**Limits (slide 37), per fortnight:**

| | Full pension up to | Part pension cut-out |
|---|---|---|
| Single | $226 | below $2,701.40 |
| Couple (combined) | $396 | below $4,128.00 |

**The taper:** the pension falls by **50 cents for every dollar** of
income over the limit.

```
Income test pension = maximum pension − (fortnightly income − limit) × 0.5
Fortnightly income  = annual income / 26
```

## The Four Case Studies

All four use the same household: home $865,000, contents $10,000, car
$55,000, bank $44,000, managed funds $110,000, shares $45,000. Only the
super and the people change. **The home is always exempt. The car and
contents count as assets but are NOT financial assets**, so they're in
the assets test but not deemed.

### Case A: Harry, 67, single homeowner, super $220,000 (slides 27–40)

**Assets test:**
```
Assessable: 55,000 + 10,000 + 44,000 + 110,000 + 45,000 + 220,000 = 484,000
Excess over $333,000 = 151,000      reduction = 151 × $3 = $453.00
Pension = 1,237.70 − 453.00 = $784.70
```

**Income test:**
```
Financial assets: 44,000 + 110,000 + 45,000 + 220,000 = 419,000
Deemed: 66,800 × 1.75% = 1,169.00   +   352,200 × 3.75% = 13,207.50   = 14,376.50 a year
Per fortnight: 14,376.50 / 26 = 552.94      over $226 by 326.94
Reduction = 326.94 × 0.5 = 163.47     Pension = 1,237.70 − 163.47 = $1,074.23
```

**Answer: $784.70 a fortnight. Assets tested** (the lower of the two).

### Case B: John and Margaret, both 67, super $220,000 and $160,000 (slides 41–49)

**Assets test:** assets 644,000, excess over $499,000 = 145,000,
reduction $435.00. Couple pension 1,866.00 − 435.00 = 1,431.00, so
**$715.50 each**.

**Income test:** financial assets 579,000. Deemed
110,600 × 1.75% = 1,935.50 plus 468,400 × 3.75% = 17,565.00, total
19,500.50 a year = 750.02 a fortnight. Over $396 by 354.02, reduction
177.01. Couple pension 1,688.99, **$844.50 each**.

**Answer: $715.50 each. Assets tested.**

### Case C: Robert 67, Susan 62, super $420,000 and $160,000 (slides 50–56)

**The twist:** only Robert is of pension age. **Super in accumulation
phase held by the partner under pension age isn't counted in either
test.** (Super in *pension* phase would be.) So Susan's $160,000 is
excluded.

**Assets test:** 684,000, excess 185,000, reduction $555.00. Couple
1,311.00, **Robert $655.50**.

**Income test:** financial assets 619,000. Deemed 1,935.50 + 19,065.00
= 21,000.50 a year = 807.71 a fortnight. Over $396 by 411.71,
reduction 205.86. Couple 1,660.14, **Robert $830.07**.

**Answer: $655.50. Assets tested.**

### Case D: the re-contribution strategy (slides 63–68)

Same as C, but Robert withdraws **$360,000** from his super and
contributes it to **Susan's** super. Because Susan is under pension
age and her super is in accumulation, it disappears from both tests.

```
Robert's super 60,000, Susan's 520,000 (exempt)
Assets 324,000 — under the $499,000 limit — no reduction → $1,866.00 couple, $933.00 Robert
Financial assets 259,000 → deemed 1,935.50 + 5,565.00 = 7,500.50 / 26 = 288.48 — under $396 → no reduction
```

**Robert goes from $655.50 to $933.00 a fortnight: +$277.50, over 40%,
$7,215 a year, and no assets are lost.** This is the exam's favourite
planning point.

## Other Assets and Rules (slides 57–61)

- **Income streams** (account-based pensions and annuities, market-linked, defined benefit): generally treated as **financial assets**, but always check how a particular product is assessed.
- **Investment real estate:** the **net value** (market value less the loan) is an asset. Its **taxable income** counts in the income test. Loan interest is deductible against it, but depreciation, building write-offs, bank fees and construction costs are not.
- **Deprivation (gifting):** gifts above **$10,000 in an income year** or **$30,000 over a rolling five years** (the same limits for a single or a couple) are **deprived assets**. They're still counted for **five years** from the gift: as assets in the assets test, and deemed as financial assets in the income test. The five years include years **before** the pension starts.
- **Super and the pension, the perverse incentive (slides 60–61):** more super means less pension. In the deck's table, $350,000 of super earning 6% plus the pension gives $50,229 a year in total, *more* than $500,000 of super ($49,154) or $700,000 ($45,554). Gottliebsen: every $1,000 moved from super into the (exempt) home in the taper band earns about **$78 a year of extra pension, 7.8%**.

## Maximising the Age Pension (slide 62)

The five strategies to name:

1. **Renovate or upgrade the home.** The home is exempt, so moving money into it lowers assessable assets.
2. **Value assets properly.** Use market value (what they'd sell for), not replacement or insured value.
3. **Prepay funeral expenses** (or a funeral bond within the limit). Exempt.
4. **Gift within the limits** ($10,000 a year, $30,000 over five years).
5. **Younger spouse and the re-contribution strategy.** Move super into the under-age partner's accumulation account (Case D).

## The Jargon to Learn

| Term | What it means | Say it like this in the exam |
|---|---|---|
| **Means testing** | Reducing a payment according to the person's assets and income | "The age pension is means tested under both an assets test and an income test." |
| **Assets test** | Pension reduced $3 per fortnight per $1,000 above the limit | "Assessable assets exceed the homeowner threshold by $151,000, reducing the pension by $453 per fortnight." |
| **Income test** | Pension reduced 50c per $1 of income above the limit | "Assessable income exceeds the free area, so the pension reduces by 50 cents in the dollar." |
| **Applicable (binding) test** | Whichever test produces the lower pension | "The pension is assets tested, as the assets test gives the lower entitlement." |
| **Assessable vs exempt assets** | Counted vs ignored | "The principal residence is an exempt asset; the car and home contents are assessable." |
| **Financial assets** | Bank, shares, managed funds, super over pension age, income streams, loans, bullion | "Only financial assets are subject to deeming." |
| **Deeming / deeming rates** | Assumed returns of 1.75% and 3.75%, regardless of actual earnings | "Deemed income is calculated on financial assets at 1.75% on the first $66,800 and 3.75% above that." |
| **Homeowner vs non-homeowner thresholds** | Non-homeowners get a higher assets limit | "As a non-homeowner, the client's full-pension threshold is $600,000." |
| **Taper rate** | The rate at which the pension reduces | "The assets test taper is $3 per fortnight per $1,000." |
| **Deprivation / deprived assets** | Gifts above $10,000 a year or $30,000 in five years, still assessed for five years | "The $50,000 gift exceeds the gifting limits, so the excess is a deprived asset assessed for five years." |
| **Re-contribution strategy** | Withdrawing super and re-contributing it to the younger spouse's account | "Moving super into the younger spouse's accumulation account removes it from both means tests." |
| **Accumulation vs pension phase** | Super still growing vs paying an income stream | "Accumulation-phase super of a partner under Age Pension age is not assessed." |
| **Mutual obligation requirements** | Job search conditions on JobSeeker | "JobSeeker recipients must meet mutual obligation requirements unless exempt." |

## The Traps

1. **Taking the higher pension.** It's always the **lower** of the two tests.
2. **Counting the home.** The principal residence is exempt. Use the **homeowner** limits.
3. **Deeming the car or contents.** They're assets, not financial assets.
4. **Forgetting the younger partner's super is exempt** (accumulation phase only). Counting it gives the wrong answer for Case C.
5. **Annual vs fortnightly.** Deemed income is annual. **Divide by 26** before comparing with the fortnightly limit.
6. **Couples:** use the **combined** limits and combined maximum ($1,866.00), then **halve** for each person.
7. **Using single deeming thresholds for a couple.** Couples use $110,600.
