## What the unit is

BSB105 sits in the QUT business core. The deck frames the whole unit as
three questions, each attached to one assessment:

| Thread | The question | The assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Digital capabilities and the future of work** | Do you understand the tech, and can you use it? | A1 — reflect on where you are and how you get better |
| **The Business of Technology** | What technologies are organisations using, and why? | A2 — demonstrate you actually understand how to use and make sense of tech use |
| **Developing a Business Case for Digital Transformation** | Can you make a case for why an organisation should invest in a tech solution to solve a problem or capitalise on an opportunity? | A3 — make your recommendations to a real-world client |

The pattern is deliberate: **A1 focuses on you, A2 focuses on you, A3
focuses on the client.**

## Learning outcomes

The folder does not contain a unit outline, so there are no numbered ULO
codes here. The lecture deck states the three thread-level outcomes
verbatim, and these are the phrases to reuse when you write:

1. You will learn about **the future of work and the critical digital
   capabilities** needed for access to, and participation in, the future
   workforce. You will reflect on and plan for the development of your own
   digital capabilities.
2. You will learn about **emerging and disruptive technologies**, how they
   work, and how businesses are utilising these technologies successfully to
   deliver value. You will also consider the **information security and
   privacy challenges** that come with business use of technology.
3. You will learn how to develop an **evidence-informed business case** for
   the implementation of innovative technologies that align with the digital
   transformation strategy of a real-world business partner.

> **Don't invent ULO numbers.** If an assignment cover sheet asks you to map
> your work to ULO 1/2/3, get the real numbering off Canvas. Mapping to the
> wrong outcome reads worse than not mapping at all.

## Teaching team and expectations

A typical week, per Lecture 1:

- **Attend** — 1.5 hour lecture (in-person, live-streamed, recorded) and a
  1.5 hour tutorial (in person, via Zoom, recorded).
- **Read** — emails from the UC, and the recommended readings via QUT
  Readings on Canvas.
- **Engage with** — assessment materials, peers (group members and
  classmates), and the teaching team.

Lecture 2 housekeeping: stick to your registered tutorial, attend tutorials,
apply what you learn, and ask for help early.

## How these notes are built

Same structure as EFB335 and USB244: concept → framework → worked example
with real specifics from the deck → the trap that costs marks, with a
checkpoint in a tap-to-reveal block at the end of each major section.

This is a frameworks unit, not a formulas unit, but frameworks get the same
treatment: state it, apply it to something concrete the lecturer actually
put on a slide, then name the way it gets misused.

Every number here was recomputed in Python before it was written down. Where
the source contradicts itself — and it does, in several places — the note
shows the arithmetic and says which reading is right rather than quietly
picking one. Those places are flagged inline and collected in the
contradiction register at the end of note 05.
