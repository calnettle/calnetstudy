## Why the unit exists

> "Property transactions are a fundamental part of all property development,
> investment and valuation activities."

The aim, in the lecturer's words, is to develop your understanding of
property **sale, acquisition and leasing** transactions and the roles and
responsibilities of the various stakeholders throughout the process.

## Learning outcomes

Straight from the Week 1 deck. Assessment is written against these, so use
their language in written answers:

1. **Critically analyse** the impacts of consumer protection law and other
   property-user based legislation on property transactions.
2. **Professionally communicate** the complexity of the issues impacting the
   marketing and management of properties for sale and lease.
3. **Apply problem solving and innovation** in each stage of the property
   transaction process in relation to commercial, industrial and retail
   properties, in a team environment.
4. **Recognise the legal issues** related to entities capable of holding
   property interests or participating in the property industry.
5. **Apply community and professional responsibility**, including the effect
   of sustainable development, business ethics, codes of practice, cultural
   and historical influences on property industry activities.

## How to answer a problem question: the ISAAC method

Both the Week 3 and Week 5 tutorials open with this. It is the structure the
markers expect, and the structure used throughout note 05.

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| **I**ssue | Identify the issue. **State it as a question.** |
| **S**tate the law | State the legal principle that relates to this issue |
| **A**uthority | Give an authority — a section of an Act **or** a case |
| **A**pplication | Apply the law to the facts of *this* case |
| **C**onclusion | Identify the outcome. Was the action lawful, and what follows? |

> **The two marks people lose on ISAAC are both structural.** First, the
> issue must be phrased as a *question* ("Is Steve entitled to commission
> under the sole agency listing?"), not as a summary of the facts. Second,
> "Authority" is a separate step from "State the law" — quoting the
> principle without naming a section or a case scores half. Every problem in
> note 05 gives you a section *and* a case where the source supplies one.

## The transaction process

The spine of the whole unit. This diagram appears in the Week 3 lecture and
again in Week 4, and every topic hangs off one stage of it:

```
1. Appointment of real estate agent          → Topic 2 (agency, Form 6, POA)
2. Inspection and negotiation                → Topic 4 (misrepresentation, ACL)
   — disclosures by the seller               → Topic 3 (PLA s 99)
3. Contract in writing — TRANSFER OF RISK    → Topic 1 + Topic 3 (PLA s 7)
4. Due diligence period                      → Topic 3 (searches, RICS)
5. Stamping of the contract and settlement   → Topic 3 (PLA s 76)
```

The Week 4 deck adds the words **"transfer of risk"** to stage 3. That
label is doing a lot of work — it is the answer to the insurance limb of the
Week 4 tutorial problem.

The Week 5 deck expands the same spine into a two-column seller/buyer view:

| Stage | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-contract | Searches, compile pre-contract legal package, PLA compliance, contract drafted by solicitor, negotiate | Investigate title and surveys, full physical/technical due diligence, negotiate |
| Exchange contracts | Commitment to the deal | Pay deposit, commitment to the deal |
| Pre-completion | Continue to manage property, prepare for completion and requisitions on title | Pre-completion searches, verify information, arrange finance for settlement |
| Completion | Discharge mortgage | Pay settlement monies, assume responsibility for managing the property |
