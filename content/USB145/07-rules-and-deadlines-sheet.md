# Rules, Deadlines and Exam Traps

unit, plus the traps that change an answer. Nothing here is new — it is
Topics 1 to 4 and the tutorials, compressed.

> **Numbers that look like a pair are usually doing two different jobs.**
> 60/90 is *terminable after* versus *maximum term*. 10%/20% is *existing
> lot* versus *proposed lot*. 1,000 m²/12 months is *who is caught* versus
> *how long the certificate lasts*. Read which half the question is asking
> for before you answer with the other one.

## Who bears what risk, and when

```
Appointment of agent          Seller carries agency risk: an unlicensed
                              agent or an unsigned Form 6 means the agent
                              cannot sue for commission at all

Inspection and negotiation    Buyer carries caveat emptor. Seller/agent
                              carry ACL s 18 risk for anything they SAY,
                              and for silence where there is a reasonable
                              expectation of disclosure

Before the buyer signs        Seller carries the s 99 disclosure risk.
                              Non-compliance → buyer may terminate, s 104.
                              Cannot be contracted out of, s 98

CONTRACT SIGNED               ─── TRANSFER OF RISK ───
                              From 5pm on the first business day after the
                              contract date, the property is at the BUYER's
                              risk under the standard REIQ contract. The
                              buyer must insure from that moment

Due diligence period          Buyer carries everything the s 99 statement
                              warns it does not cover: flooding history,
                              structural soundness, building approvals,
                              zoning limits

Settlement                    Time is of the essence. Tendering short is a
                              failure to complete: the innocent party may
                              terminate, forfeit the deposit, sue for
                              damages, or seek specific performance
```

## Disclosure — what is and is not on the list

**Included in the s 99 statement:** seller's name, address and real property
description; **unregistered encumbrances**; impacts from **transport
infrastructure proposals** or a **notice of intention to resume**; **zoning
and heritage**; **contamination**; applications under the *Neighbourhood
Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011* (Qld); **rates and water**.

**Certificates annexed:** title and registered plan; notices under building
and planning Acts; notices to complete work; heritage register documents;
notice of intention to resume; **pool compliance**; community title scheme
material.

**Warnings the statement itself carries:** possible **statutory easements**;
**no warranty as to structural integrity**; rates and charges may differ
from those imposed on the seller.

**Expressly NOT included:**

```
Flooding history
Structural soundness
Current or past building approvals, or zoning limits
```

**Not required by any Act:** the property's **history** — murders, suicides,
hauntings, notoriety. No PLA duty, no POA duty. Only **ACL s 18** reaches
it, and only where the circumstances create a **reasonable expectation of
disclosure**.

> **The three items on the "not included" list are the three a buyer most
> wants to know, and that is not an accident.** They are the items that
> cannot be certified cheaply and reliably by a seller. Every quiz statement
> of the form "the PLA gives the buyer all the information they need" is
> **false**, and every statement that s 99 covers flooding or structural
> integrity is **false**. Two Week 6 MCQs test exactly this.

## Elements checklists

**Contract formation**

```
Offer  →  Acceptance (unconditional + communicated)  →  Intention
       →  Consideration  →  Capacity
For land, add: in writing AND signed — PLA 2023 s 7
```

**Negligence**

```
1. Duty of care    foreseeability (a real possibility, not far-fetched or
                   fanciful) + proximity (physical / circumstantial /
                   causal)
2. Breach          standard owed = question of LAW
                   failure to meet it = question of FACT
                   reasonable person, all reasonable precautions = no
                   liability
3. Damage          compensable type + caused ('but for', or materially
                   contributed) + not too remote
                   egg shell skull extends liability; novus actus
                   interveniens breaks the chain
Defences           elements not made out; contributory negligence
                   (reduces); volenti non fit injuria (defeats)
```

**Negligent misstatement** — same elements; the fight is over duty:

```
Serious or business matter?
Should the adviser have realised the person was trusting and would act?
Was reliance reasonable, or would a solicitor normally be engaged?
   = assumption of responsibility + reliance
```

**ACL s 18** — "**A person** must not, **in trade or commerce**, engage in
**conduct** that is misleading or deceptive **or is likely to** mislead or
deceive."

```
a person            → agents personally, not just corporations
in trade or commerce→ excludes purely private one-off dealings
conduct             → includes SILENCE and includes passing on a third
                      party's statement
likely to mislead   → nobody need actually have been misled
no fault required   → honesty is not a defence
no criminal penalty → damages, injunctions, compensatory orders
```

**ISAAC**

```
I  Issue           state it as a QUESTION
S  State the law   the principle
A  Authority       a SECTION or a CASE — a separate step
A  Application     to these facts, naming these parties
C  Conclusion      lawful or not, and what follows
```

## Ten traps that change the answer

1. **Sole versus exclusive agency.** Exclusive → paid **regardless** of
   effective cause. Sole → **not** paid if the **seller** was the effective
   cause. Open → must **be** the effective cause. The Week 3 MCQ states this
   backwards and the answer is **False**.
2. **Section 23(1) contradicts the lecture slide.** The slide says the
   difference is a prohibition on the vendor selling; s 23(1) says the
   **only** difference is the **entitlement to commission**. Learn the
   section. Confirm with Chris.
3. **Section 7, not section 59.** Two speaker notes still cite the 1974 Act.
   Three other sources — the Week 3 slide, the Week 5 answer key and the
   Week 6 MCQ — say **s 7**.
4. **Section 18, not section 52.** The *Trade Practices Act 1974* became the
   *Competition and Consumer Act 2010*; s 52 TPA is now **ACL s 18**. Citing
   s 52 cites a repealed provision.
5. **Part IV is competition, Schedule 2 is consumer protection.**
   Price fixing and misuse of market power are **not in the ACL**.
6. **Penalties differ by provision.** s 18: **no criminal liability, no
   pecuniary penalty**. Unconscionable conduct: **civil penalties only**.
   Country of origin: **criminal and civil**.
7. **Reasonable foreseeability belongs to duty; probability belongs to
   breach.** The duty test is a **real possibility, not far-fetched** — a
   low bar. Magnitude and likelihood set the **standard of care** in element
   2.
8. **Contributory negligence reduces; *volenti* defeats.** Plead *volenti*
   first if the facts support it.
9. **The energy rating MCQ is not "all of the above".** New offices under
   two years old and **assignments of a lease** are both exempt, so only
   "when advertising or providing information about premises for sale or
   lease" survives.
10. **The 10% instalment MCQ appears in two decks with the letters
    swapped** — (b) in Week 4, (a) in Week 5. Learn the proposition, not the
    letter.

## Things the decks never tell you

Flagged so you do not go into the quiz assuming they were covered.

- **No cooling-off period is mentioned anywhere in the USB145 lecture or
  tutorial material.** Queensland residential contracts do carry a statutory
  cooling-off right under the POA, commonly stated as **five business days**
  with a termination penalty of **0.25% of the purchase price**, and it does
  not apply to auction sales. **None of that is in the unit's sources** —
  do not cite a number for it in an assessment without checking the Act, and
  ask Chris whether it is examinable.
- **The right to rescind where a dwelling is damaged before completion and
  rendered unfit for occupation** was *Property Law Act 1974* (Qld) **s 64**
  and has been carried into the 2023 Act under a new number. The decks never
  mention it, and the Week 4 tutorial problem is squarely about damage
  before completion — so it is worth knowing that the right exists, that it
  is a right to **terminate rather than to discount**, and that you should
  **confirm the current section number** before citing it.
- **The REIQ standard contract clause numbers.** The Week 4 tutorial says
  "let's look to the standard form contract" and reproduces none of it. The
  contract is in the tutorial folder. Read the **building and pest**,
  **risk/insurance**, **time of the essence** and **default** clauses and
  write their numbers here before the quiz.
- **How the 60- and 90-day agency periods are counted** — calendar days from
  signing, on the face of it, but the decks give the numbers without the
  counting rule.
